## 3. How a child plays it

1. **The crack.** On landing, a thin crack runs across Chronosia's striped sky. `look` says so. [Source: Zara's profile: "too much time travel is ripping holes in the universe."]
2. **The case board,** beside the Council Hall. The Council's note: someone used the time portal last night, and four people have keys. A finished example shows how a grid works, using a different case (who ate a pie). [Source: your rule, "see the idea before you do it."]
3. **The time portal,** east of the Council Hall, by the river. Its log shows four nights, each with two clocks: when the traveller left and when they came back. The child reads last night's clocks and works out the minutes. The dial still shows a point, such as (6, 2).
4. **The chart house,** just south of the long path. One big map with grid lines. The child taps the point from the dial, and the map names the place there. The top band is purple: other dimensions. A star means on Chronosia.
5. **The danger meter,** just north of the long path. A line from 0 to 1, cut into 4, 6 or 8 equal parts. The child taps last night and writes where the needle is. A note says: past one half, something big is coming, so the trip was risky.
6. **The grid.** Each person's rule sits beside their name:
   - Sara: must stay on Chronosia all week.
   - Tim: cannot go to other dimensions.
   - Alex: no risky trips without a chaperone, and there are no chaperones.
   - Zara: cannot be away longer than 45 minutes.
7. **Naming the traveller.** If one person is left, the child names them.
   - A wrong name: "Zara yawns. 'Check how long that trip lasted.' Look again at the time portal's log."
   - The right name: they say where they went and why.
8. **The choice.** "Each trip rips the sky a little more. What will you do?"
   - **Tell the Council.** They chain the portal shut. The person is cross with you for a day. The crack fades.
   - **Keep the secret.** The person promises never to go again. The crack stays in the sky.
   - Either way the child earns a card. The sky, the portal, `look` on the map and the person's first words in the Council Hall all change.

## 8. What could prove this wrong

- **Children may fill the grid by trying names.** There are only four. The log keeps the number of tries, so you can see it.
- **The rules may not be read.** Without them the grid is guessing. Watch whether children read the rules beside the names.
- **The clocks are small on an iPad mini.** They are 60 pixels wide. I checked them on a server, not on a real iPad.
- **"Old Chronosia" may confuse.** Is a trip to Chronosia long ago a trip that stays on Chronosia? The star says yes. A child may argue no. If they do, that argument is the maths.
- **Reading load.** The board has the note, an example, four rules and a grid. On a phone it scrolls.
