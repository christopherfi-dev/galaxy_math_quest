# Character test: Elder Thimble in Gemini

**Date:** 24 November 2026. **For:** Marc, to paste into Gemini. **Nothing in the game was changed.**

**Why this test** [Source: Marc, 24 November]: "I've been using a Gemini for making images for the stories I'm doing for my class and asked for like four images for one reading text and the characters are consistent across them… Let's just test it out three or four images… give me a very, very long and detailed prompt."

**Why Elder Thimble** [Proposal, Claude's pick: Marc said "the character is whatever we need"]:
- He is already in the big world, with words the game says about him. So we can check the pictures against the game: "An old Mender with a white beard. His lid has a dent in it." [Built]
- The Menders' look is already drawn in the game: a small copper pot with a lid on its head, a gold knob, two handles, a dark band, big round eyes, brown feet. [Built] So the test also shows whether Gemini can match a look that already exists.
- He is a named character who talks a lot, and he appears in more than one place.
- **One thing is mine, not the game's:** the drawn Menders have no hands. The prompts give him small three-fingered hands at the ends of his handles, so he can hold his board and his pouch. Change it if you prefer.

## Prompt 1 (paste first: the whole description, and the character sheet)

```
Please draw a character for a children's space adventure game played by 8-year-olds on iPads. I will ask you for four pictures of this same character, so please keep him exactly the same in every picture.

THE CHARACTER: Elder Thimble, the oldest of the Menders. The Menders are small, friendly folk made of copper who look after old machines on a planet with two suns. Elder Thimble is kind, old and wise, a little worried, and proud of his work.

HIS BODY IS A COPPER POT. He has no separate head and no neck. His whole body is one round-bellied cooking pot made of warm copper: about as wide as it is tall, a little wider at the bottom than at the top, with a flat bottom.
- On top, like a hat, sits the pot's lid: a shallow dome, a little wider than the pot's rim, in a slightly darker copper, with a small round gold knob on the very top.
- THE LID HAS ONE DENT: a clear dent on the front left of the lid, as if a hammer once hit it. The dent must show in every picture where the front of the lid can be seen. (Menders never mend their own lid: a dent is a story.)
- Around the middle of the pot runs a dark brown metal band.
- His arms are the pot's two curved handles, one on each side, made of the same copper. Each handle ends in a small round copper hand with three stubby fingers and a thumb.
- Two short, round, dark brown feet stick out under the pot.

HIS FACE is on the upper front of the pot, just under the lid:
- two large round white eyes, close together, with small dark navy pupils, looking kind
- bushy white eyebrows
- a big soft white beard that starts just under his eyes and covers the front of the pot down to the brown band, shaped like a small cloud
- a small mouth that shows through the beard when he talks or smiles
- no nose, no ears, no hair other than the eyebrows and the beard

COLOURS (keep exactly these in every picture):
- copper body: warm orange-brown (about #B86A2A), darker copper in shadow (about #8A4F1F), a soft light copper shine on the upper left (about #E0A060)
- lid: a slightly darker copper than the body
- knob: gold (about #FFD25E)
- band: dark brown (about #3B2A12); feet: brown (about #6B4A22)
- beard and eyebrows: soft white (about #F4F1EA)
- eyes: white, with dark navy pupils (about #0E1626)

DRAWING STYLE (very important: he must fit a game that is drawn this way):
- a clean, flat 2D cartoon, like a simple vector drawing for a children's game
- a bold, smooth outline of even thickness in dark navy (about #0E1626) around every shape
- flat colours, with only one darker shade on the side away from the light, and one small white shine on the upper left
- the light comes from the upper left
- simple, rounded, friendly shapes; nothing sharp or scary
- NOT a 3D render, NOT a photo, NOT a painting, NOT anime; no texture, no brush strokes, no glow, no gradient background

FOR EVERY PICTURE:
- a plain, pure white background: no floor, no ground line, no shadow under him, no frame, no border
- no words, no letters, no numbers, no labels and no signature anywhere in the picture
- show his whole body; do not cut off his feet or the knob on his lid; leave white space all round him

PICTURE 1 OF 4: A CHARACTER SHEET.
One wide picture (16:9). Show Elder Thimble four times in a row, standing, all the same size, with even white gaps between them:
1. from the front
2. turned three-quarters to his right
3. from the side
4. from the back (the knob, the back of the pot, the two handles; no face)
The dent must be in the same place on the lid each time, so it shows in the front view and the three-quarter view.
```

## What I will do with the four pictures

1. **Check them** (written down, picture by picture):

   - Do they match what the game says? "An old Mender with a white beard. His lid has a dent in it."
   - Do they match the game's drawing? The outline, the flat colours.
   - Did any words, letters or numbers creep in?
2. **Cut him out** of the white background (I have the tools here).
3. **Put him into the big world sample** (`prototypes/world_look_sample.html`), in his village, in place of the drawn elder. Your real big world page is not touched.
4. **Make him move a little,** in code. See below.
5. **Tell you plainly** whether it worked, and what it would cost for every character in the game.

## Can a still picture move?

**Yes, a little, in code:**
- breathing (a slow, small stretch and squash)
- a gentle bob, and a small hop when he starts to talk
- blinking (I paint his eyelids shut for a moment over his eyes)
- turning to face left or right (the picture is flipped)
- changing his face when his words change: happy, worried, thinking, surprised (pictures 2)

**Not from one picture:** walking with his feet, or waving an arm. That needs a drawing of each step. Image AIs are less steady at that. We can try it later, if this test goes well.

## What could prove this wrong

- Four pictures in one chat may match, but **a fifth picture made next month, in a new chat,** may not. Next test: a new chat, picture 1 attached, one new picture.
- He may look right alone and **wrong beside the drawn game:** too detailed, a thinner outline, other colours. Picture 4 and the sample will show it.
- **Words or numbers may creep in** (on the board, on the huts). Each must be checked.
- **Small details may drift between pictures:** the dent's side, the number of fingers, the beard's length. A child notices these.
