## 3. The kinds of pattern

| | Kind | A small example | The question that makes it maths |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Repeating** | red, gold, gold, red, gold, gold | What is the part that repeats? What is the 24th? |
| B | **Repeating, two things at once** | Colour goes red, blue. Shape goes round, square, star | When is it a red round one again? (Every 6th) |
| C | **Growing by the same step** | 5, 8, 11, 14 | The 10th? Which place holds 35? |
| D | **Shrinking** | 60, 56, 52, 48 | When is it gone? |
| E | **Growing by more each time** | 1, 3, 6, 10, 15 | How much is added each time? What is the pattern in *that*? |
| F | **Doubling, or times each time** | 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 | When does it pass 100? |
| G | **A growing shape** | squares stacked like steps | How do you see it growing? Then: the 7th? |
| H | **Two things that change together** | day 1: 4. Day 2: 7. Day 3: 10 | What is the link between the day and the number? |
| I | **Patterns inside the number tables** | every answer in the 4 times table is even | Why? |
| J | **Cycles in time** | one sun is high every 3rd day, the other every 4th | When are both high? (Day 12, day 24) |
| K | **Two rules taking turns** | 2, 5, 4, 7, 6, 9 (add 3, take 1) | What are the two rules? |
| L | **Two patterns woven together** | 1, 10, 2, 20, 3, 30 | Pull them apart |
| M | **Patterns in space** | a mirror pattern on a mat; tiles on a floor | What goes in the missing corner? |
| N | **Patterns you hear or do** | clap, stamp, stamp | Show the same pattern with colours |
| O | **A broken pattern** | 3, 6, 9, 13, 15 | Which one is wrong? |
| P | **Not a pattern yet** | 2, 4, ... | It could be 6. It could be 8. What else must you know? |

**About P.** Two numbers do not make a pattern. This is the fault my reviewers kept finding in other words: "it can be read two ways". For patterns it is not a fault to hide. It is a thing to teach.

## 4. What a child can *do* with a pattern

Going on with a pattern is only the first thing. There are eleven more.

| | The child... | An example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | goes on with it | the next three |
| 2 | fills a gap | 4, 8, __, 16 |
| 3 | finds the mistake | one lantern on the bridge is the wrong colour |
| 4 | says the rule in words | "it goes up by 3, and it starts at 5" |
| 5 | **jumps far ahead** | the 24th day, with no list to count along |
| 6 | **goes backwards** | "which day has 35?" |
| 7 | asks "will it ever?" | "will the count ever land on 45?" |
| 8 | **makes one** for someone else to go on with | a villager must be able to continue it |
| 9 | **shows it another way** | hears a drum pattern, lays it out in shells |
| 10 | compares two | which reaches 50 first? On which day are they the same? |
| 11 | sorts | which of these belong to the pattern? |
| 12 | says why | why is 4 times anything always even? |

- **5 and 6 are what your "24th day" is about.** Section 6 says why they matter.
- **8 and 9 are the ones where a child makes something.** They are also the ones a typed world is good at: the child types or lays out a pattern, and a villager tries to go on with it.

## 5. Ways to show a pattern

| Way | Good for | A caution |
|---|---|---|
| **Things in a row** (beads, lanterns, rafts) | repeating, two things at once, the mistake | A long row can be counted along. For "the 24th", show only the first 8 |
| **A growing shape** | seeing growth in more than one way | Draw it so that more than one way of seeing is possible |
| **A table the child fills** | two things that change together; the far jump | See below |
| **A path with jumps** (a number line as a road, stones, a ladder) | the same step; going backwards | |
| **A hundred board with colour** | skip counts; "will it ever land on 45?"; diagonals | |
| **A wheel** (a sky wheel, a calendar ring) | cycles. Going round and round shows why the 24th is like the 3rd | |
| **Two wheels, or two rows, side by side** | two cycles lining up | |
| **Sound** | repeating. Algora already makes its music in code: a tune *is* a pattern, and the page could play one for the child to catch | Needs a way to see it too, for a quiet room |
| **A creature's tracks** | hop, hop, jump: a pattern to follow across the map | |
| **Heights side by side** (plants, towers, the tide post) | growing and shrinking; comparing two | |
| **Cloth and weaving** (over, under) | patterns in space; two things at once | |

### The table, and the 24th day

- **A table with every row** (day 1, 2, 3, 4, 5...) lets a child just add down the column. To reach day 24 that is 23 additions. It works, and it is slow.
- **A table that skips** (day 1, 2, 3, then 10, then 24) cannot be done by adding down. The child must look *across*: what links the day to the number?
- **The child's own table in the notebook** is better than a table the game draws. The child decides which days to write down. [The notebook already holds the machines' tests.]
- **24 and 28 are good numbers.** 24 is a multiple of 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 12. 28 is a multiple of 2, 4, 7 and 14. So "the 24th" lands on the end of the repeating part for many patterns, and "the 28th" for a 4 or a 7.

## 6. What the research says, in four points

1. **Children first think "add 3 each time".** That is thinking down the list. It works for the next one. It is slow for the 24th.
2. **"The number is 3 times the day, and 2 more" is a different thought.** It links two things. Far-ahead questions and backwards questions are what move a child from the first thought to the second. So the 24th day is not just a bigger number. It asks for a better idea.
3. **For a repeating pattern, the key is to name the part that repeats.** Then number the places. Every gold lantern is at a multiple of 3. Patterns and times tables meet here.
4. **For a growing shape, ask "how do you see it?" before "how many?"** There are many right ways to see it. Each way gives a different sum for the same answer.

**Grade levels, plainly:**
- Patterns in the number tables, and saying why: Grade 3 (3.OA.9).
- Making a pattern from a rule and noticing what the rule did not say: Grade 4 (4.OA.5).
- "The 31st shape" of a repeating pattern: I found it in a Grade 4 lesson. With a repeating part of 2, 3 or 4, and a place that is a multiple, I think Grade 3 can do it. **That is a guess. You know your class.**
- Growing by more each time, and doubling: a stretch.

## 8. Five ideas I think are the freshest

1. **A pattern is a thing you can hear.** The page already plays tunes made in code. No other part of the game can do this.
2. **The child makes the pattern, and the world must be able to go on with it.** Then the child is the one who knows the rule.
3. **Two cycles that line up.** Two suns are already in the sky. Nobody has asked what they are for.
4. **"Not a pattern yet."** A villager shows two numbers and asks what comes next. The right reply is "I cannot know yet. Show me one more." It turns my reviewers' hardest fault into a lesson.
5. **Tracks through the mist.** The map is big and mostly dark. A pattern could be the way to cross it.

## 9. Cautions, from this week's reviews

- **Enough of the pattern must show** for one reading only. Or the child can ask the world for more ("show me another day"), as with test pebbles in a machine.
- **Tapping the next number from three choices is guessing.** Typing it, or laying it out, is not.
- **A long row lets a child count along.** Then "the 24th" needs no thinking.
- **A table the game fills in does the work.** The child's own table does not.
- **More than one way of seeing a growing shape is right.** The world must accept them all.

---

## 10. What could prove these ideas wrong

- **Your class may find repeating patterns babyish.** They are Kindergarten work until the far jump is asked.
- **Sound in a classroom of 14 iPads** may be noise.
- **I have read about Exemplars tasks, not the tasks.** Yours may do something none of this does.
- **"Grade 3 can find the 24th" is my guess.**
- **Nobody has tried any of it.**
