3. They do the task with you.
4. You say the code. They type it. They get the card.

### Three ways to give a code [Proposal]

| Way | Cost | Weakness |

## Added 2026-11-17: kind words only [Source: Marc, "our children cannot write curse words, for the ship name or anywhere"]

**Where a child can type words:** the ship-name box at the start, and the command line. Every other box takes numbers only.

**What is built (sketch version 22):**

| Guard | What it does |
|---|---|
| The word check | Refuses rude words. Also with numbers or signs for letters, stretched letters, or letters spelled out with spaces. Words that only look close, like "glass", "cockpit" and "butterfly", are let through. |
| Names | The start box, `name ship` and `name robot` all use the check, in a stricter form that also reads the name with the spaces taken out. The answer is "The paint will not stick. Pick a kind name." |
| Commands | A rude command is not done. The game answers "Kind words only, cadet." |
| What the game shows back | Only words the game knows, and numbers. Any other typed word is shown as "…". So a rude word that is not on my list still never appears on the screen. |
| Old names | A rude name already saved on a device is wiped when the page opens. |

**Checked by `checks/kind_words_test.js`:** 110 rude inputs refused, 77 ordinary names and commands let through, and none of the game's own 819 words refused.

**What this cannot do. Please read.**

- **No word list is complete.** A child can invent a rude name that is not on it. For names, the list is the only guard.
- **Thai words written in English letters are not on the list.** I do not know them well enough to list them without also blocking ordinary words. Thai letters cannot be typed into a name at all, and are never shown back.
- **In the real game the server must do the check.** A page can be changed by the person using it.

**Decision for you: how should ships be named?**

| Option | Cost |
|---|---|
| **A. Free typing with the word check** (built) | A clever child may still find a gap. |
| **B. Pick from two lists,** such as "Silver" + "Comet" | No gap at all. But children lose the fun of their own name. |
| **C. Free typing, but only you see a new name until you approve it** | No gap, and free choice. Needs your server, and a minute of your time per class. |

**You can also give me words to add,** in any language.

## Added 2026-11-17: read, then OK [Source: Marc, after watching his daughter play]

**What you saw:** she did not notice the words at the bottom. She tapped and tapped. "She thinks it's just a click click click game."

**Your idea, built (sketch version 24):**

**Two things I added [Proposal]:**

| What | Why | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **OK is asleep at first.** It fills up and wakes after a short wait: half a second, plus a little for each word, and never more than three and a half seconds. | Without it, OK is one more thing to tap fast. | A fast reader waits a moment. |
| **Words already read once do not stop the game again.** They go straight to the line below, with a flash. | Walking back through six known places would be six OKs for nothing. | A child who tapped OK without reading will not see that card again. |

- Two messages that come together share one card.
- A task panel never has a card over it. The panel is the thing to read.

**What could prove this wrong:**

| Claim | Test |
|---|---|
| The card makes her read | Watch her eyes on three cards. Then ask what the last one said. |
| The wait is long enough, and not annoying | See whether she taps the sleeping OK again and again, or reads while it fills. |
| Skipping words she has seen is right | See whether she is lost in a place she has been to before. |

**Decisions for you:**
1. Keep the wait on OK, shorten it, or remove it?

## Added 2026-11-17: the game remembers what you were doing [Source: Marc, "use wrench... on what?"]

**What was wrong:** a child stood at a thing, typed "use wrench", and was asked "Use the wrench on what?" The game had forgotten what they were looking at.

**What is built (sketch version 28):**

| The child does | The game now |
|---|---|
| looks at the vent, then types `use wrench` | uses the wrench on the vent |
| looks at the locker, then types `open` | opens the locker |
| looks at the crate, then types `push it` | pushes the crate |
| types `open` while standing beside one thing | opens that thing |
| types `use wrench` with nothing in mind and nothing beside them | is asked "Use the wrench on what? Tap a thing." The command is started for them. One tap on a word in the **Here** row finishes it. |

- The line the game shows back says what it took the child to mean: `use wrench on vent`.
- It works on the station, on Voltara ("give fruit" beside the fish) and on the small worlds.
- **It does not solve puzzles for the child.** It only uses a thing the child chose to look at, or is standing beside. It never picks "the thing that would work".
- It forgets when the child walks into another room.

**Changed in version 29, at your request:** if the child walks off by their own choice, the old thing is let go.

| The child does | The game now |
|---|---|
| looks at the ship, walks to the crate, types `open` | opens the crate, because that is what they stand beside |
| looks at the ship, walks to an empty spot, types `open` | asks "Open what? Tap a thing." It does not reach back for the ship. |
| types `look vent`, and the game walks them to the vent, then `use wrench` | uses the wrench on the vent. A walk the game makes for them does not count as walking off. |

**How I weighed the two ways [Proposal]:**

| Rule | Good | Bad |
|---|---|---|
| The thing last looked at always wins (version 28) | simple | opens a thing the child has walked away from. Nobody expects that. |
| The thing they stand beside always wins | matches what the eye sees | breaks your own first example on a big room: a child looks at the vent from across the room, types `use wrench`, and the game uses it on whatever is nearest |
| **Built:** the thing last looked at wins, until the child walks off by their own choice. Then the thing beside them wins. | covers both cases | one more rule inside the game. The child never sees it. |

**The one case left:** a child stands between two things, having walked there, and types `open`. On the station the game asks which. On Voltara and the small worlds it takes the nearer one, which is the one with its name showing above it.

### What changed: a picture and a desk
