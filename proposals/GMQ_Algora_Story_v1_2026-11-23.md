**Date:** 23 November 2026. **For:** Marc, and the big world thread. **From:** the story thread.

**Tags:** [Source] = your pages or your words. [Proposal] = my suggestion. [Built] = on the Algora page today, and still only the big world thread's proposal unless it says [Source]. [Conflict] = two things disagree.

## 0. In short

- **Your maths needs a year.** "How much by month 11?" and "How many farms do we need?" only mean something if a people lives by a calendar, and must decide early. So this note starts with the year.
- **Your own page gives the people their way of life.** "Multiplica and Dividia... a delicate balance between multiplication and division." [Source] On Algora that is the two suns, and the two halves of the year: **grow, then share.**
- **One cause, as on Chronosia:** the Makers' machines did all the counting ahead. The Gate broke, the machines went dark, and nobody remembers how to count ahead by hand. The cadet does it, and gives the skill back.
- **What comes back to the big world thread** is in sections 2 to 5: the calendar, the peoples and places, eight things that grow by the same amount, and three decisions, each with "too little", "too much" and a small version.
- **Three questions nobody had decided** get options in section 6: why the Gate broke, how long the year is, and whether storing too much is a mistake.
- **Twelve decisions are yours.** Section 9.

### The story in one breath [Proposal]

1. The Algorans live under two suns: under the gold sun everything grows by the same amount each month, and under the white sun the high water comes and they share out what they stored.
2. Long ago, after a hungry year, their best counters built machines to do the counting ahead, and within two lifetimes nobody else knew how.
3. This year a lost ship came through the Gate, the Gate broke, and every machine went dark.
4. So the cadet counts ahead by hand: how much by the last raft, how many farms, how much stone.
5. What the cadet decides is carried out, the high water shows how it went, and the peoples learn to keep their own count again.

## 1. Who the Algorans are

All [Proposal] unless tagged.

- **A people who live by the Count.** Everything that matters to them comes a little at a time, the same amount each time: sacks from a field, fish from a boat, stone from a quarry. Nothing can be hurried. So everything must be counted ahead.
- **Two suns** [Built]. The gold sun is **Multiplica**. The white sun is **Dividia**. [Source: your names.]
  - **Under Multiplica, things grow.** Seven months of planting, fishing, cutting and carrying.
  - **Under Dividia, things are shared.** Three months of high water. No raft sails [Built]. No field is worked. Everyone lives on the stores.
  - Their saying: "Grow by the gold sun. Share by the white."
  - **For now the game is the gold half:** things that grow by the same amount. Your multiples. The white half, sharing a store out equally, is division. It is there when you want it.
- **Every store is empty at New Year.** The last of the food is eaten on the last day of the high water. An empty jar at New Year is good luck: nothing was wasted. **So every count starts from nothing.** That is your "3, 6, 9, 12", for a reason a child can be told.
  - **Later:** a good year leaves something over. Then a count starts with some already. That is your "3x + 2", and the story needs no change to hold it.
- **Nothing is spare.** Seed, stone, reeds and rafts are shared between the towns. What one town takes too much of, another town lacks. (This is what makes "too much" a real cost. Section 6c.)

### Their history, in three ages

| Age | What happened | What is left to find |
|---|---|---|
| **The Tally Age** | Each people counted by hand: pips in bowls, knots on ropes, cuts on store walls, counting songs. | The ferry rope with number tags [Built]. The post marked "SEEDS, LONG AGO" [Built]. Old cuts on every store wall: one row for each year. |
| **The Thin Year** | One year the count was wrong. The stores ran out in the middle of the high water. | On every old store wall, one row is short. Children ask about it. |
| **The Makers' Age** | The best counters of every people met at the lake, at the Two Suns Fair. They built machines that counted ahead, so there would never be another Thin Year. They were called the Makers. They also built the Gate. | The Makers' coin: "That coin was my grandfather's" [Built]. The machines [Built]. The harbour's loading board [Built]. |
| **Now** | The machines did the counting for two lifetimes. Nobody else learned how. Then the Gate broke, and every rule plate burnt black [Built]. | Dark boards. Machines nobody trusts. Store walls with no new cuts since the Makers. |

- **The Makers were not strangers.** They were these peoples' own great-grandparents. [Built, in one line: the coin was a grandfather's.] So a child can find them: in a family's keepsake, a wall, a song.
- **What a child should come away knowing:** a people handed their counting to machines, and lost the skill. Counting ahead by hand is not a chore here. It is what keeps a town fed.

### Why the cadet comes, and what the cadet does

- **The call** [Built, from your page]: "This is the S.S. Infinity. We are lost in Patternox. The Gate's code is broken. Please help."
- **The mission** [Built]: find out why the Gate broke. Mend it.
- **What is new here:** the Gate cannot be mended in a day. Meanwhile it is planting month, the machines are dark, and the high water will come in month 8 whatever anyone does. **Somebody must count ahead, now.** That is the cadet's work on the way to the Gate.

## 2. The calendar

**The year has ten months** (decision 2). Each is named for what happens in it, so a child learning English can read the year off its names. Every name is mine to change.

| Month | Name | Sun | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sprout** | gold | New Year. The stores are empty. Seed is handed out. Planting. **Plans are made now.** |
| 2 | **Leaf** | gold | Fields grow. Boats go out. Reeds are cut. |
| 3 | **Basket** | gold | The first small harvest. Each house brings a basket to the store. |
| 4 | **Bloom** | gold | Stone is cut and carried while the roads are dry. |
| 5 | **Fair** | gold | Both suns stand high together. The peoples meet at the lake to trade and to sing. |
| 6 | **Fruit** | gold | The orchards are picked. Wood is cut for the wet months. |
| 7 | **Last Raft** | gold | The last month a raft can sail [Built: "After it, no raft can sail for a long time"]. Everything must be in the stores. |
| 8 | **Rain** | white | The high water comes. |
| 9 | **High Water** | white | The towns live on their stores. Mending, weaving, telling. |
| 10 | **Song** | white | The water goes down. The last food is shared. The Rillfolk sing the year out [Built: they sing]. |

- **"By month 11" becomes "by Last Raft".** It is the day a child can care about: after it, nothing more comes in.
- **Other "by" days,** so that problems differ and each means something: by Basket month (the first harvest); by the Fair (goods to trade); by Rain (walls must be up).
- **The two suns show the month.** In the gold months the gold sun is higher. In the white months the white one is. At the Fair they are level. A child can read the season from the sky.
- **Day and tide:** the high water is once a year. The small tide on the lake is every day [Built: "the tide is in"]. They are different things, and the words should say so.

### How the year moves

The game has no clock. So the story needs a rule for "now". (This was a blocker on Chronosia.)

1. **It is Sprout month when the cadet lands.** The Gate broke in the last days of Song, as the water went down.
2. **The month does not turn by itself.** The cadet goes round the towns and makes the plans.
3. **When the plans are made, the cadet tells the Year Keeper at the lake: "Let the year run."** The months go by in the picture, one at a time. Every store fills by its own amount each month. A child watches the count happen.
4. **At Last Raft the filling stops. Then the high water shows how each plan went.** The cadet can walk the towns in the wet months and see.
5. **Then it is Sprout again.** New numbers. The same places. This is "a new problem of the same size", "a reason to come back", and the place where harder years arrive without a word.
6. **A plan the cadet never made:** that town's keeper guesses. It goes neither well nor badly. No child is stuck.

**This is the story's need, not a design.** How it is drawn and coded is the big world thread's. What the story needs is only this: a plan made early is seen through, month by month, to Last Raft.

## 5. Three decisions

Your words: "they must analyze it and make a decision." [Source] Each decision below is made in Sprout month and seen through to Last Raft.

**The same shape each time:**
- **A table to read:** an old year's cuts on the store wall, from the Tally Age. Each month the same amount more. (A finished example, and it is about another year.)
- **A decision to make,** from this year's facts.
- **The year runs. Nobody says "wrong".** The picture shows how it went.
- **Both ways of missing are seen, are kind, and are put right.**

### Decision A. Plant: "How many fields?" [Source: "How many farms do we need"]

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who asks** | A town's Count Keeper, in Sprout month. |
| **The facts** | One field brings in the same number of sacks each month. The town needs so many sacks in its store by Last Raft. Seed must be handed out now. |
| **The decision** | How many fields to plant. The child takes that much seed from the pip bins. |
| **Too little** | The store's line stops short of its mark at Last Raft. In High Water month the town's baskets run out. The next town sends some of theirs by rope across the water. A thin month, then Song. |
| **Too much** | The seed bins are shared. Another town's last field stands bare, and a keeper there says so. Or: the extra sacks do not fit above the water line, and get wet. |
| **Put right** | Next Sprout month. The town that was helped sends a gift back at the Fair. |
| **The small version** | Pim's bean rows, for his own family's shelf. |
| **In how many places** | Six: the three towns, the Mender village, the terraces, the Eights' camp. |

### Decision B. Store: "How much by Last Raft?" [Source: "How much do they need by month 11"]

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who asks** | The town's potter, or the store builder. Jars and shelves take months to make. |
| **The facts** | The boats bring in the same number of strings of fish each trip. They sail so many trips before Last Raft. |
| **The decision** | How many jars to order, so that every fish has a jar. |
| **Too little** | At Last Raft, fish lie on the dock with no jar. The gulls get them. The store has less than it could have had. |
| **Too much** | Rows of empty jars. The potter's clay came from the bank the wall needed (decision C), and the wall is a little lower there. |
| **Put right** | Empty jars are kept for next year: the store "is not empty". (That is the door to your later pattern.) |
| **The small version** | One family's shelf: jars for the fruit of their own tree. |
| **In how many places** | Each town's store, the pip bins, the wood stack. |

### Decision C. Build: "How much stone?" [Source: "how much stone do we need in the 12th month"]

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who asks** | Mud Town's wall keeper. The high water comes over the low bank. |
| **The facts** | One cart brings the same number of blocks each trip. The wall needs so many blocks before Rain month. The carts can make so many trips. |
| **The decision** | How many carts to send to the quarry. |
| **Too little** | The wall is short at one end. In Rain month the water comes over there. Wet floors. The town bails out together, and laughs about it at the Fair. |
| **Too much** | The carts were borrowed from the Furrows, who needed them for their own terrace walls. A terrace has slumped, and a Furrow shows the child. |
| **Put right** | The spare blocks go back up the hill. The terrace is rebuilt in Bloom month. |
| **The small version** | A child's garden step: a few blocks, a hand cart. |
| **In how many places** | Mud Town's wall, the harbour wall, the Furrows' terraces, the causeway at the lake. |

**The decision already built** is a fourth: **send.** The fleet in the Reach [Built] is a Last Raft job: which rafts carry which town's rice. It fits the year as it stands.

## 10. What could prove this wrong

| Claim | What to check |
|---|---|
| A child cares about "by Last Raft" | Ask a child, before the year runs: "What happens if the store is not full?" If they cannot say, the calendar is decoration. |
| "Too much" feels fair | Watch a child who planted too many fields. Do they say "that's not fair, more is better"? Then 6c needs B. |
| "The spare is already counted" is heard | It is one line. If children still add a little "to be safe", the line is not enough. |
| Watching the year run teaches the count | Does a child say the numbers aloud as the store fills? Or look away? |
| The story holds when a child pushes | "Why can't the Eights fly away and fetch food?" The ship is grounded until the Gate is mended. "Why not just count the store when it is full?" Seed is handed out in Sprout month; by Last Raft it is too late. "Why not plant extra anyway?" The seed bins are shared. I looked for more pushes and may have missed some. |
| One planet's moral is not the other's | Chronosia: a people grew careless. Algora: a people forgot a skill. If they read as the same story, one should change. |
| Ten names are not too many | A child learning English must hold ten month names. The picture of the sky and a month strip on screen must carry them. |
| The Thin Year is not too sad | Brief says: no harm that stays. It is long ago, and the walls show that houses shared. |
