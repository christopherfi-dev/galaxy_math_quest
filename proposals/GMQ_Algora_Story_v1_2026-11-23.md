## 0. In short

- **Your maths needs a year.** "How much by month 11?" only means something if a people must decide early, and wait.
- **Your own page gives the people their way of life.** "Multiplica and Dividia... a delicate balance between multiplication and division." [Source] On Algora those are the two suns, and the two halves of the year: grow, then share.
- **One cause:** the Makers' machines did all the planning. The Gate broke. The machines went dark. Nobody remembers how to plan a year by hand.
- **The table the big world thread asked for is section 1.** The rest explains it.
- **Three things nobody had decided** have options in section 6.
- **Fourteen decisions are yours.** Section 9.

### The story in one breath [Proposal]

1. The Algorans live under two suns.
2. Under the gold sun everything comes in by the same amount each month, and under the white sun the high water comes and they live on what they stored.
3. Long ago their best counters built machines to plan each year, and in two lifetimes everyone else forgot how.
4. Now the Gate is broken, the machines are dark, and the cadet plans a town's year by hand.
5. The year runs, the high water shows how the plan went, and the towns learn to plan for themselves again.

## 2. Who the Algorans are

All [Proposal] unless tagged.

- **Everything comes a little at a time.** A field is picked once a month. A boat comes home once a month. Under two suns, things grow fast and steadily. Nothing can be hurried. So a town must plan in Gold 1 for what it will need in White 3.
- **Two suns** [Built]. The gold sun is **Multiplica**. The white sun is **Dividia**. [Source: your names.]
  - Under Multiplica, things grow. That is the maths you want now.
  - Under Dividia, a store is shared out, month by month and house by house. That is division. It waits there for you.
  - Their saying: "Grow by the gold sun. Share by the white."
- **Every store is empty at New Year.** What is left on the last day of White 3 is eaten at the New Year feast. So every count starts from nothing. That is your "3, 6, 9, 12", for a reason a child can be told.
  - **Later:** a town may choose to keep some back. Then a count starts with some already. That is your "3x + 2".
- **In the gold months they eat as they go.** The river feeds them. The store is only for the white months.
- **A town's hands are all it has.** A hand in the field is not on the wall. This is what makes "too much" a real cost, in the same town and the same picture. Section 6c.

### Their history

| Time | What happened | What is left to find |
|---|---|---|
| **The Tally Time** | Each town planned by hand. In Gold 1 a keeper drew a line on the store wall: where the pile should reach. Then one cut for each count day, climbing to meet it. | Old rows on every store wall: a line first, then cuts up to it. |
| **The Thin Year** | One year a plan was wrong. A store ran out in White 2. | One old row whose cuts stop short of its line. |
| **The Makers' Time** | The best planners of every people met at the Lake Market. They built machines to plan the year, so it could not happen again. They also built the Gate. | The Makers' coin: "That coin was my grandfather's" [Built]. The machines [Built]. The harbour's loading board [Built]. |
| **Two lifetimes** | The machines planned. The keepers still cut a mark each count day. Nobody drew the line first any more. | Newer rows on the walls: cuts, and no line. |
| **Now** | The Gate broke. Every rule plate burnt black [Built]. | Dark machines. Keepers who can count what has come in, and cannot say what will. |

- **What was lost is exact:** counting what is there is easy, and everyone can do it. Counting ahead is the lost skill. That is why a table is kept (the cuts), and why a child's prediction is worth something (the line).
- **The Makers were these peoples' own great-grandparents.** (Decision 6. It changes no built words: Elder Rivet says only "long ago".)
- **What a child should come away knowing:** a people handed their planning to machines, and forgot how. Planning ahead is what keeps a town fed.

| When | What |

| **White 1 to White 3** | High water. This year's stores were planned by the machines, before the ship and its crew. So they are a little short everywhere. A quiet time: the old walls can be read. |
| **Gold 1, the next New Year** | The machines are still dark. For the first time in two lifetimes, somebody must draw the line by hand. **The planning years begin here.** |

### How a planning year goes

1. **One town asks, each year.** The cadet makes that town's plan in Gold 1.
2. **The cadet tells the town's keeper: "Let the year run."** In that town's picture the months go by, one count day at a time. The pile climbs by the same amount each time. A child watches the count happen.
3. **Then the white months show how the plan went,** in that same town.
4. **Then it is New Year again,** and another town asks. New numbers, because the Twinseers say how strong the gold sun is each year. This is "a new problem of the same size". A harder year arrives without a word: "The gold sun is strong this year."
5. **A town the cadet has planned for keeps that plan** each year after, until the cadet comes back. A good plan stays good. A poor one stays poor. That is the reason to come back.
6. **A town the cadet has never helped** runs on the machines' last plan. It is a little short every year, and its keeper says so. It is never a disaster, and never the best choice.

**Why a cadet can stay for years:** the year is short. Ten months of six days.

**This is the story's need, not a design.** How it is drawn and coded is the big world thread's. And one thing is theirs to solve, not mine: in a world a whole class shares, whose year is it?

## 4. The peoples

The five peoples, their names and their regions are [Built]. You have confirmed none of them. I build on them.

- **Each people has a custom of its own** (the table in section 1), so they are not one people in five costumes. This is a start, not enough. Homes, food and play are still to write.
- **Each town has a tally keeper.** She cuts the mark on count day. She can tell the cadet what the town needs, because she has every old row in front of her. She cannot work out what will come in.
- **The Twinseers watch the suns.** At New Year they announce the gold sun's strength. That is why the same field gives a different amount each year, and why an old row on a wall is never this year's answer.
- **The Eights are the ship's crew.** Guests. Extra mouths in every town's need. They cut wood for their keep. They can count patterns in a log. They do not know a field from a pond, and they ask the cadet too.

**The same need in many places:** three Rillfolk towns, the Mender village, the Furrows' terraces. Five towns, each with three jobs, and a small version in each: a family with one job.

## 5. The three decisions, in full

Your words: "they must analyze it and make a decision." [Source] All three use one rule: **a town has only so many hands, and each does one job all year.**

**Before any decision, a finished example:** an old row on the store wall, from another year and another crop. A line, then cuts climbing to it by the same amount.

**What is the same every time**
- "Each" always means each one: each field, each boat, each cart.
- "By Gold 7" always means after Gold 7's count day.
- Nobody says "wrong". The year runs, and the picture shows what followed.
- What followed is put right at New Year. Nothing lasts.

### A. How many? [Source: "How many farms do we need"]

- **Who asks:** the tally keeper, in Gold 1.
- **She says:** what the town needs in its store by Last Raft. What one field brings in each count day this year.
- **The cadet decides:** how many of the town's hands farm. The rest mend roofs and carry stone.
- **Too few farmers:** the pile stops below the line. White 3 is thin. Bowls are smaller. Then the feast, and it is over.
- **Too many farmers:** the pile passes the line. A big feast. But in the same picture the wall is a row short, or three roofs drip. A roof mender says: "I was in the fields all year."
- **The small version:** Pim's family [Built: Pim]. One job. How many bean rows for their own shelf?

### B. How much? [Source: "How much do they need by month 11"]

- **Who asks:** the sack weaver. Sacks are woven from the town's reeds, and they rot each wet season.
- **She says:** how many fields are planted. What each brings in on a count day.
- **The cadet decides:** how many sacks to weave. That is the cadet's line on the wall.
- **Too few sacks:** at Last Raft, part of the harvest has no sack. It stays in the field. The birds are pleased.
- **Too many sacks:** spare sacks in a heap. The reeds they used were for roofs. Some roofs drip.
- **The small version:** one family's fruit tree, and jars for its shelf by Gold 4.

### C. When?

- **Who asks:** the wall keeper. Every high water knocks the flood wall down. Every gold season it is built again. The carters are borrowed from their own fields.
- **She says:** how many blocks the wall needs. How many carts there are. What each cart brings on a count day.
- **The cadet decides:** after which count day the carters may go home.
- **Too early:** the wall is short at one end. In White 1 the water comes over there. Wet floors. The town bails out together.
- **Too late:** the wall was finished, and the carters stood idle. Their own fields went unpicked for a month. Their pile is one count day short.
- **The small version:** a child's garden step, and one hand cart.

**The decision already built** is a fourth: **send.** The Reach's fleet [Built] is the Last Raft job.

**For the maths thread, said plainly:**
- Each decision has three numbers behind it: how many, how much each, how many count days. Which two the child is given, and whether one of them comes as a table to read, is yours to shape.
- Count days never pass seven. Bigger factors must come from "how much each" or "how many". If that is too tight, the year can be longer (section 6b).
- I have not checked that every "too much" shows. If one town's jobs are over and under by amounts that cancel, the picture might show nothing. Your code must make sure a miss is seen.

|---|---|---|

| One year went wrong. | the same walls | one row whose cuts stop short of its line |

| The Makers were their own people. | a family's coin [Built]; the stones at the Lake Market | a Maker's mark beside a family's mark |

- **A child's row shows only that a plan was made:** a line, in that child's colour. It does not show how the year went. That stays in the child's own notebook. So nothing a child got wrong is left on a wall for others to see.
- **Someone who remembers:** an old Rillfolk singer knows a counting song from her gran. She does not know what it is for. The song is a plan, sung.
- **Still thin, and I say so:** this is one custom for each people, and one shared history. It is not yet a culture a child could describe. Homes, food, games and stories are still to write.
- **Borrowed from the real world, on purpose:** knotted and tallied counts, and farming by a yearly flood, are things real peoples have done. They are here because a child can understand them, and because they need counting ahead. No real people's names, places or stories are used.

## 10. What could prove this wrong

| Claim | What to check |
|---|---|
| A child cares about Last Raft | Before the year runs, ask: "What happens if the store is not full?" If they cannot say, the calendar is decoration. |
| "Too much" is seen, and feels fair | Watch a child who put too many hands in the fields. Do they see the dripping roofs, and link them? Ask: "Did the town do well?" |
| One reading only | Give decision A on paper to three children. Does anyone count a different number of count days? |
| Watching the year run teaches the count | Does a child say the numbers as the pile climbs? Or look away? |
| The story holds when a child pushes | "My ship landed. Why can't it fetch food?" It holds one cadet's lunch, not a town's winter. "What do they eat before the first count day?" The river feeds them in the gold months. "Why not copy the old row?" The gold sun is different each year. "Why not ask the Eights?" They ask the cadet. "Why not put everyone in the fields?" Then the wall is not built. I looked for more, and may have missed some. |
| A year for each decision is not too slow | Count how long one year takes on screen. If it is over two minutes, children will stop watching. |
| One planet's moral is not the other's | Chronosia: a people grew careless. Algora: a people forgot a skill. If they read as one story, one should change. |
