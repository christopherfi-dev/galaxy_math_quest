## 2. The short version

- Voltara is now **nine places you walk between**, with things to find, people to talk to and a story that turns.
- The story is yours: the energy crisis, the Energy Council, the 21 city places, the 1,650 energy. [Source]
- **Every piece of maths opens something.** You round to cross a river. You regroup crystals to open a count. You add to learn what a street needs. You subtract to find what is missing.
- **Each one shows the idea first,** then asks. That was your point about understanding before doing.
- The city lights up by **the student's own plan**.
- **The plan has effects.** A plan lasts one day. Next morning a report says what happened to the people, and how much energy the tower has now. A careless plan leaves the planet worse off. A student can try again the next day.
- Then there is a **twist**, as in your Atlantis project. The student's own morning sum does not match the tower: energy is vanishing.
- The last step is **done in real life**, with blocks and a spoken reason. You give a code. It earns the **gold card**.

## 5. Your choice matters [Proposal, built]

### What was wrong

Before, any plan that fitted was as good as any other. Leaving the solar plant dark cost nothing. It was a sum with a light show. You said so, and you were right.

### How it works now

- **A plan lasts one day.** Switch on, and night falls.
- **In the morning, read the tower log.** It tells you what happened to the people.
- **Then you work out how much energy the tower has.** That number is the next day's supply.
- **Then you plan again.** You can do this as many times as you like. The planet remembers every day.

### What people need

| Need | Met when you light | If not |
|---|---|---|
| Homes | both Neighborhoods | "Some families sat in the dark and the cold." |
| Health | the Hospital | "The sick could not be treated. More Voltarans are ill today." |
| Food | the Supermarket | "The fridges were warm. Food spoiled." |
| Learning | the Schools | "The schools stayed shut." |
| Work | the Factory or the Office Buildings | "There was no work. No one was paid." |
| Travel | the Bus Stop or the Gas Station | "No bus came and no car could fill up. People walked for hours." |
| Clean streets | the Recycling Plant | "Rubbish piled up in the streets." You see the heaps in every street. |
| Joy | any two of: Parks, Zoo, Swimming Pool, Sports Arena, Arts Theater, Amusement Park, Shopping Mall, Restaurants | "There was no play and no show. Voltara is glum." |
| Power for tomorrow | the Solar Power Plant | "The solar plant slept. The tower gets 300 less." |
| Good neighbours (after the Council's choice) | Nomad Hollow, 240 | "The Nomads' town was dark all night." Their houses go dark. |

- The Hotels and the Warehouse meet no need. They are extras, like the big wheel.
- A dark place also says what happened when you look at it: "Last night: The schools stayed shut."

### How the tower fills each morning

| Part | Energy |
|---|---|
| The tower makes by itself | 1,350 |
| The Solar Power Plant, if you lit it | + 300 |
| What you saved | + your spare |
| The Nomads mend a panel, if you lit their town | + 100 |
| Taken in the night, until the Council decides | − 240 |

**So, your question: "We don't turn on the solar plant. What happens?"**
- The plant costs 250 and gives back 300.
- Leave it dark and spend everything: next morning the tower holds **1,110**, not 1,650.
- Meeting all nine needs costs at least **1,220**. So on that morning some need must go unmet. The best you can do is 8 of 9.
- Light it again that day and the tower climbs back the day after.

### Three ways it can go (all checked in code)

| Day 1 plan | Needs met | Tower on day 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Careless: malls, hotels and fun first, no solar plant | 5 of 9 | 1,250 |
| Spend everything, no solar plant | up to 8 of 9 | 1,110 |
| Careful: the nine needs for 1,220, save 430 | 9 of 9 | 1,840 |

### How the student sees the state of the planet

- The **Mission box** says: "Voltara, day 3: Voltara is getting by: 5 of 9 needs were met. The tower holds 1,250. It held 1,650 when you came, so it is weaker now."
- The **Council**, the **wall map** and the **tower log** say the same.
- The **streets** show rubbish. The **Nomad town** is lit or dark. The elder thanks you, or says "it is very dark down here."
- **Flying away** tells you how you left it. If it is worse off, it says "You could come back and do better."

### The twist now comes from the student's own sum

- Before, the game showed a table and the student read off the difference.
- Now the student adds up what the tower **should** hold. The tower holds 240 less. They find the 240 themselves.
- Then: 8 hours in the night, the same each hour. 240 ÷ 8 = 30. That links to the 30 houses.

### After the Council's choice

- The game cannot know what the student said to you. So the choice carries on inside the game: **Nomad Hollow is on the plan table every day.**
- Light it (240): they are neighbours and mend a panel (+100).
- Leave it dark: their town is dark, and that need is unmet.
- The 240 is no longer taken in the night either way.
- All ten needs cost 1,460. A strong tower can pay that. A weak one cannot.

### What I found while checking: saving has no limit

- A careful student can light **all 21 places on day 4**. After that the shortage is gone.
- Even students tapping at random got a perfect day within 8 days, 2,126 times in 5,000, by piling up saved energy.
- So the choice is hard for about three days, then easy. Decision 1 in section 11 is about this.

## 9. How it was tested

- `checks/voltara_quest_test.js` plays the whole quest at iPad size and phone size: 9 of 9 steps, 7 of 7 secrets, the gold card, and flying away.
  - It works each answer out from the rules, not from the page.
  - It checks that you cannot cross, open the tower, plan, or pass the tunnel too early.
  - **It plays four days.** Day 1 is a careless plan: 5 of 9 needs, tower down to 1,250, rubbish in the streets, the schools say they stayed shut. Day 2: yesterday's plan no longer fits, so it mends the city for 1,220. Day 3, after the choice: all ten needs do not fit in 1,440, so one is given up. Day 4: all ten fit, and the game says Voltara is doing well and the tower is stronger.
  - It checks both Nomad endings: the dark town and the thankful elder.
- `checks/voltara_quest_check.py` checks every number. All pass.
  - The river rule was run on 10,000 seeds: 40,000 stones, each with one right answer.
  - It tried all 2,097,152 plans. 2,025,758 fit on day 1. Only 4,249 meet all nine needs. The cheapest costs 1,220.
  - It ran 5,000 random students for 8 days each. Every morning sum had one whole-number answer. The tower never fell below 1,110, so a plan is always possible.
- A game saved under sketch version 19 opens under version 20 and carries on from day 1. Tested on four old saves.
- **A bug an earlier test found:** walking from a street back to the plaza froze the game. It is fixed, and one bad frame can no longer stop the game.

**Not tested:** real children, a real iPad, and how long it takes. My guess is three or four lessons, plus the block lesson. That is a guess. I also have not tested whether children read a nine-line report.

## 10. What could prove this wrong

| Claim | Test |
|---|---|
| It feels like a place, not a quiz | Ask five students what they did on Voltara. See whether they start with the fish and the hidden town, or with the sums. |
| The maths feels needed, not added | Ask why they had to round. A good answer is "to get across". |
| Going back for the glow stone is fun, not a chore | Watch faces on the walk back. It is six screens. |
| The twist lands | See whether anyone guesses before the hollow. |
| Adding eight numbers on a board is fair | Watch Home Street. It is the longest sum in the quest. |
| The effects make students think | Compare a student's day 1 and day 2 plans. If the bad morning changed nothing, the report is not working. |
| Students see why the tower is lower | Ask: "Why do you have less today?" A good answer names the solar plant or the missing 240. |
| Nine needs is not too many to hold in mind | Watch whether they use the row of ticks, or ignore it. |
| The shortage lasts long enough | Count how many days a student plays. If most reach day 4, saving makes it too easy. |

## 11. Decisions for you

**New, about the effects:**

1. **Should saving have a limit?**
   - **A. No limit** (built). Costs nothing to explain. But a careful student lights everything on day 4, and the hard choice ends.
   - **B. The tower holds at most 2,000.** One more rule to explain. The city (2,220) never fully fits, so the choice stays hard on every day.
   - **C. Nothing carries over.** Simplest. But then there is no reason to save, and less to think about.
2. **Are these the right nine needs?** They are mine. You know what your class talked about in the real lesson. You could cut, add or rename any. The Hotels and Warehouse count for nothing now.
3. **Should a need get worse if it is unmet two nights running?** For example, a second night with no hospital. It would make the stakes grow. It costs one more rule and a longer report.
4. **Should the ending change the reward?** Now the gold card comes from the Council's choice, however the planet is doing. The ending is only told. A card or badge for leaving Voltara stronger is possible. Rewards are your rules, so I have not touched them.
5. **Is 1,350 + 300 the right balance?** I chose it so that a lit solar plant brings the tower back to exactly 1,650. Any other numbers can be checked in a minute.

**Still open from v1:**

6. **Which needs list?** The class version (2,220, so 570 short), or your planning list (1,940, so 290 short)?
7. **Is the walk back for the glow stone too long?** I can put a second stone nearer, or keep it as the price of not exploring.
8. **Should every student have the same city numbers?** They do now, so a class can argue about one plan and compare towers on day 2. The river numbers differ for each student.
9. **Cards:** each of the seven maths steps gives a random card, and the last step gives gold. Later days give no cards. Too many?
