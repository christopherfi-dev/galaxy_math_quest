# Chronosia as one story

**Date:** 23 November 2026. **For:** Marc. **From:** the Chronosia story thread.
**Stage:** a design note. Nothing here is built. I did not touch the game.
**Tags:** [Source] = from your pages or your words. [Proposal] = my suggestion. [Conflict] = your sources disagree. [Built] = in the game today (preview, version 90), and a proposal of an earlier thread unless it says [Source].

---

## 0. In short

- **Chronosia has good parts and no spine.** The six quests, the 19 finds, the case and the three trails were built at different times. Nothing says why any of it is happening.
- **Your own material already holds a spine.** Zara's profile says: "too much time travel is ripping holes in the universe." [Source] I propose that as the one cause. It is your decision 1.
- **I found 33 contradictions and loose threads.** Section 7. Each has a fix. Five are faults a child could catch.
- **Of 30 tasks: 12 keep, 10 rework, 5 merge into others, 3 become scenery.** Nothing a child has earned is lost. Section 4.
- **Your lantern chain of today is section 5.** The cave is black. Nothing can be done there without the lantern.
- **Thirteen decisions are yours.** Section 11.

### The story in one breath [Proposal]

1. The Council of Timekeepers mends trouble in other times, through a time portal.
2. Eight missions a year the sky can bear, but extra trips by night have torn it.
3. The tear set off Time Quakes, and the quakes stopped the clocks and the day.
4. The cadet starts the day again, holds the seventh quake, and plans the missions so no trip is wasted.
5. If the cadet also stops the night trips, the crack in the sky fades.

## 1. The story on one page

Everything here is [Proposal] unless tagged.

### What happened on Chronosia

1. **Time runs at different speeds in different places.** A river that rushes and crawls. A mountain that grows younger as you climb. A sky in stripes, each colour its own time zone. [Source: your Chronosia page]
2. **A Clockmaker built the great machines:** the great clock with its eight small clocks, the Time Stabilizer, and the time portal. [Built: the Clockmaker, version 87]
3. **The Council of Timekeepers uses the portal for its missions:** Atlantis, the dinosaurs, the black hole, and the rest of your booklet. [Source: the eight missions. Built: the chart house map shows "every place the portal reaches".] The portal makes the jump. On the far side, a mission may still need a ship or a submarine. [Source: your booklet's pages]
4. **Every trip tears the sky a little.** [Source: Zara's profile, "too much time travel is ripping holes in the universe".] A small tear mends by itself. The sky can bear the eight missions of the Annual Timetable. It cannot bear more.
5. **The extra trips are the trouble.** Members slip through by night: a quick errand, a look at a favourite place. There are a few more each year.
6. **The Clockmaker saw the first thin crack, years ago.** The Clockmaker built a brass cougar to guard the portal by night. The Council said no. They did not believe a few errands could hurt.
7. **So the Clockmaker trusted the future instead.** The cougar was left on its rock, not wound. Its key was set adrift on the river. Notes were left for whoever came later. Then the Clockmaker went through the portal, one way.
8. **Today the tear grew too big.** Six Time Quakes came this afternoon, each longer than the last. [Source: your Task 2] They stopped the clocks. [Source: your Task 1] With the great clock stopped, the day cannot turn. [Source: your Task 1, "the day-night cycle is disrupted"]
9. **Chronosia called for help.** [Source: "a mysterious call from the newly discovered world of Chronosia"]

### Why the cadet comes

- Chronosia called. [Source]
- The ship needs an advanced Engine Core, and Chronosia has one. "The Chronosians insist we help them first." [Source]
- **Today the game says neither.** A child lands and reads: "Time is broken here. Walk around. Find who needs help." [Built] That breaks your rule 5: a child must arrive knowing what happened and why they are here.

### What the cadet does

**The main road** (your four tasks of 2023, in your order):
1. **The Clock Tower.** Set the eight slipped clocks true. The great clock can run. The day can turn again.
2. **The Quake Station.** A day that turns lets the seventh quake come. Work it out to the minute, and hold it.
3. **The Council Hall.** The ground is safe. The sky is still torn. Choose one leader for each mission. Then find the mission each member cannot finish. No trip is wasted.

**Off the main road** (nobody sends you):
- **The case:** who used the portal last night?
- **The guard:** the tortoise's eggs lead to the pond, the pond to the Clockmaker's note, the note to the key, the key to the cougar.
- **The black cave:** a lantern from another planet lights the way to the Clockmaker's workshop.
- **The Backward Mountain:** Sara's time capsule.

### How it ends, and what is different afterwards

| What the cadet did | What a child can see afterwards |
|---|---|
| Set the eight clocks | The tower ticks. The tiny clocks in the hollow tree tick together. |
| Held the seventh quake | The needle draws a flat line. The digger sits outside its burrow. The landing words no longer say "time is broken". |
| Planned the missions | The Council gives the Engine Core, and the name Timekeeper Cadet. |
| Closed the case | The portal is locked by night, or one member owes you a secret. |
| Woke the guard | The rock is empty. The cougar sits by the portal. |
| Carried the lantern in | The cave is lit. The workshop is open. |

**The crack is the scoreboard.** It fades only when the night trips really stop: the Council locks the portal by night, or the guard takes its post. If the cadet keeps the secret and never wakes the guard, the crack stays, and one day the quakes will come back. That is a choice that matters.
