# Should a higher attack get past a lower shield or dodge card?

**Built on 22 November, after you said it a third time.** Section 10 is now in the battle, as its starting rule. What was built, and the five readings I made, are in `GMQ_Card_Battle_Practice_v1_2026-11-22.md`, section 1f. Dodge cards (section 4) are not built.

## 1. Your question

> "Should an epic attack be able to bypass a rare shield card? Or we should have a common and a rare card that is like Wormhole Zip and Triple Illusion. Cards at those levels that can avoid being hit. But a higher attack can still hit. It may miss the AP. But the gold attack card can still hit it. And if the gold attack card is a multiplier for the AP then that would probably be able to hit..."

I read three questions in it.

1. Does an epic attack card get past a lower shield card?
2. Should there be dodge cards at common and rare, which a higher attack card can still hit?
3. Could a dodge card make only part of the attack miss: the ship's own AP, but not what the card adds?

## 2. What the page does today

| Card played against a higher attack card | Today |
|---|---|
| **A block card** ("Block 8... or bring back a broken shield") | **Does nothing.** No block, no shield. This is your rule of 22 November. |
| **A card that adds to the Main Shield** (Shield Spark, Nano Shield, Dome Top-Up, Quarter Dome) | **Works in full.** A common Shield Spark on a broken shield soaks up a whole epic Hundred Cannon. |

**The middle row is a hole I left.** Your kind rule was written for block cards. When the five shield-adding cards became shield cards today, they did not get the rule. So right now the answer to your first question is "yes for ten cards, no for five". A child cannot be expected to know which is which.

## 3. One law that makes it hang together

**Kind beats kind.**

> A shield card or a dodge card answers a plain attack, or an attack card of its own kind or lower. A higher attack card gets past it.

- It is your rule of 22 November, said once for every card that defends.
- Common, rare and epic then mean the same thing on attack cards, shield cards and dodge cards.
- A child needs one sentence, and the rim colour tells them the kind.

**What "gets past" means for each sort of card:**

| Card | A higher attack card gets past it, so... |
|---|---|
| Block | it blocks nothing (as today). |
| Dodge | you are hit. |
| Adds to the Main Shield | **two choices, below.** |

**The choice for cards that add to the shield:**

- **(a) The card does nothing.** Simplest. Cost: the child's card is gone for nothing, and they could not have known if they went first.
- **(b) The shield goes up too late.** The higher attack lands first. Then the card's shield goes up, ready for the next round. Cost: one more idea to explain ("a higher card is faster"). Gain: the card is never wasted. **I would choose (b).**

## 4. Dodge cards at common and rare

**Your 2023 deck had them.** 21 cards in the November deck said "you cannot be hit this round": 2 common, 10 advanced, 9 epic. [Source: the review of your old cards]

**The problem to solve first.** After your shield rule, a common block card on a broken shield already stops a whole attack. A dodge card does the same, and also saves a shield that is still standing. So a plain common dodge would be better than every common block card. Nobody would want the block cards.

**So a dodge needs a price.** One that fits the story:

- **Wormhole Zip (common):** your ship jumps away. Nothing hits you. **You do not fire this round either.**
- **Triple Illusion (rare):** the same, and it also fools a rare attack card.
- **Vanishing Act (epic, in the set now):** nothing hits you, **and you still fire.** That is what makes it epic.

"Do I dodge and lose my shot, or take the hit and shoot?" is a real choice for a third grader.

**They should replace two block cards,** not be added. The deck already has 15 cards that can stop a hit.

---

## 5. "It may miss the AP, but the card still hits"

Your idea: a lower dodge makes the ship's own attack miss, but the part a higher card adds still lands.

- **It works for a card that adds.** 55 from the ship misses. 20 from the card hits.
- **It does not work for a card that multiplies,** as you saw yourself. A doubled 55 has no "ship part" and "card part". Both epic attack cards in the set work on the AP: Doubling Drive and Hundred Cannon.
- **So it would need two rules:** one for adders, one for multipliers. And the child would have to keep two numbers apart in every attack.

> **Correction, the same day.** I was wrong that a multiplier cannot be split. The page already writes every attack as ship plus card: "110 = 55 + 55" for Doubling Drive, "100 = 55 + 45" for Hundred Cannon. So the card's part is always a number the child can see. Section 10 uses that.

**(What I wrote first:) I would not split the attack.** All or nothing, by kind. A higher attack card hits with everything. It is one rule, and it gives the answer you wanted for the multiplier case anyway.

## 6. What a rough trial showed

2,000 battles for each line, two computer players, outside the page. Rough numbers, no test behind them.

| | A battle lasts (random play / clever play) | An epic attack card hurts no part |
|---|---|---|
| **Today** | 11 / 14 rounds | 53 to 62 times in 100 |
| **Kind beats kind for every shield card, choice (a)** | 11 / 14 | 46 to 53 |
| **The same, choice (b)** | 11 / 14 | 46 to 53 |
| **And two block cards become dodge cards, free** | 11 / 14 | 46 to 53 |
| **And a common or rare dodge costs your own attack** | 12 / 15 | 47 to 55 |

**What I take from it**

1. **None of these changes the length of a battle much.** You can choose on what is clearest for a child.
2. **An epic attack card is played only once or twice in a battle.** So the rule decides little. What it gives is fairness: the same answer every time.
3. **The real surprise is the right-hand column.** Even with the rule, an epic attack card hurts no part about half the time. The cards are not what stops it. **The Main Shield is.** By your rule "nothing carries over", a shield with 1 HP left soaks up Hundred Cannon whole.

## 7. One more option, because of point 3

If an epic attack should feel epic, the lever is the shield rule, not the shield cards.

- **Option: an epic attack card breaks through.** What the Main Shield cannot hold goes on to the parts. Common and rare attacks stay as now: nothing carries over.
- **Cost:** it changes your rule "nothing carries over" for two cards. It is one more thing to learn.
- **I have not measured it.** Say the word and I will, before you decide.

## 8. What I would do, in order

1. **Kind beats kind, for every card that defends.** It closes the hole in section 2.
2. **Choice (b)** for the cards that add to the shield: too late, not wasted.
3. **Two dodge cards with a price:** Wormhole Zip (common) and Triple Illusion (rare), in place of two block cards.
4. **No splitting of an attack.**
5. **Think about section 7.** It is the one that would change how a battle feels.

## 9. What could prove this wrong

- **Computer players do not bluff.** A child who goes second may hold an epic attack card just to beat a shield card. That is good play. It could also make going first feel bad. Watch for it.
- **The trial's last column is a blunt measure.** "Hurts no part" counts a hit on a standing shield as no hurt.
- **A price on dodging may feel like a punishment** to a child who only wanted to be safe. Only play will show.

## 10. Your second idea: the shield carries a kind

> "Or any newly rebuilt shield could be labeled as common or rare, and still blocks the AP. Special attacks could still damage the ship parts."

**How I read it**

- A shield that a card puts back up is **common, rare or epic, like the card that made it.**
- **It always stops the ship's own attack** (the AP).
- **The part that a higher attack card adds goes through** to the parts. A rare or epic attack card is the "special attack".
- An attack card of the shield's own kind, or lower, is stopped whole, as now.

**An example.** Your shield is broken. You play Dust Screen (common): a green shield of 8 goes up. The enemy plays Twin Tens (rare): its attack is 75, shown at the top as "55 + 20".
- The 55 goes into your green shield. The shield breaks and soaks it up.
- The 20 is rare, so it goes through. You share 20 out between your parts.

**Why I think this is better than my "kind beats kind"**

1. **A child can see it.** The bubble can be drawn green, purple or gold. A purple attack card goes through a green bubble. No rule to remember: look at the two colours.
2. **A shield card is never wasted.** It always stops the ship's guns. My version needed "too late" to save the card.
3. **The numbers on attack cards start to matter.** Until now a hit either lands or it does not. Here "+20" means 20 gets through.
4. **It uses what is already on the screen:** "75 = 55 + 20".
5. **It is the same idea as your dodge sentence:** "It may miss the AP, but the gold attack card can still hit." One law for both:

> **A lower shield or dodge stops the ship's own attack. The part a higher attack card adds still gets through.**

**A rough trial** (2,000 battles each, two computer players, outside the page)

| | Today | Your idea |
|---|---|---|
| **A battle lasts (random play / clever play)** | 11 / 14 rounds | 11 / 14 rounds |
| **A special attack goes through a weaker shield** | never | about once every one or two battles |
| **How much goes through, each time** | | about 31 points |
| **A rare attack card hurts a part** | 36 to 41 times in 100 | 44 to 46 |
| **An epic attack card hurts a part** | 38 to 47 times in 100 | 47 to 55 |
| **Battles that got stuck** | 0 | 0 |

- **Battle length does not move.**
- **Rare and epic attack cards become a little stronger,** which is what you were after.
- The computer does not yet aim a special attack at a weak shield. A child would. So expect more than the trial shows.
