Three ways to draw Planet Chronosia

Every picture below is drawn by code, and it stays still. Each one shows the same places: the Clock Tower, the Quake Station, the Tortoise Grove, the Council Hall, Cougar Rock, the river and the bridge.

Today

Voltara is a painted view from the side. Chronosia is a board of squares seen from above. They do not look like the same game.

Choice 1 · Proposal

Dress the squares

What changes: the same map, the same squares, the same walking. Only the paint is new: evening light, long shadows, a river that shows the striped sky, buildings that stand up.

Cost: the smallest. About one working session for each planet. No test has to change, because nothing moves.

Risk: low. It is still a board. Two green planets will still look like cousins unless each gets its own light and ground.

Choice 2 · Proposal

A big view when you arrive

What changes: each planet gets one painted view from the ground, like Voltara. A child sees it on arrival, and again at each place (the Clock Tower, the Council Hall). The walking map stays as it is.

Cost: middle. One big picture for each planet, then a smaller one for each place inside it. Chronosia has five places. This is also the picture your arrival screens need (job A).

Risk: the map between the views is still the plain board, so the jump between the two looks will show.

Choice 3 · Proposal

A painted map with no squares showing

What changes: the map becomes one picture. The river bends. The path winds. Trees grow in clumps. The squares are still there underneath for walking, but a child does not see them.

Cost: the largest. Each planet's map is placed by hand. The walking tests must be checked again, because a tap has to land on a square nobody can see. Xylophora cannot use it: there the squares are the maths.

Risk: a child may not know where they can walk. The ring of twelve stones is my idea, not yours. It can come out.
