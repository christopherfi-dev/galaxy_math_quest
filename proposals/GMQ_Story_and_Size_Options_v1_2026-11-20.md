## 6. Ways to make a place bigger [Proposal]

More rooms is only one way, and not the best.

| | Choice | Example on the ship | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Wider:** more rooms | a cargo hold, crew cabins, a lower deck | drawing, and each room needs a reason to be there. Empty rooms are worse than none. |
| 2 | **Deeper:** the same rooms change | the night before, launch morning, the ship's return | less drawing, more story. Six rooms in three states is more to explore than eighteen that never change. |
| 3 | **Longer:** the quest leaves the place | after lift-off you follow the signal through space to where it comes from | a new small place. It gives the radio its ending. It uses space travel. |
| 4 | **Fuller:** more to find | secrets, things that talk back, a thing here that opens something there | cheap for each one. This is what the station has and the ship lacks. |

**What you said you enjoyed** was going back and forth and looking at different things. That is choice 2 and choice 4 more than choice 1.

---

## 7. What could prove this wrong

- **Story may not be what a nine-year-old misses.** You are an adult who has played story games. Your students wanted to go on through break on the station, which has the least story of all. Ask them what they want more of before building it.
- **A spine can turn into reading.** Every report home is more text on a screen. You have told me commands are three or four words and the game is mostly visual.
- **"Find a new home" has a hard ending.** If the class picks a home, the game is over. If it never picks, the spine is a decoration.
- **Deeper costs testing.** Every state a room can be in is a state that can break.
