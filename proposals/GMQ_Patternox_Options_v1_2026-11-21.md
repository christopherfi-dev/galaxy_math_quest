## 1. The facts

- **You are right: Patterns is not an AERO domain.** Your AERO document has 25 Grade 3 standards in 5 domains: Operations and Algebraic Thinking, Base Ten, Fractions, Measurement and Data, and Geometry. Patterns is one standard, 3.OA.9, inside the first. [Source: `proposals/GMQ_AERO_Grade3_Map_v1_2026-11-19.md`, from your AERO document]
- **But patterns are the doorway to algebra,** which is why your school kept them. The "Algebraic Thinking" half of that domain climbs through the grades, and you teach where it ends:
  - Grade 3: patterns and unknowns (8 × ? = 48)
  - Grade 4: a rule makes a pattern
  - Grade 5: two rules make pairs, which are plotted on a grid
  - Grade 6: letters for numbers, expressions and equations
  - then functions
- **Two Grade 3 standards show nothing in the game,** and both are algebraic thinking [Source: the same map's table]:
  - **3.OA.4:** find the unknown, as in 8 × ? = 48
  - **3.OA.5:** the properties of ×: 4 × 6 = 6 × 4, and 7 × 8 = 7 × 5 + 7 × 3. This one is also on your list of four standards with nothing (`GMQ_Last_Four_Standards_Options_v1_2026-11-19.md`).
  - And **3.OA.9**, patterns, is only "light" so far.
- **Patternox is still an empty shell in the game.** It has a title, a broken wormhole, and a lock that waits on a Numerix Quest 2 that was never written [Conflict: `design/GMQ_World_Map_v1_2026-11-16.md` §7]. So whatever we choose costs nothing to undo.

## 3. My recommendation: C now, with coding inside it (E) later

### What "the galaxy of rules" would hold

- **The wormhole repair is the way in.** The gate is broken because its rule is broken. Find the rule, and Patternox opens. This also fixes the lock that waits on a quest nobody wrote.
- **Number machines** (3.OA.9): a machine turns numbers into other numbers. Feed it, watch it, find its rule, then make it open a door. [Source: your "Cracking the Cosmic Code", Ways to Play number 10]
- **Missing parts** (3.OA.4): a machine's dial reads 8 × ? = 48. The child finds the missing number, and the door opens.
- **Split and swap** (3.OA.5): a power core too big to carry. The child splits 7 × 8 into 7 × 5 and 7 × 3, two loads that add up to the same, and sees that the answer stays the same.

### Coding without copying code.org

- **Pushback on blocks:**
  - Code.org already exists. It is free, and it does blocks better than we could.
  - Dragging blocks on an iPad is fiddly on a small screen.
  - A block editor is a big build inside one file.
  - CLAUDE.md's rule is not to copy a feature because it is common.
- **Your game already is code.** Children type commands: go north, push crate, use wrench. A program is just a list of those, with one new word, *repeat*.
  - The child taps command chips into a list, as they already tap words.
  - "Repeat 4: move 3" is 4 × 3 = 12, so a loop is multiplication (3.OA.1). Finding the wrong step is checking an answer (3.OA.8).
  - It is meaningful because the code does maths work in the story: a probe must land on an exact square.
- **Comets** are already yours [Source: your opening, "study comets"]. Coding Comets could be short events in space, not a galaxy: a comet passes, and its probe must be programmed before it is out of reach. They could come after Patternox, with the same command chips.

### Why this is the meaningful version

- It covers the two Grade 3 standards that have nothing yet.
- It starts the line you already teach in algebra, so Grade 4, 5 and 6 bands can grow out of it (see `GMQ_Multi_Grade_Analysis_v1_2026-11-21.md`).
- Coding appears where it serves the maths, not as a separate subject.

---

## 4. What could prove this wrong

- **Your school's report card may use five domains with Patterns.** If so, keep the name Patternox, so the game matches what parents and the school see, even though AERO does not list it.
- **Children may want coding for its own sake,** and code.org may serve them better in its own lesson. That is fine: the game does not need to do everything.
- **A planet of rules can feel abstract.** It must be told as machines, doors and power cores, never as "find the rule" on a blank screen. If it feels like a worksheet, it has failed.
