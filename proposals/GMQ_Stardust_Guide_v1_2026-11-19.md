## 5. The four quests

They can be done in any order. Nothing tells the child which is first.

### Quest 1. Chart the ship (your Task 2, Level 1)

**The purpose.** Mission Control: "This ship leaves at dawn. But the sensors on board are dead. How warm is each of the five rooms? Send us a chart."

**See it first.** [Source: your rule] The first time a child opens the wall chart, an old chart is still pinned there: five finished bars, how many lamps each room has. One bar is read aloud: "Find the Greenhouse bar. It ends three lines above 20. Each line is a step of 2. So the Greenhouse has 26 lamps." Then: **Start a new chart**.

Copying the old chart does not work. It is about lamps.

**What the child must work out, with no steps given:**

1. Each room has a thermometer. Go and read it. Write it on the clipboard.
2. Set five bars on the wall chart. Each line on the chart is a step of 2.
3. Mission Control then asks: "Now look at the sign above each thermometer. One room is too hot or too cold for its sign. Which room? By how many degrees?"

**What happens when it is wrong:**

- A wrong chart: "3 of the five bars do not match." It does not say which.
- A wrong room: "Engineers hurry to the Greenhouse. It is just as its sign says."
- Too few degrees: "The Engine Room is still not as its sign says."
- Too many: "It is not as its sign says. But it is not 6 degrees out."

**The signs are not on the clipboard.** A child who forgot them must walk back and read them. That is on purpose.

### Quest 2. The last-quarter locker (your Task 1)

**The purpose.** The Supply Chief: "Months ten, eleven and twelve. Help me fill the locker for them. There are four of us. Each one needs 20 energy points a month. Nothing in the locker may go bad before the trip ends. And there must be exactly enough. The locker is small: ten packs of any one food, and no more."

**What is not given:** the whole amount. The child must find 4 people × 20 points × 3 months = 240.

**The shelf** has six of your foods. Four last the trip. Two do not. The table shows the energy in one pack and how many months it lasts.

**What happens when it is wrong:**

- A food that goes bad: "Month ten comes. The crew opens it. One kind of food has gone bad."
- Too little: "The food is good. But it runs out before the trip ends."
- Too much: "The door will not shut."

**There are many right answers.** At least 13 for every cadet. Half the cadets have 100 or more.

### Quest 3. The fuel (your Task 2, Levels 2 and 3)

**Part one, the log.** Two bar graphs from the last trip: fuel burned, and how far the ship went. "In one month, something went badly wrong. Which month?"

- One month burned the most fuel and went the least far. That is the solar wind. [Source]
- **Another month burned just as much, but went the farthest.** [Proposal] So the fuel graph alone cannot answer. The child needs both graphs.
- The gravity month burned the least. A child who picks it hears: "A planet pulled the ship along that month. Nothing went wrong."

**Part two, the tanks.** "Fill the tanks. Every one to the top, and not a drop over."

- Each tank has a sign: "MAIN TANK. HOLDS 200 LITRES WHEN FULL."
- Each gauge is one line from EMPTY to FULL, cut into equal parts. A needle stands at one of the marks.
- **See it first:** a small glass test tank stands in the Engine Room. You can see the fuel in it: 3 of its 5 parts. Its gauge is drawn right under it, with the needle on the third mark. It shows what a gauge means. It gives no litres and does no sum. A child finds it by looking, or from the first nudge.
- The pump has a dial for each tank: how many litres to add.
- Too little: "The Main needle stops short of FULL." Too much: "The Reserve tank overflows. Fuel runs across the floor!"
- A tank that is right stays full. The others are let back out.

### Quest 4. What the crew ate (your Task 2, Level 4)

**The purpose.** The Supply Chief: "The fresh food ran out. We know that. But of the kinds on both charts, one fell far more than the others. Which kind? By how many units?"

- Two bar graphs: the first three months and the last three. Each line is 50.
- Fruits and vegetables are only on the first. Canned and freeze dried are only on the second. They are there to be set aside.
- Four kinds are on both. One of them goes up a little, as grains did in your data. [Source]
- Wrong kind: "Another kind fell further than dairy." Wrong number: "It fell by more than 150."

### The end

When all four are done, Mission Control says: "Rooms, fuel, food. All clear! Operation Stardust is go." The cadet earns a gold card.

### The nudges

A child gets a nudge only by tapping **Hint**. Each quest has two to four. They point at where to look, never at an answer. After the last one: "That is all the help there is. The rest is yours to work out."

## 8. A key for you

Each child has their own numbers, so there is no one answer sheet. This is how to check a child's work at a glance.

**The room:** read the five signs. One thermometer is outside its sign. The answer is how far outside.

| Room | Its sign |
|---|---|
| Control Room | 18 to 24 degrees |
| Living Quarters | 18 to 24 degrees |
| Greenhouse | 24 to 28 degrees |
| Food Storage | 2 to 6 degrees |
| Engine Room | 20 to 28 degrees |

**The locker:** 4 × the Chief's number × 3. That is 120 or 240. Only foods that last 12 months or more. Packs × energy must add up to exactly that.

**The month:** the tallest fuel bar that sits above the shortest distance bar.

**A tank:** litres in one part = what the tank holds ÷ the number of parts. Then × the parts that are empty.

| Tank | 2 parts | 4 parts | 5 parts | 10 parts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main, 200 litres | 100 each | 50 each | 40 each | 20 each |
| Backup, 50 litres | 25 each | — | 10 each | 5 each |
| Reserve, 20 litres | 10 each | 5 each | 4 each | 2 each |

**The food:** for Protein, Dairy, Grains and Snacks, take the second chart from the first. The biggest answer wins.

**The cans:** beds × litres ÷ the can.

**The signal:** it adds the same number each time.

**The Teacher's log** (More, Teacher) shows each job when it is finished: the try it was solved on, and how many nudges were used. A try is a wrong answer, or the last right one. So a child who gets everything right is "solved on try 1".

This guide is in a public repository. A student who finds it has these tables.

## 12. What could prove this wrong

- **Reading a thermometer may be the hard part, not the graph.** Each small line is 1 degree. Only the tens have numbers. If children misread by one line, the chart will keep saying "1 bar does not match" and they will not know why. Watch the first child at a thermometer.
- **The locker table was too big for an iPad mini,** for cadets with long food names. The question slid out of view when the number keys opened. A test caught it only because it failed one run in three. The table is smaller now, and the test always uses the six longest names. It is the kind of fault only a real iPad mini will fully settle.
- **"Breaks its plate" was not clear.** It was my phrase. You asked what it meant on 20 November. The words are changed: see the top of this guide. The new words have not been tried by anyone either.
- **The locker may be guess-and-fix, not planning.** "Too little" and "too much" let a child creep up on the answer one pack at a time. That is still adding and multiplying, but it is not a plan. Look at how many tries the log shows.
- **Children may never find the whole amount.** If nobody multiplies 4 × 20 × 3, the locker is the wrong place to hide it.
- **The fuel log can be solved by guessing.** Six months, six tries. The log will show it: a child who thought needs one or two tries.
- **Tapping the chart may not be obvious.** The desk says "Tap the big chart to set each bar." If children type instead, Quest 1 stalls.
- **I cannot tell how long it takes.** My guess is 45 to 80 minutes over two sittings. [Estimate]
- **Four quests may be one too many** after your own verdict that Stardust was too long. Each is shorter than your lesson was. Together they are not short.
- **"See it first" here is a picture and three sentences.** On your slides it is a talk: "which is easier, the paragraph or the graph?" A screen cannot hold that talk. If the finished chart is skipped past with one tap, it has shown nothing.
- **What your Atlantis unit has and this does not:** sorting real cards by hand, a twist ("it seems like a perfect success... but is it?"), and an ending where the child explains in words. Here every ending is a number.

**What I would watch with one child:** do they go and read the thermometers without being told; do they write the signs down on paper; what is their first try at the locker; do they look at both fuel graphs.
