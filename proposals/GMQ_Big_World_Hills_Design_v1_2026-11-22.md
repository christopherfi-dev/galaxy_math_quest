## 1. The story, told before any maths

East of the Reach a wall of stone holds up the hill. A lift goes up it. On top are fields in steps: the terraces of the Furrows, tall quiet farmers.

Told by the head planter, in four short pages:

1. "Planting day is here. Every field must get exactly its seedlings. Too few leaves bare earth. Too many, and they choke each other."
2. "We Furrows count with knot cords. We have cords for ones, twos, fives and tens. That is all."
3. "For anything bigger, the Makers gave us the counting frame. When the Gate broke, it burnt. Now a field of 7 rows of 14 is more than we can count."
4. "Help us plant. A big field is only small fields, side by side."

**What is wrong:** the Furrows can only count parts with a side of 1, 2, 5 or 10.
**What the cadet does:** turns and fences each field into parts the Furrows can count, and tells them how many seedlings to carry up.

## 2. The big quest: planting day

### What the child looks at

One terrace fills the picture, seen from above: a rectangle of bare earth with a row of posts along two sides. The posts are numbered at 5 and 10, so its sides can be read: 7 by 14. **No squares are drawn inside.** The seedlings cannot be counted one by one; there is nothing to count yet.

### What the child does

- **Turn it** (swap): one tap turns the field a quarter turn. 7 rows of 14 becomes 14 rows of 7. The Furrows' fence only runs across the rows, so turning decides which side can be cut.
- **Fence it** (split): the child taps a post. A fence runs across the field from that post. The field is now two fields. A third tap makes three.
- **Say each part:** each part shows its two sides, for example 7 by 10 and 7 by 4. The child types how many seedlings each part takes.
- **Say the whole field,** and tap "Carry them up".

### What the world does

- **A part the Furrows cannot count** (no side of 1, 2, 5 or 10): "The planters look at their cords, and shake their heads. This part is still too big for us." Nothing is planted. The child fences again.
- **Right:** the Furrows carry the seedlings up, part by part. The field turns green.
- **Wrong:** they plant what the child said. A strip is left bare, or seedlings are left in a heap by the wall. No number is said. The child can pull them up and try again.

### The fields

Four terraces. Each child's are their own.

| Terrace | Sides | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The low terrace | both from 6 to 9: say 7 by 8 | A fact many children half know. The split makes it sure |
| Two middle terraces | one side 3 to 8, the other 11 to 16, never over 100 in all: say 7 by 14 | **Bigger than the times table.** Here the split is the only way, not a ritual |
| **The crooked terrace** | an L shape: two rectangles joined | The open problem. It can be cut two ways. The parts differ; the whole does not |

- **The crooked terrace lights the Rule Stone.** The child chooses where to cut. Both cuts are right. The head planter says: "Two ways to cut it. One field. The same seedlings."
- **More than one split is always right.** 7 by 14 can be 7 by 10 and 7 by 4. Or 7 by 5, 7 by 5 and 7 by 4. Or turned, 14 by 5 and 14 by 2.

### The finished example

A quilt on the head planter's wall. About **patches of cloth**, not seedlings.
- "My grandmother's quilt is 6 patches by 12. Too many for my cords."
- "She sewed it as two quilts: 6 by 10, and 6 by 2."
- "6 tens are 60. 6 twos are 12. 60 and 12 make 72 patches."
- Picture: the quilt, with a seam where the two parts meet. The patches are drawn here, because this is the example.

No child gets a field of 6 by 12.

### What the child wins

- The Furrows' Rule Stone.
- **The ship's Shield goes to Level 2:** "The Furrows line your ship's Shield with terrace stone. It has held up a hill for a thousand years."
- A card. And a rope ladder: a tool, for places already seen (the west cliff in the Meadow has a ledge below it).

## 3. The numbers, to be checked on 20,000 children before building

The rule is not written yet. It must keep:
- every side from 3 to 16; every whole field 100 or less; every part's count a fact with 1, 2, 5 or 10
- every field can be split into two or three parts the Furrows can count, in at least two different ways
- the crooked terrace has exactly two single cuts, and both give the same total
- no field is 6 by 12 (the quilt)
- a wrong total is never the total of some other allowed split (so "wrong" is never ambiguous)

## 7. What could prove this wrong

- **A child who knows 7 × 8 will not see why to fence it.** That is why only the first field is inside the times table.
- **7 × 14 may be too much** for some of your class. If so, the middle terraces could stop at 12.
- **Fencing with a finger on an iPad** must be easy. A tap on a numbered post, not a drag.
- **"Parts the Furrows can count"** is a made-up limit. It may feel unfair: "why can't they just count?" The cords must be seen in the picture, so the limit is a thing, not a rule.
- **Xylophora already has area.** Your class may have done L shapes there. Then the crooked terrace is practice, not new.
- **The wrong-answer picture** (a bare strip, or a heap) shows too few or too many. That is more than the fleet's red lamp tells. It is what a real field would show.
- **Nobody has seen any of it.**
