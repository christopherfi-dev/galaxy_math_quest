# The Terrace Hills: design for region 3 of Planet Algora

**Date:** 22 November 2026, night. **For:** Marc. **From:** the big world thread.
**Nothing here is built.** Everything is **[Proposal]** unless it says [Source] or [built].

**This is version 2, written the same night.** An independent reviewer found two blockers in version 1:
- The Furrows could "only count parts with a side of 1, 2, 5 or 10". By that rule my own example (7 by 10 and 7 by 4) was not allowed. **The world would have refused correct maths.** Children would learn to guess the planter's rule, not the property.
- With that rule most fields had exactly one allowed split. It was a procedure, not an open problem.

So that rule is gone. **In this version the world never refuses correct maths.** What changed is in section 9. **Version 2 has not been reviewed again.** Please read it before I build.

---

## 1. The story, told before any maths

East of the Reach a wall of stone holds up the hill. On top are fields in steps: the terraces of the Furrows, tall quiet farmers.

Told by the head planter, in four short pages:

1. "Planting day is here. Every field must get exactly its seedlings. Too few leaves bare earth. Too many, and they choke each other."
2. "We sing our times tables as we plant. Our songs stop at ten tens."
3. "For a field bigger than the songs, the Makers gave us a counting frame. When the Gate broke, it burnt. Now a field of 14 rows is more than we can sing."
4. "You know numbers, sky-walker. How many seedlings for each field?"

**What is wrong:** the fields are bigger than the times table, and the frame that counted them is burnt.
**What the cadet does:** works out each field, and shows the Furrows a way they can keep: a big field is small fields, side by side.

## 2. The big quest: planting day

### What the child looks at

One terrace fills the picture, from above: bare earth, drawn as rows. Each row is one line of earth. **No seedlings are drawn,** so there is nothing to count one by one. Two numbers are on the wall: how many rows, and how many seedlings in a row.

### The one rule of the land, with its reason

**"A row can be at most 10 long. The planting cart holds 10 seedlings."**
It is told before the first field. It is a thing in the picture: the cart, with ten cups.

### What the child may do. None of it is forced.

- **Turn the field** (swap). A field of 7 rows of 14 has rows too long for the cart. One tap turns it: 14 rows of 7. "The same field. The same seedlings."
- **Fence it** (split). Tap between any two rows. A fence goes in. 14 rows of 7 becomes 10 rows of 7, and 4 rows of 7. **Any place is allowed.** 7 and 7. 12 and 2. Two fences, if the child likes.
- **Say each part,** if there are parts: how many seedlings.
- **Say the whole field.**
- A child who can work out 14 × 7 with no fence may just say it. The fence is a tool, not a ritual.

### What the world does

- **Right:** the Furrows carry the seedlings up and plant, row by row. The field turns green. They sing the child's split: "Ten sevens, and four sevens!"
- **Wrong:** "They plant. It does not come out even. They pull the seedlings up again." No number, and no "too few" or "too many".
- **A row longer than 10:** the cart cannot do it. "Turn the field first." This is the rule of the land, told before. It is not a refusal of maths.

### The fields

| Field | Rows and row length | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The low terrace | both from 6 to 9: say 7 rows of 8 | A fact many children half know. Fencing is offered, not needed |
| Two middle terraces | one side 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 or 9; the other 11 to 16; never over 100 in all. Say 7 by 14 | **Bigger than the times table.** Here turning and fencing are how it gets done |
| **The top terrace** | see below | The open problem. It lights the Rule Stone |

### The open problem: two crops on the top terrace

- **The purpose:** "The top terrace feeds us all winter. This year we have 60 seedlings for it, beans and gourds mixed as you choose. Its rows are 12 long. Every seedling must go in, and every row must be full."
- **The bare facts:** 60 seedlings. Rows of 12, which is too long for the cart. Two crops.
- **No steps.**
- **What the child must find:** the rows are too long: turn it, or split each row's 12 into 10 and 2. How many rows do 60 seedlings make? Then: how many rows of beans, how many of gourds?
- **More than one answer is right:** 1 and 4. 2 and 3. 3 and 2. 4 and 1.
- **The child's choice stays.** The top terrace grows what the child planted, in two colours, on the map, for good. The head planter says what it will mean: "More beans than gourds. A lean winter for soup, a good one for bread." [Your rule 4: your decision must matter.]
- **The idea underneath:** 3 rows of 12 and 2 rows of 12 are 5 rows of 12. That is the same property, the other way round.

### The finished example

A quilt on the head planter's wall. About **patches of cloth**.
- "My grandmother's quilt is 13 rows of 6 patches. Too many for my songs."
- "She sewed it as two quilts: 10 rows, and 3 rows."
- "Ten sixes are 60. Three sixes are 18. 60 and 18 make 78 patches."
- "Any seam would do. She chose the one she could sing."

No child gets a field of 13 by 6.

### What the child wins

- The Furrows' Rule Stone.
- **The ship's Shield goes to Level 2:** "The Furrows line your ship's Shield with terrace stone. It has held up a hill for a thousand years."
- A card. And a rope ladder: a tool, for places already seen.

## 3. The numbers, to be checked on 20,000 children before building

The rule is not written yet. It must keep:
- the short side is 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 or 9 (never 5 or 10: those need no thought); the long side 11 to 16; the whole field 100 or less
- no field is 13 by 6 (the quilt)
- the top terrace: the seedlings divide by the row length into 4 to 8 rows, so there are at least three ways to share the rows between two crops
- **every split a child can make is accepted if its sums are right.** The check must try every place for one fence and two, and show the page marks each as right

## 7. What could prove this wrong

- **Too hard.** 14 × 7 and "60 in rows of 12" are at the top of Grade 3 or past it. If your class is not there, the long sides could stop at 12.
- **Too easy to skip.** A child may type totals and never fence. That is allowed. Then the split is never met. The Furrows' song, and the quilt, are the only teachers.
- **A wrong total gets no hint** but "it does not come out even". With a nudge button, as everywhere.
- **Tapping between rows** on a small screen. A box on the desk ("fence after row __") must do the same.
- **"The planting cart holds 10"** is a made-up limit. It is a thing in the picture, and it is told first. Watch whether children accept it or argue.
- **Nobody has seen any of it,** and this version has had no reviewer.

| The reviewer's fault | What I did |
|---|---|
| **The "1, 2, 5, 10" rule refused correct maths,** and my own example broke it | The rule is gone. Any split is accepted. So is no split |
| **Not an open problem:** one allowed split for most sides | The open problem is new: two crops on the top terrace, four right answers, and the choice stays |
