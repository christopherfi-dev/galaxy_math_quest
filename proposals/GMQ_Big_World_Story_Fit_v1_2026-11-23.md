One kind of problem, in many skins. Not seven thin tasks.

| Pick one from each row | |

| **From each** | plot, boat, tree, cart, cutter |

## 4. Where the maths does not fit yet

I counted every problem the calendar allows, with every fact inside 10 × 10 and every total 100 or less.

### 4a. The easiest maths is asked last

| The story's order | The maths |
|---|---|
| **A first:** how many plots? | need = plots × each × 7 count days. An unknown number, with two steps |
| **C second:** for how many months? | blocks = carts × each × months. The same |
| **B last:** four cuts on the wall; how much by Last Raft? | 6, 12, 18, 24, and on to the 7th. **This is your "3, 6, 9, 12". It is the plainest of the three** |

The order is A, C, B for a story reason: so that only the cadet's decision can cause a miss. For the maths it is hardest first. **A child should meet B's kind first.**

### 4b. Every decision has three numbers. You asked for multiples

[Source: the story, section 5] "Each decision has three numbers behind it: how many, how much each, how many count days."
You said: "For these they use the multiples." A and C as written are two steps, and one of them is finding an unknown.
**A way through, to try:** the keeper gives what *all* the town's plots bring on one count day. Then it is one row of multiples again. "Each plot brings 3, and we have 4 plots" becomes the harder size.

### 4c. Seven count days is tight

| The question | Different problems at Grade 3 size |
|---|---|
| B as written: always "by count day 7" | **8.** All of them the 7 times table |
| A as written: plots × each × 7, up to 100 | **14** |
| "How much by count day 3, 4, 5, 6 or 7?" | 40 |
| The same, if count days ran to 9 | 56 |
| The same, if count days ran to 10 | 64 |

- With 14 children and 8 problems, children share answers.
- Your "month 11", and your "24th day or 28th day", cannot be said in a year of seven count days.
- **Two ways out.** A longer year (the story's decision 2). Or a count day every week, so seven gold months hold 28 count days: then "by the 24th count day" can be asked, with a table and skip counting. The story says a month's length is "never said", so this is open.

### 4d. The chains from decision to consequence are long

- **A is short:** too few food plots, smaller bowls. Too many, fewer reeds, roofs drip. A Grade 3 child can follow that.
- **B is long:** say too much fish, the keeper sells fish the town does not have, a word at the lake is kept, the fish goes, the jars run out in White month 3.
- **C is long:** too many months, carts are paid in food, more food plots, fewer reed plots, roofs drip.
- Many of your children are learning English. Every link is a sentence to read, and a place to lose the thread between the number and what followed.

### 4e. "Nothing is carried over" against "what a child changes stays" [Conflict]

| | |
|---|---|
| The story | "Every year is fresh. A thin White month 3 ends at New Year." All that stays is a mark that a plan was made, and a keeper who has learned |
| You, 17 November | "Your decisions must matter" |
| You, 23 November | "A hole is dug. Someone build something incorrectly, then what happens then? So we need to show it" |

The story chose kindness: nothing bad lasts. That is right for 8-year-olds. But then very little a child does lasts either. **Yours to weigh.**

## 7. Examples, asked for by Marc the same day

Every number was worked out again. None of this is a design. They are examples of the kind.

### Question 1: plain multiples, and the two-step size

**Plain: one row.**

| | The problem | The answer | Too little | Too much |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| How much? | Reed Town's fish row has four cuts: 6, 12, 18, 24. "How many strings by Last Raft?" (the 7th count day) | 30, 36, **42** | Said 36: the keeper sells too little, and the lamps go out early | Said 48: she sells fish the town does not have |
| For how long? | "The wall needs 40 blocks. The carts bring 8 on each count day. For how many months do we hire them?" | 8, 16, 24, 32, 40: **5 months** | 4 months: the wall is 8 blocks short at one end | 6 months: a month of carts paid for and not needed |
| How many? | "The town needs 35 sacks by Last Raft. One plot fills 7 sacks in a year. How many plots do we sow with food?" | 7, 14, 21, 28, 35: **5 plots** | 4 plots: 28 sacks. The bowls are small in White month 3 | 6 plots: one plot of roof reed lost. A roof drips |

**Two steps: the harder size.**

| The problem | The answer | Why it is harder |
|---|---|---|
| "Each plot brings 3 sacks on a count day. We sowed 4 plots. How many sacks by Gold month 5?" | 12 each count day. 12, 24, 36, 48, **60** | Two things to hold: plots, and count days |
| The story's decision A as written: "The town needs 84 sacks by Last Raft. One plot brings 3 on each count day. How many plots?" | One plot brings 21 in the year. 21, 42, 63, 84: **4 plots** | 84 ÷ 21 is outside the times tables |

### Question 2: how far ahead

| How far | The problem | What the child does |
|---|---|---|
| **7 count days** | 6, 12, 18, 24. By the 7th? **42** | Writes three more rows. Counting on is enough. No better idea is needed |
| **9 count days** (a longer year) | 6, 12, 18, 24. By the 9th? **54** | Writes five more rows. Still counting on |
| **28 count days** (a count every week) | "A boat brings 3 strings each week. Four cuts: 3, 6, 9, 12. How many by week 24?" **72** | Twenty more rows is slow. A better idea pays: 10 weeks is 30, 20 weeks is 60, 4 more weeks is 12. Or: week 12 is 36, and double it |
| | The same boat, by Last Raft (week 28)? **84** | 20 weeks is 60, 8 weeks is 24 |

- **Only the last one asks for your "24th day" thinking.** The first two can be done by adding down the column.
- Under 100, the far count works for steps of 2, 3 and 4 (4 × 24 = 96). A step of 5 gives 120: the harder size.
- **Both can live in one calendar:** boats come home every week, plots are picked every month. Near counts for the first problems, far counts later. That is the story thread's to shape.

### Question 3: my opinion

**Yes. Good things and memories should last. Harm should not. Stores should not, yet.**

| Lasts past New Year | Does not last |
|---|---|
| **The town grows after a good year:** one more house, one more lamp lit. Next year it says "we are more houses", and the numbers are bigger. The child's success *is* the harder problem, and nobody announces it | **Harm.** A thin month ends. A wet floor dries |
| **The keeper remembers,** in one line at New Year: "Last year the bowls were small in White month 3." It ties this year to the last | **A child's number, on any wall a classmate can see** |
| **The plan mark on the store wall,** in the child's colour | **The stores themselves,** for now |

- **Why not the stores:** if spare food carried over, next year's count would start with some already. That is 3x + 2, which you want later. So "this town keeps some back now" is the natural door to that level, when you choose to open it.
- **What could prove me wrong:** children may not notice a town growing. Ask one: "Is this town different from last year?" And a child who struggles has a town that stays small: in a class that could be seen.

## 8. A class in one world: how the constraints could be threaded

[Source: Marc, 23 November] "If all the students in a class are on a certain server so they can see like how others do things on that planet... For these main quests... do others come and talk to... other stores/families? ...How do we thread these constraints?"

Not a design. One way to hold the constraints together, for you to judge. All [Proposal].

### What you said before [Source]

- 22 November: each class has its own copy of the planets, and children see each other's traces.
- 23 November: "A hole is dug. Someone build something incorrectly, then what happens then? So we need to show it."
- 23 November: "The amount of space where they would be building would have to be growing... the world must be big enough to allow multiple students."

### The knot

| | Constraint | Pulls against |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every child does their own maths | 2 |
| 2 | Children see how others do things | 1 and 3 |
| 3 | A mistake is carried out and seen, kindly. A step down is never announced | 2 |
| 4 | The story: one town asks one cadet for one decision a year | 14 children and 5 towns |
| 5 | What follows must come soon after the decision | one shared year for a whole class |

### One thread through it: the town is everyone's, the family is yours

| | The idea | Which constraints it holds |
|---|---|---|
| a | **Every child helps their own family in a town.** A street of family stores, grown by code: a new child, a new house. The story already has a family version of all three decisions | 4. And your "do others talk to other families?": yes, each to their own |
| b | **Each child has their own numbers and their own years.** "Let the year run" is that child's year, in that child's family | 1 and 5 |
| c | **What follows happens in your family's house.** Small bowls at your family's table. A classmate does not see it | 3 |
| d | **Everyone works with a family, so a step down cannot be seen.** It is a smaller household, not a move from "town" to "family" | 3 |
| e | **The town is the sum.** Each right year lights one more lamp on the town's street. The count only goes up, and has no "out of 14" | 2, without ranking |
| f | **A classmate's finished work shows how, never what.** After a year that came out right, the child's own table stays on their family's wall. Another child can walk in and read it: "they counted 10 weeks, then 20". Their numbers are different, so the table cannot be copied as an answer | 2, safely. **Different numbers are what make showing safe** |
| g | Code names only. The teacher sees everything | |

### What this costs, and what I do not know

- **The story's arc changes.** "The keeper learns" becomes "a family learns". The town's keeper needs a new part: perhaps she adds the families up. That is the story thread's.
- **Reading a classmate's table may become copying a method without understanding it.** That is also how children learn from each other. You know which it would be in your class.
- **No server has run yet.** All of this is for later. What is cheap now: keep every change with its owner, place and time, and test with pretend classmates.

### Yours to decide

1. **Each child's own year, or one year the whole class shares?** A shared year means what follows arrives when the class is ready, not when the child is.
2. **May a child read a classmate's finished table?**
3. **What does the town show of the class:** lamps that only go up, or nothing?
