## 2. The story

### 2a. What happened before the child arrives (the truth, found piece by piece)

1. **Tally Station** listens to the stars and counts what it hears. That fits your galaxy: "a cosmic labyrinth of interstellar data streams" [Source: the Galaxy Databa page]. Four people live there [Source: "a crew of four"].
2. The supply ship stopped coming, months ago. Fresh food ran low. Its pilot's last visit left a handwritten note in the visitors' book: something kept pulling the supply ship off course. Mission Control was told only "delayed". Nobody aboard thought much of the note.
3. Then **a strange signal** came [Source: "a mysterious signal… like someone or something is calling us for help"].
4. The big ship, the *Stardust*, was not ready: its tanks were low and its locker was empty. The small shuttle is fast, but it cannot go far. The signal seemed close. So three of the crew took **the shuttle** to follow it, "back in 6 days". The Supply Chief [Source] stayed to look after the station.
5. The shuttle sent its log to the station's Hangar screen once a day. On one day, it burned a lot of fuel but went hardly anywhere. **Something was pulling it.** The pull carried it much farther than a shuttle can fly back. The next day the log stopped.
6. The Chief saw the log stop. She had no time to study it. She climbed up to the Observatory to look for the shuttle through the telescope.
7. Two days ago, while she was up there, a heater on the station got stuck on full. It pulled more and more power, until the main power failed. The sensors went dark, so the station's reports to Mission Control stopped. The backup power keeps dim lights on, and it keeps feeding the stuck heater. That is why the room is still out of its range, and why the power trips each time it is reset. Two doors shut themselves, as station doors do when the power fails, to keep the air in. **The Observatory's door shut the Chief in.**
8. Mission Control knew the shuttle had gone quiet. The station's last reports said only "shuttle silent", not who was in it. It was waiting for the shuttle's return day. Then the station went quiet too.
9. **Now** is the day the child docks. Mission Control has heard nothing from the station for two days.
10. **Why not fly to the crew in your own ship?** It is built for short hops between nearby planets. It cannot carry enough fuel or food for a long trip. The *Stardust* can. So the cadet's ship stays docked, and the cadet flies in the *Stardust* with the Chief.

**Who pulled the shuttle, and why, is not answered on the station.** That is the mystery the rescue opens (section 5).

### 2b. Arrival: three screens, read aloud

1. "This is Tally Station. It listens to the stars. Four people live here."
2. "Six days ago, its shuttle flew off to follow a strange signal. Then the shuttle went quiet. Two days ago, the station went quiet too."
3. Mission Control: "We don't know who flew and who stayed. Nobody answers, Cadet. Go aboard. Find out what happened. Find the crew."

The child now knows where they are, what happened, and what to do (your rule 5). How many flew is left for the child to find (the duty board, C3). The numbers of days are story facts. The builder may change them.

## 4. The jobs

Each job passes the story test [Source: the master plan, section 5b]: **need** (someone needs it worked out, and the child is told first), **meaning** (the answer changes the world because of what it means), **consequence** (a wrong answer plays out and shows why), **picture** (what the child works on fills the picture). The builder makes and checks every number.

### Job 1. The warmth chart (kept, clearer) [Source: your Task 2, Level 1]

- **Need:** Mission Control, by radio: "The station's sensors are dead. We can't see inside. Something is pulling too much power. Read the five thermometers and make a chart on the Control Room wall. Then find the room outside its safe range, and by how much. That is where the stuck heater is."
- **Meaning:** that room's heater (or cooler) is stuck on. It cannot be switched off; its dial can only be turned back, by the right amount. The child goes to that room's dial and turns it back by the number of degrees they found. Then the power can be reset. The answer is used, not typed as a password.
- **Consequence:** wrong room: the child turns a dial in a room that was fine, and the power still trips. Wrong amount: the dial turns, but the thermometer is still outside its sign, and the power still trips. **What a dial does in a fine room is the builder's choice,** made so that turning every dial is not quicker than thinking (section 8).
- **No way on?** Nudges come only when the child taps Hint, as everywhere. Job 1 holds the main road, so it needs one more help that no other job needs: **decision 16.**
- **Picture:** the thermometer, then the chart, then the dial, each filling the picture.
- **Finished example:** the "reports sent each day" chart (A3).
- **Clearer** [Proposal]:
  - The sign says that both of its end numbers are safe, not "between", which reads two ways. The builder picks the numbers.
  - The question comes in two steps: "Which room is outside its safe range?" Then: "How many degrees too hot (or too cold)?"
  - Mission Control names the five rooms, so the child knows which rooms to find.

## 8. What could prove this wrong

- **The look may not be the fault.** You think it put them off; it may also have been the lack of a reason to act, or of cards. The new Stardust changes all three at once, so a class will not show which one mattered.
- **Sixteen screens may tire, not delight.** One room per screen means the child cannot see next door. Walking back for a sign is good once, dull by the fourth time. Watch whether they use the map.
- **More story is more reading.** Every clue is words. Each line here is one or two sentences. "Read to me" must work on every one.
- **The Chief shut in for two days may worry a child.** You have said children know it is not real [Source, 21 November]. She is fine and a little bored.
- **Job 1 now holds the main road.** A child who cannot do job 1 cannot find the Chief. The vent, the open jobs and decision 16 soften that. Check how many get through job 1.
- **The crew's cards may pull children away from the jobs.** That may be fine, or not. Watch.
- **Six jobs may be too long.** You said in 2023 the food was "a bit too long" [Source, 16 November]. My guess is three lessons. [Estimate]
- **"Turn the dial back by the amount"** must not let a child guess by turning every dial. The builder must decide what a dial in a fine room does.
- **The power board, the dial and the look must not give away the hot room.** The visual design thread already checked this for the floors. Check again for the dials.
- **A rescue that never comes is a lost story again.** If the next place is not built soon, choose 5b option 3.
- **Carrying things needs a bag.** Taking a key card or a cocoa tin needs the game to hold what the child carries, and to give it. The station already has a bag; this is new work for the builder in Stardust, with tests.
- **No child has met any of this.** It is a plan on paper.
