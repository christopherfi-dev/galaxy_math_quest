## 1. The real fault, and a rule to stop it

- **[Source]** Your Novus law says most things should do nothing: dead ends stay dead. The Xylophora guide used that to explain the pines, the cacti, the pond, the tent and the rocks.
- **But some of these are not dead ends. They are invitations.**
  - "The sand in the middle is soft."
  - "You can see the bottom: more squares."
  - A dark cave where water drips.
  - A cougar with a clock in its chest.
  - Each one tells a child to dig, dive, go in, or wind. When nothing happens, it feels broken, not real.
- **[Proposal] A rule for every place, from now on:**
  - **A dead end must look like scenery,** and say so plainly: "The pines grow too thick to walk through."
  - **Anything whose words invite** (soft, deep, under, inside, a door, a clock, a hole) **must pay off:** a thing found, a place entered, or some maths that matters.
  - The finds kit can check this: a find whose words invite must have something behind it.

## 3. The plan for the rest

### The square pond on Xylophora: dive in

- **What happens:** "dive", "swim" or "go in" takes the cadet under the water. The picture fills with the pond floor: a rectangle of square stone tiles. Some are missing, and the pond is leaking through the holes.
- **Why it matters:** the rains are coming, and the colony needs this water.
- **The maths:** area, which is what Xylophora is for.
  - The floor is 6 squares by 4.
  - The cadet counts the tiles still there in rows.
  - The tiles missing are the whole floor take away the tiles there.
  - They fetch exactly that many new tiles from the giant's skin.
- **The find:** in the mud under one gap lies the Site Chief's lost survey wheel, or another thing you choose.
- **What it changes:** the pond fills and stays full on the map. The colony has water. A card.

### The dark cave on Chronosia: go in

- **Today:** one question at the cave mouth, then the back wall slides away. The finds kit warned about this: "A cave that asks one sum is not going into a cave."
- **The plan:** three small chambers, each a picture you are in.
  1. The dripping chamber: water drips into a pool at a steady rate. How long until it overflows? (Time passed, 3.MD.1.)
  2. A chamber of pillars in rows. (Equal groups.)
  3. The map wall, as now. It shows the stone door in the north-east hills.
- **What it changes:** the cave stays open and lit on the map, and the way to the stone door is shown.

### Cougar Rock and the warm stones: make the cougar matter

- **Today:** the Clockwork Cougar is a clock-reading quest with no part in the story. The warm stones are something to sit on.
- **The plan:** one story that joins four pieces already there.
  - **The Clockwork Cougar is the guard of the time portal.** The cosmic storm (the station's storm) knocked a gear loose and blew its key away. That is why nobody stopped the secret trips through the portal: your time portal case.
  - **The warm stones are warm because the gear fell under them.** Lift them, and the gear is there, hot from turning.
  - **The tin box's "clockmaker's key"** (built today, at the end of the sundial and boat chain) **is the cougar's winding key.** Today that key goes nowhere; this gives it a home.
  - **With the key and the gear,** the clock-reading quest winds the cougar. It walks to the portal and sits beside it.
  - **After the case:** if you told the Council, it guards the chained portal. If you kept the secret, it watches the crack in the sky.
- **What it changes:** the sundial, the boat, the tin box, the warm stones, the cougar and the case become one story.

## 4. What could prove this wrong

- **Not every rock should open something.** A world where everything pays off feels like a puzzle box, not a place. That is why the rule is about words that invite, not about every object.
- **Inside places cost the most.** The cave's chambers and the pond's floor are new pictures, like the arrival screens.
- **The cougar plan changes your Chronosia story.** The cougar becomes the portal's guard. That is your call.
