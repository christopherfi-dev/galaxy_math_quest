## 1. Stepping back

You said: *"For third graders we need to be fun... a space battle. That's what they should feel like. I made it a card deck because that's all I could do in the past. This has more potential."*

Here is how I read the problem.

- **In 2023 the cards had to do three jobs,** because paper was all there was.
  1. They were the thing a child **earns and collects**.
  2. They were the thing a child **chooses** in a round.
  3. They were the **whole battle**. Everything that happened was a number on a card, and the child was the computer who added and took away.
- **Jobs 1 and 2 are good.** They are why a child wants to finish a task. They stay.
- **Job 3 is what a screen can take over.** A battle is something you watch. A page can add up, and it can show a laser.
- **So I split the jobs.** [Proposal]
  - **The cards choose.** A card is an order you give your ship.
  - **The ships show.** Two ships in space. What a card does, you see happen.
  - **The child decides.** Which order to give. Where the enemy's hit lands.
- **Where the maths went.** You said the battle can be a break from sums. One piece of number work is left, and it is your one: sharing a hit between parts.
  - The hit arrives as **blocks: tens, fives and ones.** A hit of 65 is six tens and a five.
  - The child sends each block to a part. A ten can be **broken** into two fives, a five into five ones.
  - That is place value with their hands. Nobody types a number. [Proposal: tell me if you would rather they typed.]

## 1c. Your rule: a shield card puts the shield back up

You said: *"anytime you play a shield card, it puts your ship's shield back up... which then blocks you from the enemy's attack for that one time."* You asked me to run it first. Then you played, and asked: *"Do I need to change any settings?... their entire attack of 70 or whatever is going to go into my four shield, which will protect my ship again."*

**You no longer need a setting. It is how the battle starts now.** [Your ruling, said three times on 22 November. I took the wide reading, B below, because you named block cards: "block four for each card that I have".]

- **Any block card, and any card that adds to the Main Shield,** played when your shield is broken, puts up a new shield. It is as strong as the number on the card. Card Wall with four other cards in your hand puts up a shield of 16.
- **The enemy's whole attack goes into it.** The shield breaks and your parts are not touched.
- **When your shield is broken, those cards are marked "shield back" in your hand.** The opened card says: "This card puts your Main Shield back up: 16. It will break, and soak up the whole hit."
- **The words on 15 cards changed** to say so. "Block 8 of the attack on you. Or bring back a broken shield with 8 HP." No card says "if it is still up" any more.
- **Your saved settings were cleared once,** so that your iPad starts on the new rule too.
- The two narrower rules are still on the switch, if you want to compare.

Below is what I measured before I made it the start.

**Why it works as you hoped.** Your rule is "nothing carries over". So a shield of any size soaks one whole hit. A card that puts up a shield of 8 saves the ship from a hit of 60, once.

**Two ways to read "shield card".** In my set, the cards with a shield on them are the block cards. The cards that add to the Main Shield have a heart. So I tried both.

|---|---|---|---|
| **Cards in a deck of 45 that can do it** | 1 | 5 | 15 |
| **Random play: a battle lasts** | 9 rounds | 9 rounds | 11 rounds |
| **Random play: longest** | 13 | 13 | 19 |
| **Clever play: a battle lasts** | 9 rounds | 10 rounds | 14 rounds |
| **Clever play: 9 battles in 10 are over by** | round 10 | round 12 | round 17 |
| **Clever play: longest** | 13 | 17 | 23 |
| **A shield comes back, in one battle** | less than once | 2 or 3 times | 5 to 10 times |
| **Nobody wins** | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| **The leader of the last round wins** | 60 in 100 | 56 or 57 | 53 or 54 |

2,000 battles for each box, two computer players, Standard ships.

**What I see in the numbers**

- **No battle got stuck.** None came near the limit of 60 rounds. I had feared two players would shield for ever. They do not: a shield card played is an attack card not played, and a ship that loses the right part can no longer do it.
- **It does what you want.** A ship that is losing gets a way back, several times in a battle.
- **It gives the split a real choice.** This answers my worry in section 4. A block card needs the Shield Generator. A card that adds to the shield needs the Power Module. So "which part do I give up?" now matters. I told the computer to keep its Shield Generator under B, and that alone moved the middle battle from 12 rounds to 14.
- **It softens the edge of leading the last round:** from 60 in 100 to about 54.

**What it costs**

1. **Under B, battles are half as long again.** 14 rounds with clever play, and some over 20. A round is watched for 10 to 15 seconds, plus the choosing.
2. **Once the shield is down, the number on a shield card stops mattering.** A shield of 8 and a shield of 25 both soak one whole hit. So a common card does the work of a rare one.
3. **Some cards' words would be wrong.** "Add 10 HP to your Main Shield, if it is still up." I have not changed any card. If you rule for this, I rewrite them.

**One more thing** [Proposal]: the cards that add to the Main Shield should now carry a shield, not a heart. Then the rule a child sees is simple: "a card with a shield on it puts my shield up."

**What could prove this wrong:** the computer plays a shield card when it has one. A child may save them all for the end and make a battle very long. Only play will show.

**Two things your rule changes that you may not expect**

1. **Shield Reborn is no longer special.** It was the one epic card that could bring a shield back. Now 15 cards can. It still gives the biggest shield, 50, and a shield of 50 can last more than one hit. Say if it should do something new.
2. **A clever player now beats a random one 81 times in 100.** Before your rule it was 69. Knowing when to play a shield card matters. That fits what you called "a logic response game".

**To compare the old ways:** press **Rules**, find "A broken Main Shield is put back up by", choose, then "New battle with these rules".

Your block rule still holds: a common block card does nothing against a rare attack card, shield or no shield.

## 3. How a round goes

1. **One player goes first** and gives an order. Who goes first in round 1 is chance. After that you take turns.
2. **The other sees it, then answers.**
3. **The cards shrink away and the battle plays.** The two orders sit above the ships.
4. **A policeman stops a Spy.**
5. **A Spy that is not stopped acts first.** If it is yours, you tap the enemy part to hit.
6. **Hearts repair.**
7. **Both ships attack.** The page adds up the AP and the card. The attack of the player who went first lands first. A ship that loses its last part does not strike back.
8. **A hit meets the bubble first.** If the hit is bigger than the shield, the shield breaks and soaks up all of it.
9. **With no shield, you choose which parts take the hit.**
   - The blocks are in a row. One is lit.
   - The view moves in on your ship.
   - Tap a part: it takes the lit block. The part's HP goes down as you tap. Over the part you see the sum: "20 − 15".
   - A tap that would destroy a part asks first: "GIVE IT UP? tap again".
   - "Break a block", "Undo" and "Take all back" are there.
   - "Take the hit" lights up when every block is placed. If a part will be lost, the button is red and names it. Then the lasers fire at the parts you chose.
10. **"Next round."**

**The deck:** each player has their own cards, shuffled, and holds 5. A played card goes to the bottom of its deck.
**Saving:** the battle is saved on the iPad at the start of each round.

## 4. What I think you should look hardest at

These are the things that could prove this design wrong.

1. **Is it fun? I cannot know.** I have looked at every screen. A child has not. Ten seconds of a child playing will tell you more than my tests.

2. **The split may not be much of a choice.** This comes from the rules, not from the screen. [Your decision]
   - Your rule: "the defender decides how to split each attack across their parts." [Source: rules sheet 4]
   - A part may be sent more than the HP it has. [Source: rules sheet 4, your example: "the whole 80 can go onto one part with 5 HP left. That part is lost, and the other 75 points are lost with it."]
   - So the best answer to a hit of 65 is always the same: **put all 65 on your least useful part.** One part is lost, and 50 points of the hit vanish.
   - The new screen makes this easy to see: one tap after another on the same part, "it will be lost".
   - A child who finds it will do it every time. Then the one job you kept is no longer a decision.
   - It also makes attack cards weak. A hit of 60 and a hit of 100 cost the defender the same: one part.
   - **Options, each with its cost.** I tried each one roughly: 4,000 battles between two computer players, outside the page.

     | | A battle lasts | Nobody wins | Cost |

     | **A. Leave it** (the page today) | 9 rounds in the middle | 19 to 22 in 100 | After the trick is found, the split is one tap repeated. |

## 6. Things I had to settle to make it run [Proposal: each is yours to change]

1. **A ship that loses its Weapons to the first hit, but not its last part, still strikes back that round.**
2. **A hit may only be put on working parts.**
3. **A block comes off the attack before it reaches the shield.**
4. **Inside a round the order is:** the policeman, then Spies (the leader's first), then repairs, then the leader's attack, then the other's. Both attacks are added up before either lands.
5. **"Lose 1 card" throws away a card at random,** at the moment the part is lost.
6. **An upgrade card played in a battle** makes that part Level 2 until the battle ends.
7. **A Spy played second cannot be stopped.** The first player has already given an order. That follows from your rule.
8. **Both ships attack every round,** whoever went first. Only a ship with no part left does not strike back.
9. **The block rule compares the two cards on the table.** If the other player played no attack card, any block card works against the ship's plain attack.

## 10. The independent review
