## 1. What is wrong at the root

1. **It asks for ratios.** Choosing well means comparing "pay 3, get 8" with "pay 7, get 16". That is a rate: Grade 6 maths (6.RP). Grade 3 compares by **taking away**: 8 − 3 = 5 left over, 16 − 7 = 9 left over.
2. **The numbers are too big.** A season reaches 7,000 gems. Grade 3 adds and subtracts **within 1,000** (AERO 3.NBT.2).
3. **There is no need.** Gems buy nothing. "Make the most" is a score, not a reason.
4. **Rounding has no job.** A child rounds because the screen says so.
5. **Too many choices, and all hidden.** 10, then 20 planets, each "?" until rounded. The thinking is buried under the practice.

## 2. The idea in one breath

**Galactic Trade becomes the galaxy's market.** Each planet wants something and sells something. A child trades because they **need** something: first the glow stone, for the lantern. Each trip is one decision: **which offers are worth the fuel?** Round to estimate before you fly. Count exactly when you land.

## 3. How a trip works

- **The trade board fills the picture:** 4 to 6 offers, each pinned to its planet on the map. Every number is in view. Nothing is "?".
  - "Planet B wants Voltaran fruit. It pays 240 gems. 40 light-years away."
- **Fuel costs 2 gems for each light-year** (3.NBT.3: 2 × 40 = 80). The beacon pulls you home for free, so a planet costs its distance.
- **Your hold carries 3 crates.** One crate for each offer. So a trip is at most three planets.
- **Before you fly, estimate.** "About how many gems will you come home with?" Rounding to the nearest ten or hundred (3.NBT.1) is how a trader thinks fast.
- **When you land, the trader counts exactly.** The child adds what was paid and takes away the fuel (3.NBT.2, 3.OA.8). The estimate and the real count sit side by side. Were you close?
- **A loss shows itself** (your rule of 22 November). "Planet T paid 120. Your fuel cost 140. You came home 20 gems poorer."

### A sample first trip (worked out by hand; the builder checks the rule on 20,000 seeds)

The Council lends **100 gems** for fuel.

| Offer | Pays | Light-years | Fuel |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 180 | 20 | 40 |
| B | 240 | 40 | 80 |
| C | 130 | 10 | 20 |
| D | 290 | 50 | 100 |
| E | 150 | 30 | 60 |

- B and C: fuel 100. Home with 100 − 100 + 370 = **370**. The best.
- A and C: fuel 60. Home with 100 − 60 + 310 = 350.
- D alone: fuel 100. Home with 290.
- A and B: fuel 120. **Too far:** more than 100.

There is no single "biggest pay" answer: D pays most and is not best. That is the struggle, and it is Grade 3 maths: adding tens, taking away, comparing.

## 4. What gems buy: the need

A **price list** is in view from the first trip, so every trip has a goal.

| What | Where | Price | Why a child wants it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A glow stone** | a far planet, 60 light-years | 300 gems, plus 120 in fuel | the lantern, for Chronosia's black cave [Source: Marc's idea, above] |
| **Supplies for Voltara** | Voltara | 500 gems | the Council's call for help [Source: the Task 2 page]. The city's lights show it. |
| **The Power Module** | Voltara's Council, in return for the supplies | the supplies | your ship part [Source: the Task 2 page]. Waits on the plan's decision 1. |

- **The glow stone is a planning problem:** "I have 370. I need 300 for the stone and 120 for the fuel. Is that enough?" No. One more trip.
- **Voltara's tall-tree glow stone stays** as a secret, a second way. A child who found it does not need to buy one.

## 5. The maths, and what is left out on purpose

| What the child does | Standard |
|---|---|
| Rounds to estimate a trip | 3.NBT.1 |
| Fuel: 2 gems for each light-year | 3.NBT.3 |
| Adds pay, takes away fuel, within 1,000 | 3.NBT.2 |
| Plans: price and fuel, against what they have | 3.OA.8 |
| Judges an estimate against the real count | NUM.EST.1 on your own skill map |

- **Left out:** rates and ratios. **The same market can grow with the grades** [your multi-grade idea]. In Grade 5 or 6, the board shows "gems for each light-year", and the child compares rates. That is the Novus version you described.

## 6. The four story questions

1. **Need:** the glow stone, Voltara's supplies.
2. **Meaning:** the answer decides the trip, and what you can buy.
3. **Consequence:** a bad trip comes home poorer, and the stone is further away.
4. **Picture:** the trade board and the map. The desk holds only the boxes.

## 9. What could prove this wrong

- **Children pick by the biggest pay, and never look at fuel.** Watch the second trip: do they change?
- **Estimating, then counting, feels like doing the sum twice.** Watch for boredom. If so, estimate only on far trips.
- **Three crates may be too few to struggle with.** Try four.
- **The glow stone becomes a chore.** If a child must trade three times only to reach the lantern, the chain is too long. The tall-tree stone is the way round.
