## 2. What the AERO document holds for Grade 3 [Source]

| Domain | Standards | What they are |
|---|---|---|
| Operations and Algebraic Thinking (OA) | 3.OA.1 to 3.OA.9 | what × and ÷ mean, unknowns, properties, facts within 100, two-step problems, arithmetic patterns |
| Number and Operations in Base Ten (NBT) | 3.NBT.1 to 3.NBT.3 | round to 10 or 100, add and subtract within 1,000, multiply by multiples of 10 |
| Number and Operations: Fractions (NF) | 3.NF.1 to 3.NF.3 | unit fractions, fractions on a number line, equal fractions and comparing |
| Measurement and Data (MD) | 3.MD.1 to 3.MD.8 | time, litres and grams, graphs, measuring length, area, perimeter |
| Geometry (G) | 3.G.1, 3.G.2 | kinds of shapes, cutting a shape into equal parts |

- Each standard also has a **DOK** mark in red, from 1 to 3. DOK 1 is recall. DOK 3 is reasoning through a problem.
- The last six pages are the eight **Mathematical Practices**, such as "make sense of problems" and "model with mathematics".
- **One thing about the layout:** it is a "progressions" table. A few Grade 3 standards sit on a page with another domain's heading. For example 3.OA.2 is on the Base Ten page. I have used the letters in each code, not the page heading.

## 5. Things that do not fit [Conflict]

1. **The document starts at Grade 3.** The hazards, the mud rats and half of each wisp practise Grade 1 and 2 skills. To give them codes I need the AERO document for the earlier grades. Or they sit in a group called "before Grade 3".
2. **Voltara and Galactic Trade go past 1,000.** The city needs 2,220, the tower holds 1,650, and a trade season reaches 7,000. Grade 3 says "within 1,000". The numbers are yours and they work in the story. But strictly they are Grade 4 sums.
3. **24-hour time is not in the document at all,** in any of the three grades. Two of your clocks and the whole timetable use it.
4. **Plotting points is Grade 5.** The station has a task called Coordinate Plotting.
5. **Reading a clock to 5 minutes is below Grade 3.** To reach 3.MD.1 the Cougar's clock would show times like 3:47.

## 6. What the DOK marks suggest [Proposal]

Your document marks how deep each standard goes. That gives a clean answer to your point that a skill is tied to "a particular challenge or any sort of math the students do".

| Kind of maths in the game | Depth it can show | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A quick question: wisp, hazard, lock | DOK 1. Recall and a procedure. | 3.NBT.1: round 347 to the nearest 10 |
| A step inside a quest | DOK 2. Use it to get somewhere. | 3.MD.1: how long did quake 4 last? |
| A decision in a quest | DOK 3. Reason and choose. | 3.OA.8: the energy plan |

So one standard could show two things side by side: **quick answers** and **used in a quest**. A child can be fast at rounding and still not think to round when a problem needs it. A teacher would want to see both.

The eight Mathematical Practices cannot be checked by a machine. Code can see the plan a child made. It cannot see whether they "made sense of the problem".
