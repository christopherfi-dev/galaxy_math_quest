**My proposal: 118 cards now.** 93 battle cards, 20 ship parts and 5 upgrades. With 9 ideas that wait for a rule from you.
- **Commons you finish, rares that last, epics that are special.** A child fills the commons in today's game, and keeps finding rares as the game grows.
- **Why not 150 now:** with no new rule, more cards would be the same cards with one number changed (+15, +16, +17…). That is filler, not variety.
- **The weak point is epics** (section 6, decision 2). Every epic must do something no other card does, and the rules allow only so many. A child who finishes today's game holds all 9.

## 2. One rule for the numbers

A child can say it: **Common: one small number. Rare: about double, or two things at once. Epic: it does something no other card does.**

| | Common | Rare | Epic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Attack** | 4 to 14; 2 for each working part | 15 to 30; 4 or 5 for each working part; or two things | double it; round it up to the next hundred |
| **Block** | 6 to 15; 3 for each card in your hand | 18 to 30; 4 for each card; or two things | the whole attack |
| **Dodge** | Wormhole Zip | Triple Illusion | Vanishing Act |
| **Main Shield** | 8 to 15; 3 for each card in your hand | 20 to 30; 5 for each card | back to 100; shield and parts to full |
| **Repair** | 5 to 12 on one part; 2 or 3 on every part; share 10; move 5 | 15 or 20 on one part; 4 or 5 on every part; share 20 or 25; round up; move 10 | bring a part back; bring every lost part back; every part to full |
| **Spy** | 3 or 4 in all | 6 to 9 in all | 20 in all |
| **Guard** | stops a Spy | | |

- **No two battle cards do the same thing.** The check tests it.
- **Among the plain-number cards, every rare beats every common** of its kind. A card that counts can make less: Card Wall with 3 other cards in hand blocks 12, less than Subtraction Shield's 15; Fleet Flash with 3 working parts adds 12, less than Thirteen Zaps. That was so on 22 November too (All Hands Fire).
- **Each half of a two-thing card is no bigger than a common card.**
- **An attack card and a block card of the same size cancel.** 13 such pairs, some named to match: Double-Seven Ray and Double-Seven Wall (14), Quarter Cannon and Quarter Wall (25), Triple-Ten Strike and Triple-Ten Wall (30).
- **Changed from 22 November:** the ranges are wider at both ends (common attack was 5 to 10, now 4 to 14). The rule itself is the same.

## 5. A battle with your own cards

**What a child sees and does** (try `docs/battle/own.html`):
1. The battle fills the whole screen, over the game.
2. **The story first:** "Another crew from the KIS Station has asked for a practice battle. This time you fly with your own cards."
3. **Your ship:** five Standard parts. If you own a part card (a Lucky Draw engine, say), you tap that part to use it.
4. **Your deck:** every battle card you own, shown as small cards. Tap one to read it large.
   - **One Spy.** If you own several, one goes in. **You choose which:** tap your Spy to change it. [Your rule, 22 November: one Spy in each deck.] Choosing is a real decision; it is kept for next time.
   - **At least as many Policemen as Spies.** If you have none, the station lends one. [Your rule, 22 November.]
   - **Fewer than 15 battle cards:** the station lends cards from your own starter pack until there are 15. They are marked "lent". **[pick]**
5. **The enemy's deck has the same shape as yours:** the same number of attack, shield, repair, Spy and police cards, and of common, rare and epic. Not the same cards. **[pick]**
6. **Then the practice battle, exactly as you know it,** with your rules. A child cannot change the rules: the Rules button is not shown.
7. "Leave" at any time. **The battle is saved.** Opened again, it goes on from the same round.
8. At the end: "Battle again", "Back to the station", "See the log". The game is told who won.

**Upgrade cards (the plan's decision 1)** are built both ways. The demo page has a switch.
- **A card played in the battle** (as built): the upgrade is in your deck; play it to make that part Level 2 for this battle.
- **Lasting:** an upgrade you own makes that part Level 2 before every battle, and is not in your deck.

**What winning gives:** nothing yet. **[pick]** "Every card must still come from doing a task" (the plan). The game is told the result; you decide if it is worth anything.

## 6. Decisions for you

1. **The size.** 118 cards now (93 battle cards, 20 ship parts, 5 upgrades). It grows when you give a rule for a new kind of card. Keep, or change?
2. **Epics.** Only 9, because each must do something no other card does. A child who finishes today's game holds all 9, so every later epic is a double. Choices:
   - **A.** Give a rule for some of the ideas; each becomes an epic. The ideas: Signal Jammer (the other card does nothing), Power Cut (a part counts as lost for a round), Card Grab, Mirror Moon, Triple Drive (new: triple your AP), and more.
   - **B.** Accept doubles. A double rare or epic is what a child can put up in a challenge battle.
   - **C.** Fewer epics given by the game.
3. **Spies: 11 designs, but one in each deck.** About 1 card in 10 that a child earns is a Spy. By the end of today's game a child owns about 8 Spy cards and uses one.
   - **A.** Keep: they are collected, and the child chooses which one flies (built).
   - **B.** Fewer Spy designs (back to 7).
   - **C.** The game gives Spy cards less often (the cards thread's picking rule).
4. **The starter pack's 11 commons** (4 attack, 4 shield, 3 repair) and a common Spy. Keep?
5. **"Policeman" or "Station Guard"?** [Conflict] Your words say Policeman; the card says Station Guard.
6. **Upgrades** (decision 1): lasting, or one battle? Both are built.
7. **Where in the game the battle opens.** The main thread asks you.
8. **Two new epics to confirm.** Comeback Crew (every lost part back) uses your revival rule for all parts at once. 9. **In a classmate's battle, their ship is called "the enemy"** (the first screen says so). It could carry the classmate's ship name. [pick: keep for now; long names could break the phone's top bar]
10. **Ship parts: two sets of numbers with the same names.** **[Conflict]** (found by the cards thread, 26 November night)

## 7. What could prove this wrong

- **Children may not read 93 different cards.** 31 of the 44 commons are one plain number (add, block, shield or repair) and differ from their neighbours only by that number. Watch: do children say card names, or only numbers? Do they read the pop-up, or tap past it?
- **The practice battle's decks grew** from 47 to 89 cards (each deck holds the whole set, your rule). "Try them all" now takes several battles. Battle length did not move (section 8).
- **The enemy's same-shape deck** may feel too strong or too weak. Only children playing will show it.
- **"Lent" may not be understood** by a child learning English.
- **The frame:** the battle opens inside a frame over the game, so its words and styles never touch the game's. It works in Chromium at all three sizes. It has never run on a real iPad.
- **The piece is big:** it carries the whole battle page, about 204 KB. The game file would grow from about 1.27 MB to 1.47 MB.

## 8. The independent review (26 November, while you slept)

A second reviewer saw only the rules, the card list and pictures of the screens, not my notes.

**Found, and fixed:**
1. **The enemy's dodge cards said the wrong thing.** When the computer led with Wormhole Zip, its order box said "The enemy ship's attack misses it. It still fire." This was in the practice battle since 23 November. Each dodge card now has its own words for the enemy ("Your attack misses it. Your rare or epic card can still hit. It still fires."), and "It still fires" is right for every card.
2. **"Or bring back a broken shield" read as a choice.** The battle gives none: on a broken shield the card always puts it back up. 19 cards now say "Block 8 of the attack on you. A broken shield comes back with 8 HP." Card Wall and Card Fence now say how strong ("with that many HP"). No rule changed: either reading ends the same, because nothing carries over.
3. **The two cheaper dodge cards did not say what still hits.** Wormhole Zip: "A rare or epic card can still hit." Triple Illusion: "An epic card can still hit." Triple Illusion's badge was "×3", which reads as times three; it is now "miss".
4. **The child had to count the enemy's hidden hand.** When the computer leads with Card Wall, Card Fence, Nano Shield or Card Castle, or a card that counts its parts, its order box now says the number: "It blocks 16 of your attack." [You: the child should not have to work out the enemy's sums.]
5. **Rounding Rover's "the next ten" could be read two ways** (does a part at 10 go to 20?). Now: "Round each damaged part up to a ten. A part already on a ten stays."
6. **"lent":** children learning English mix up lend and borrow. Now "you borrow 11 from the station", and the cards say "borrowed". The tag no longer covers the card's kind.
7. **The deck line said "shield"**; the cards say DEFENCE. Now "defence". "Tap a card to read it" is on the screen.
8. **Challenge words:** "In a tie, nobody does" could mean both cards are lost. Now "In a tie, you each keep your own card." Also: whose deck each card came from; that both may be played; "Finish within 20 minutes. If you do not, you each keep your own card" (in place of "or nothing moves"); and "Blue Fox lets classmates battle Blue Fox's deck".
9. **Found while fixing these: words cut off above the ships.** The order box over a ship shows 3 lines. Six cards ran longer, so their last words never showed (Nano Shield, from 22 November, lost "back."; Triple Illusion lost "fire."). Each now fits: for example Nano Shield says "Add 3 shield HP for each other card in your hand. A broken shield comes back." The own-cards test now checks every card, both sides.

**Found, and left for you or for later** (no change made):
- Badges that read two ways: "2×" (2 for each part) beside "×2" (double the AP); "4×" on both Card Wall and Fleet Flash; "100s" and "10s". A different badge would need room in the circle. Say if you want them changed.
- One card, three names: Station Guard (name), POLICE (label), policeman (words). Waits on your choice of name.
- The battle calls a card "an order" ("Choose your order to answer it"). A child learning English may read "order" as a sequence. It is the battle's word since 22 November.
- On a phone the top bar says "YOU 55" and "ENEMY 55" without the word ATTACK, for room.
- The small cards in the deck are hard to read on an iPad (tap one to read it large; the screen now says so).
- "Standard Shield" (a part) sits beside "MAIN SHIELD" (the bubble). Your 2023 name.
- An old card at stake (one of the 6 from before) shows only its name and kind: it has no face. The cards thread decides what the old cards become.
