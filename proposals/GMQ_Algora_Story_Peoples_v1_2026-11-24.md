## 4. Four things to find

Each one is off the path, needs no maths, and tells part of a people's past. Each starts a thread that leads somewhere. **Nothing a person says changes until the child shows them the thing, or asks** (your rule 9).

### D. The north-west copse (Menders): a fallen painted board

**Where:** in the trees of the copse, near the old wall. On the way, a child passes the rubble where the Gate's shard lay [Built].

**Look:**
> "A painted board, fallen face down in the roots. You turn it over."
> "A great machine stands by a lake. Tiny copper folk climb ladders all over it."
> "One of them has a lid with a dent in it."

**Show it to the elder:**
> "My grandmother painted that. Our family kept the Year Machine. Every gold month we went to the lake to oil it."
> "I was there the night it burnt. The Gate flashed on the mountain. Every plate went white, then black."
> "Menders mend everything. We could not mend that. There was nothing left to mend."

**What it tells:** the Year Machine was real, and the Menders kept it. The elder was there. **Where it leads:** the Year Machine's black frame at the Lake Market, later.

**The dented lid** is the elder as a young Mender. A child who notices it has found something, and the game does not say so.

### E. The far bank where the old stones land (Twinseers): the telling stone

**Where:** in the grass on the far bank, off the path north of the lookout.

**Look:**
> "A flat stone in the grass, half grown over. Two rings are cut in it, side by side."
> "One ring is rubbed gold. The other is rubbed white."
> "A groove runs from the stone toward the lake."

**Ask the old Mender, or the Rillfolk guard:**
> Old Mender: "A telling stone! My gran said the Twinseers came every New Year, two by two. They stood there and told both banks the suns' word."
> Rillfolk guard: "My gran heard them too. Then the Year Machine took their word and made the plans, and they stopped coming."

**What it tells:** the Twinseers once walked the shores; the Menders and Rillfolk once heard them together. **Where it leads:** the groove points to the lake, and the Twinseers.

### G. The south beach (Eights): a page from a ship's log

**Where:** on the sand below the orchard, half under a stone.

**Look:**
> "A page of thin, shining paper, torn along one edge. The sea has not spoiled it."
> "It is written in tidy rows: ‘Still lost in the Pattern Universe. Eight of us, as always.’"
> "‘The youngest writes tonight. Ahead, a light on a mountain. The captain says—’ The page is torn there."

**Show it to the orchard keeper:**
> "Pages came down like leaves, on the day the Gate broke. Most went into the sea."
> "Whoever wrote that is still out there, in the woods behind the old wall."

**What it tells:** the Eights are a lost crew far from home, who write every day. It stops before it says why the Gate broke, because that is learned at the Gate [Built: "each lit stone shows one picture of that day"]. **Where it leads:** the hedge in the old wall, and the Pattern Woods. **A child may keep it,** and one day give it back (section 6).

### H. The far bank south of the bridge (Furrows): a ring of white stones

**Where:** in the grass just south of the bridge's landing, by the reeds.

**Look:**
> "A ring of white stones, sunk deep in the grass. Moss grows on them."
> "In the middle lies an old cart axle, as long as two of you."

**Ask the Rillfolk guard:**
> "Furrow carts came down that way in my gran's time. They set their loads down here, before the bridge. My gran said you could hear them from the harbour."
> "A Furrow lays a white stone for every load, where the load is set down. That ring is one carter's working life."

**What it tells:** in the guard's gran's time, Furrow carts came all the way down to the river, and each load was counted with a white stone. **Where it leads:** the hill lift, and the Terrace Hills.

**Why no maths:** the stones can be counted, and nothing asks for it. If a child counts them, nothing happens. This is history, not a lock.

## 5. Pushing like a child

| "Why didn't the Menders mend the Year Machine?" | It burnt to its frame. "There was nothing left to mend." Menders mend. The Makers made, and they are long gone |

## 6. Homes for later quests [Proposal, for the big world thread]

**Not designs, and no numbers.** Each is a reason someone in the story needs a thing worked out.

- **The Menders:** a painted board is how Menders show how a thing was found. So a finished example on the Meadow can always be a painted board, as Elder Rivet's already is.
- **The Furrows:** a gran's quilt, sewn in pieces and joined, can be the finished example the design outline asks for ("a quilt, not a field").
- **The Twinseers:** "share by the white". In the white months what was grown is shared out, house by house. A home for division, when it comes.
- **The Eights:** the log page from G. Given back, the youngest Eight finds that the torn pages leave gaps, which is the outline's "mend the log". A thread from the beach to the Woods.

## 7. What could prove this wrong

| Claim | What to check |
|---|---|
| A found thing reads as history | Do children read a find and then look for a lock to open? If so, the finds look like puzzles. Ask after: "What did you find out?" |
| A thread leads somewhere | Does any child show the board, the page or the stones to a person, without being told? If none do, nobody will hear the elder's story. The big world thread may need a nudge, such as "Someone might know what this is" |
| The arc can be put together | After two regions, ask: "What did the Menders give to the machines? What did the Rillfolk?" If nobody can say, the arc is only in this note |
| The elder's grief is not too heavy | "We could not mend that." Marc: "Elementary students know this isn't real." Watch one child's face anyway |
| One planet's moral is not the other's | Chronosia: a people grew careless. Algora: peoples forgot skills. If children read them as one story, one should change |
| The Eights are not blamed | If a child says "the Eights broke it" with anger, the "kind guests" line is not carrying |
