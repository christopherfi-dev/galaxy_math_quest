**Where I was wrong.** In my review of your 33 tasks I marked several things "above Grade 3". I used a general list for the grade. I did not have this evidence. So:

- I will treat every "above Grade 3" flag in `reviews/` as **"check with Marc"**, not "remove".

## 4. Why these work: the bones

This is what I would keep. The content can change every time.

| # | The bone | In Atlantis | In the moths |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A job, not a worksheet.** Students get a title. | Zoo Architects, then Zookeeper Detectives | scientists with a data set |
| 2 | **A question the maths answers.** | "How do we even know what to build?" | "What caused this?" |
| 3 | **Hands first.** | cut and sort real cards | make the table and the graph by hand |
| 4 | **Teach the tool when it is needed.** | "Why do we make graphs?" comes just before the graphs | — |
| 5 | **Each stage feeds the next.** | the sort becomes the graph, the graph becomes the habitat sizes | the graph becomes the question for the reading |
| 6 | **Spiral back.** Old skills return inside new ones. | sorting, then times 5 and 10, then adding, then graphs again | graphs again, in science |
| 7 | **Room to disagree.** | small, medium or large? | "predict what might have caused it" |
| 8 | **A twist.** Success turns into a mystery. | "but is it?" | the answer is in a history text |
| 9 | **Wild ideas first, then steer.** | "Are they secretly shrinking?" | — |
| 10 | **Every reader gets there.** | — | one text at three levels, the same question |
| 11 | **End by explaining,** in words. | why are the herbivores falling? | 2 to 3 sentences |

**The one I had missed completely is number 8.** My own sketches end when the sum is right. Yours end when a student says "wait, could it be...". That is what makes a project a quest.

## 6. One of my own, to show I can do it without copying [Proposal]

This is an outline, not a finished quest. It is for your **Magnificent Measurements** quest in Galaxy Metrica, which has nothing built. Your Planet Lengthar draft may be its home. I have not used that draft here.

**The Seed Keepers**

| Stage | What students do | Maths | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | A probe returns with 20 sealed seed pods from a dying garden world. You are the Seed Keepers. | — | on screen |
| Sort | Pod cards show soil, water and light. Sort them three ways. Graph each. Decide which beds to build. | **spirals back:** sorting and bar graphs | table, then on screen |
| Measure | Each pod needs a strip of bed a set number of centimetres long. Measure and cut real paper strips. Lay out each bed on the floor. | **new:** length in cm and m | real life, your code |
| Water | Each pod drinks a set number of millilitres a day. Work out a bed's water for a day. Fill a real jug to check. | **spirals back:** multiply. **new:** ml and litres | real life, your code |
| Watch | Four weeks of plant heights for two beds. Plot two lines. | **spirals back:** line graphs | on screen |
| Twist | The bed with **more** water is growing **slower**. Why? | reasoning | class talk |
| Read | A short text at three levels about the planet. Its nights are long and cold, and wet roots freeze. | reading | paper |
| Explain | Write 2 to 3 sentences. What should the Seed Keepers change? | — | you check it, gold card |

**How it differs from Atlantis:** different story, different new skill, real measuring tools, and a twist about too much of a good thing. **What it shares:** the eleven bones.

**How it sits in the game:**
- The planet opens when the ship has the parts whose skills it needs. Here that is the Shield part, for multiplying.
- On-screen stages are marked by code. Real-life stages use your code, as the station does now.
- The explanation is yours to judge. No code can mark "why".
- The last stage gives the **gold card**. [Source: your rule for big quests]

## 7. Three things a game must do differently from a slide deck [Proposal]

1. **Projects should share one set of data.** On the station each student has their own numbers, so no one can copy. In a project the class must argue over the same graph. So project data should be the same for the class, or for a group.
2. **The clue must really be in the data.** Before a mystery goes to students, code should check that the numbers support the answer you want, and no other. I did this by hand for your carnivores and for the moths.
3. **The game should not give the twist away.** The planet should look like a success until the data is plotted.
