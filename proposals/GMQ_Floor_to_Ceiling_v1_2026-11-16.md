## 4. The art step: an honest limit

- Everything in the sketch is drawn by code. Code drawings are clean and they move, but they are simple shapes.
- An "amazing alien planet" needs painted art. I cannot paint pictures in this session.

**Options [Proposal]:**

| Option | How | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) AI-made pictures, prepared ahead** | You make them with an image tool (you have Canva). I write the brief for each picture and build the scene around it. | The biggest jump in how it looks | Keeping one style across 30 pictures takes care. You approve each one. |
| **(b) Code drawings, made richer** | More layers, light, weather and creatures, all in code | Nothing to approve. Small files. Everything can move and change. | It will never look painted. |
| **(c) Both** | A painted backdrop, with code-drawn things in front that move and change | Best of both | Most work |

- The parts a student touches should stay code-drawn in every option. A painted city cannot switch its lights on. A drawn one can.
- I can also create images through your Canva account if you want. I have not, because that writes to your account. Say the word.

## 5. The ceiling

### 5.1 Battles in space: possible, and they fit
- Your battle rules are turn-based: one card each, then both ships fire. [Source: handoff §7.2]
- So a space battle does not need fast reflexes or a live connection. Two ships face each other. Each round is one card. The code runs your rules and draws the hits.
- It can be played live in class, or one move at a time across a day.
- **What it needs first:** your answers to the open battle questions in the handoff, and one official card set.

### 5.2 Flying in 3D: possible, with a risk
- The Abyssal Garden file shows it can be done in one web page.
- The risk is the iPads. **Test:** open Abyssal Garden on a student iPad. If it is smooth, 3D travel is worth planning. If not, the 2D galaxy map in the sketch does the same job.

### 5.3 A huge live online world: not needed
- A world where thousands play at once needs a team and a budget.
- What gives the same feeling for one class is small: you see classmates' ships on the map, you find what they left behind, and the class opens things together.

## 6. What makes it feel like a galaxy [Proposal]

1. **You travel, and you see the travel.** No jumping straight to a page.
2. **Every planet has its own look, creature and rule.** Voltara has talking animals. Chronosia could run on clocks. Xylophora could be built of tiles.
3. **The problem lives in the world.** City lights for energy. A clock tower for time. A map on the ground for Zephyron.
4. **What you do stays.** Holes, names, opened doors.
5. **Other people are there.** Ships on the map. Signs of what they did.
6. **There is always something you have not found.**

## 7. How AI makes the worlds ready [Proposal]

| When | What the AI does | Who checks |
|---|---|---|
| **Before class** | Drafts each scene: the place, the things, the secrets, the mini quests, the picture briefs | You approve. Code tests every number. |
| **During play** | Answers anything a student types that the scene did not plan for | The scene's facts limit it. Code never lets it mark maths. |
| **Overnight (later)** | Adds small new things: a new secret, a visitor, a change in the weather | Rules limit it. You can review. |

This is your constraint architecture. The world is built and fixed. The AI lives inside it.
