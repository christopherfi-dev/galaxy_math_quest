# One screen at a time, or a map that scrolls?

**Date:** 24 November 2026. **For:** Marc. **Nothing was changed.**

**Marc's words** [Source: Marc, 24 November]: "Prodigy math does… the similar thing as legend of Zelda, where each screen is a whole screen and then when you go off the screen, it goes to the next rectangle of the map. That works pretty seamlessly for prodigy math And I'm not sure if that's a better idea than what we're doing where I'm trying to copy like the old school dragon warrior… I'm not sure how big the prodigy math world is… Maybe you could research that? … their quests are slow down because you have to fight monsters I guess. My quest should be more about thinking and doing problems… Is it possible we need to change for the big world and what I'm trying to hope to make the planets into?"

**Read as:** Prodigy shows one whole screen at a time, as the first Zelda did. Our big world scrolls with the cadet, as Dragon Warrior did. Which suits GMQ, for the big world and for the planets?

---

## 0. The short answer

- **Prodigy is built one screen at a time.** In an old copy of its code, a zone is 11 to 17 screens on a grid, with 5 to 7 quests. [Source: Prodigy's code, see section 1]
- **Our built big world** (the Meadow and the Reach) **is about 21 to 23 such screens.** The whole planet is about 64 to 72. That is about the size of Prodigy's whole island in that copy.
- **My view** [Proposal]: one screen at a time fits GMQ's rules and your goals better than scrolling. But try it before deciding. Trying costs little; changing costs more.
- **Secrets and exploring still work one screen at a time.** The first Zelda is full of them.

---

## 1. What I found about Prodigy

**Source:** an old copy of Prodigy's own game code, public on GitHub, marked version "2-0-0". I cannot tell its year, and the game has changed since. Each screen in it has a grid name, like "forest-A3" or "lamplight-B2" (a row letter and a column number). I only searched it for these names.

| Zone | Screens | Quests |
|---|---|---|
| Firefly Forest | 11 | 5: defeat the trio, find Gerald, wake Gerald, fix a flute, talk to a merchant |
| Lamplight Town | 14 | (the town) |
| Shiverchill Mountains | 17 | 7 |
| Skywatch | 17 | 7 |
| Bonfire Spire, Shipwreck Shore | not named this way in this copy | 10 and 6 |
| Small places (Dark Tower, a toy zone, others) | 1 to 5 each | |

- **Each screen is one fixed picture.** The code sends a player to "forest-A3" at a point in the middle of it.
- **Between zones,** players walk, or jump with the map. [Source: the Prodigy fan wiki, as quoted by a web search: "walking from Lamplight Town or using the map to teleport there"]
- **Quests are steps on named screens, many of them battles.** Common Sense Media's review: "a lot of time can pass between skills questions", and "sometimes it can seem as though the math part of the game is an afterthought."
- **Your own words are a source too:** walking off one screen brings the next, and it feels seamless.

**What I could not read:** the fan wiki's own pages (they refused automatic reading). I did not play Prodigy.

## 2. What GMQ has now

- **The big world:** a scrolling map. The cadet stays in the middle; about 16 squares show across an iPad. (Dragon Warrior's way.)
- **The station:** already one room at a time, with exits at the sides ("← Shield Bay", "Navigation Bay →"). **You said the station looks OK.** I think this is part of why: every room is designed as one picture.
- **Chronosia, Xylophora, the mission ship:** each is one board on one screen, with side views of its places.

---

## 3. Why one screen at a time may fit GMQ better

1. **"The picture stays still"** (rule 8). It changes only when the child walks off an edge, on purpose.
2. **"I couldn't see very far. I didn't know where to go."** [Marc, 23 November] On a screen, the child sees the whole place: everything in it, and every way out.
3. **Every screen can be designed, like a station room.** The scrolling map is squares laid out; a screen is a composed picture. And a screen is one picture, so a painted background (from Gemini) can fit it. A scrolling map of squares cannot use one.
4. **Children can name where they are:** "the Orchard", "the Pond". The map becomes a small grid of named places. That is easier to talk about in class.
5. **A number to design to:** your 20 to 50 things on a planet is about one or two things on every screen. Today, 5 to 9 of the big world's built screens would have nothing in them.
6. **Secrets still work:** a screen can hide a cave, a loose stone, a way through.
7. **Thinking, not walking.** Prodigy fills its back-and-forth with battles. GMQ should not need filler. One screen at a time lets travel be counted: keep each quest's places within a few screens, and give every screen something to notice or decide.

## 4. What it costs, and what could go wrong

Measured on the big world today (big world 12), with screens of 16 × 11 squares (the first Zelda's size; Marc's own count of 176 squares a screen, 23 November):

| | 16 × 11 | 16 × 12 | 12 × 9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Screens in the whole map | 72 | 64 | 118 |
| Screens in the built part (Meadow and Reach) | 23 | 21 | 29 |
| Things (of 36) on a screen's edge | 7 | 9 | 10 |
| Things on one screen, while the square to use them from is on the next | 4 | 6 | 3 |
| Built screens with nothing in them | 9 | 5 | 10 |

- **The map must be re-cut,** so that nothing sits across an edge, paths cross edges at clear gaps, and every way out is marked.
- **A phone:** a whole 16 × 11 screen leaves squares of about 24 pixels, too small to tap. A phone would need smaller screens or keep scrolling. (Your class uses iPads.)
- **Less of the feeling of one big land.** Nothing can be glimpsed past the edge.
- **More screen changes when a quest sends you far.** It may need a quick way back (Prodigy uses its map to jump).
- **The big world's tests** (walking, signs, wandering, empty places) must change.
- **Owners:** the big world page is the big world thread's; the planets are the main thread's.

---

## 5. Options [Proposal]

- **A. Keep scrolling.** Improve it with signposts and the new look.
- **B. One screen at a time in the big world.** Re-cut the map into screens of 16 × 11 squares; name each screen; put something on each.
- **C. Try B first, cheaply** (recommended). A switch in my sample of the big world: "Screens: scroll / one at a time", on today's map as it is. You walk it on the iPad and feel it. It also shows exactly where today's map breaks at the edges. About one session, in my own files only.

Then decide between A and B.

---

## 6. For the planets

- A planet could be the size of one Prodigy zone: **10 to 20 screens, each a designed place, with one or two things on each.**
- Chronosia and Xylophora are one board each today. They could grow screen by screen.
- The station already works this way.

---

## 7. What could prove this wrong

- **Children may lose their way when the screen changes.** Watch: can a child walk back to the ship without the map?
- **Children may like seeing the land scroll by.** Ask them.
- **Today's Prodigy may differ** from this old copy of its code.
