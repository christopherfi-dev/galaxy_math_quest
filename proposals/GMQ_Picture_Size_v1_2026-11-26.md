# How big the picture is: three ways

**Date:** 26 November 2026. **For:** Marc. **From:** the main thread.
**Why:** Marc relayed another thread's answer about the side panel. Its pick was C: the picture fills the screen, and an Actions panel opens over it. This note checks its numbers, and compares C with a smaller change.
Everything is **[Proposal]** unless it says [Source]. Nothing here is built. The game is version 99.

---

## 1. What was measured

- An iPad held sideways (1180 × 745, the size CLAUDE.md uses for an iPad held sideways in Safari).
- Every station room, drawn at three picture sizes. The number is how many times bigger each room is drawn than in version 99. Script: `checks/room_size_measure.js`.
- Mock-up pictures of each way, in `proposals/pictures_picture_size_2026-11-26/`. They are mock-ups, not the game.

| Room | Version 99 (tile size) | B: words beside the picture | C: words over the picture | C: words in a strip below |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arrival Dock | 45 | 1.08 × | 1.24 × | 1.06 × |
| Shield Bay, Navigation Bay | 60 | 1.11 × | 1.24 × | 1.06 × |
| Launch Bay, Engine Room, Weapons Bay, Power Core | 67 | the same | 1.32 × | 1.32 × |
| Store, tunnel | 64 | the same | 1.38 × | 1.37 × |

## 2. What the measuring found

1. **The other thread's numbers are right** for what it measured: a picture with nothing under it. I got 1.24 to 1.38; it got 1.27 to 1.38.
2. **But the game's words must go somewhere.** After "look ship", the answer has to show.
   - Over the bottom of the picture: the bottom row of the room is hidden while the child reads. In the mock-up, the loose crate in the Arrival Dock is under the words.
   - In a strip below the picture: the Arrival Dock and the two bays grow only 6%.
3. **The station's rooms are nearly square. An iPad held sideways is wide.** So the room is limited by the screen's **height**, not its width. Full width mostly adds dark space at the sides (about 180 pixels each side in the Arrival Dock mock-up).
4. **The words strip under the picture uses the height**, which is the scarce part. Moving the words beside the picture gives the height back to the room. That is B.
5. **The open panel in C covers the right third:** in the Arrival Dock, the ship's nose, the Navigation Bay door and the locker. The other thread suggests sliding the room left. Either way it breaks rule 8 in the handoff [Source]: "The picture stays still. Nothing covers it."
6. **The rooms C grows most are the ones already drawn biggest** (tile size 64 to 67). The rooms children see first (the Arrival Dock, tile size 45) grow least.

## 3. The three ways

| | What it is | The Arrival Dock | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Keep version 99 | as now | none |
| **B (my pick)** | The game's words move to the top of the side panel, under the typing box. The picture takes the full height. | 1.08 × (the two bays 1.11 ×) | Medium. Every task's desk must still fit under the words. The words sit where Algora's reading card sits on a wide screen. |
| **C** | The picture fills the screen. The words lie over its bottom. Actions opens a panel over the right side. | 1.24 × with the words over the room; 1.06 × if the room sits above them | The biggest. Two layouts (exploring, and a task open). Every screen rechecked. Breaks rule 8. |

**Why B:**
- Nothing covers the picture, and the picture never moves.
- It fills the side panel. Version 99 left most of the panel empty.
- The letter keys stay 30 pixels wide; the panel keeps its width.
- Children read the answer next to the box they typed in.

## 4. What could prove B wrong (watch in class)

- **Children look for the answer under the picture**, where it has always been. Watch after their first "look": do they find it?
- **Long answers push the Actions button down.** Long readings already go to the reading card; check that the rest fit.
- **On an iPad held upright and on a phone**, the panel is below the picture. There B changes nothing, and the words stay under the picture.

## 5. Yours to decide

- A, B or C.
- Whether to try your pick on a class iPad first, on a link of its own, before the class game changes.
