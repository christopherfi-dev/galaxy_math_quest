## 1. The idea

**A cosmic storm swept through this part of space.**
- It wrecked KIS Station and broke its ships.
- It scattered rocks and parts across space.
- It knocked out Voltara's power, and it stranded the Number Nomads.

**Now Voltara is calling for help, but the message keeps breaking up.** Yours is the only ship on the station that can be fixed.
- Every task gets your ship ready for the flight to Voltara.
- Every part you fit brings back more of the message.
- When the ship is whole, you hear all of it, and you fly.

**Where each piece comes from:**
- [Source] The station is a mess and the ships are broken (your home page). Voltara's energy crisis, its Energy Council and the Number Nomads (your Voltara pages).
- [Source, not checked again] The Nomads were "stranded by a cosmic storm". The Voltara guide records this as yours. The original is in your Drive folder, which is not in this copy, so I could not reread it.
- [Proposal] That one storm caused all of it. Also the broken message, and the ship's log.

**Why this, from your goals:**
- **One cause holds the world together.** A child can explain the whole station in four words: "The storm did it."
- **Every task gets its reason from the same storm,** so no reason feels pasted on.
- **The station leads straight into Voltara.** The message says the city is dark. On Voltara, the child lands and the city across the river is dark. That is how Voltara already starts.
- **The message hints at Voltara's mystery before the child lands.** One piece says "we hear a hum under the streets". On Voltara, the hum under Factory Row leads to the Nomads' hidden town.
- **It is a rescue.** The children are the ones who help. Your opening already says "Earth is counting on you."

## 2. Three things that run through the whole station

### The broken message
- After each part is fitted, more of the message comes through.
- There are five pieces, one for each part. They come in the order the child fits the parts, whatever that order is.
- Each piece is one line, read aloud.

Draft lines:
1. "…KIS Station… this is Voltara… can anyone hear us…"
2. "…the storm hit our power tower… it gets weaker every night…"
3. "…the city goes dark at night now… we cannot cross the river…"
4. "…some of our people are missing… we hear a hum under the streets…"
5. "…please come. We will wait for you at the Landing Meadow."

The tone is hopeful. Nobody is hurt. The city is dark, and it is waiting for help.

### The ship's log
- Each task writes one line in the child's own log, with their own numbers and choices. For example:
  - "Route: by the fuel moon. 14 units left at Voltara."
  - "Cargo: power cells, water, food… 30 tons."
- At launch, the log is their flight plan.
- It could be printed as a page, if you want one.
- Later places can read it (section 5).

### Each task opens with the storm and ends with the flight
- **The reason comes before the maths.** It is three short sentences at most, read aloud, and shown in the picture where it can be.
- **The ending says what the work did for the flight.** For example, "Every rack makes pairs. Your cannons will fire straight."

## 3. The fifteen tasks

Each task is marked:
- **Keep:** it already works.
- **Words:** the same task with new words.
- **Deeper:** the task itself changes, because a better one fits the story.

The skill for each part stays yours. [Source]

### Engine Core: counting and graphs

| Task | | What the child is told, and does |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Alien Artifacts | **Words** | "The storm shook the Engine Core loose. Its cage locked itself. It will not open until it knows how many artifacts spilled out, of each kind." The counting and the four dials stay as they are. Ending: "The cage opens. Your ship has an engine again." |
| 2. Alien Species | **Deeper** | Before the message broke, Voltara sent pictures of the animals near the landing place. The child counts each kind and builds a bar graph. Then the graph answers two questions the crew needs: "Which animal will we meet most?" and "How many more ___ than ___?" The animals are Voltara's own, so the child meets them later. [Source: Voltara's talking animals, the butterfly and the Gumblefish] |
| 3. Star Map | **Keep** | Its reason is already the flight. (Still open: it earns the Engine Core, but it is about the shield.) |

### Navigation System: adding and subtracting

| Task | | What the child is told, and does |
|---|---|---|
| 4. Fuel Calculation | **Deeper** | The picture is the route to Voltara, and there are two ways to go. One goes straight through the storm's rocks: short, but risky. The other goes around, by a fuel moon: longer, with a stop to refuel. The child works out the fuel left after each leg, for both ways, and then chooses. A way that runs dry is not allowed. This is real multi-step adding and taking away, with a decision at the end. |
| 5. Coordinate Plotting | **Words** | "The storm blew the Navigation key part off the station. Mission Control tracked it, day by day." Everything else stays as in version 65. |
| 6. Cargo Load | **Deeper** | One piece of the message is a list: what Voltara needs most. The ship can lift 30 tons, and one ton more means it cannot take off. The child packs 7 crates, and Voltara's needs must be among them. They add the weights themselves: they write the total, and then the scale checks it. Many loads are right, and the log keeps the one the child chose. **This also fixes two faults:** today the scale adds for the child, and any 7 crates that make 30 tons pass. |

### Shield Generator: multiplying and dividing

| Task | | What the child is told, and does |
|---|---|---|
| 7. Asteroid Field | **Words** | "The storm left rocks drifting between here and Voltara." **Your battle and its rules do not change.** |
| 8. Energy Grid | **Words** | The rock moves to the start: "Storm rocks will hit your shield. Its energy grid must hold enough power for every section." The steps stay as they are. |
| 9. Shield Calibration | **Deeper** | The picture first shows a station shuttle that flew in the storm. Every dent is on one side. Its shield log reads 9, 9, 8, 2, 2. The child sees why: the rocks found the weak side. Then they share their own tokens equally, so their shield has no weak side. **The child finds the reason instead of being told it.** |

### Weapon System: odd and even

**One new idea for all three tasks: twin cannons, one on each side, that fire in pairs.** It makes "even" mean something. The game's maths word already says "An even number makes pairs with none left over."

| Task | | What the child is told, and does |
|---|---|---|
| 10. Weapons Coding | **Deeper** | "The storm scrambled the cannons' lock. Its screen is a grid of numbers. Light every odd number, and the unlock sign appears." The grid has 30 to 40 numbers, some with three digits. The child decides odd or even many times, and the hidden sign is the reward. |
| 11. Ammo Count | **Words** | "The twin cannons fire in pairs. An odd number in a rack leaves one shot with no partner. A shot fired alone pushes the ship sideways." |
| 12. Target Practice | **Words** | "Each hit charges the twin cannons. They need a charge from 32 to 40, split equally between them. Past 40, they overheat." Ending: "A charge of ___: ___ for each cannon." |

### Power Module: place value

| Task | | What the child is told, and does |
|---|---|---|
| 13. Circuit Connection | **Words** | "The storm burned out the old wires. Each new wire carries hundreds, tens or ones. The board needs exactly ___. Too much burns it out again. Too little, and the ship stays dark." |
| 14. Power Allocation | **Keep** | As in version 64. One line can say why the power is short: the storm. |
| 15. Emergency Power | **Deeper** | "A storm rock hits the station. Life support is failing." The child reads the cells' blocks, as now. Then they must send life support an exact amount, and to make it they have to break a hundred into tens. That is regrouping. **It is also what Voltara's power tower asks for** (8 hundreds, 70 tens and 150 ones). [Source] So the station teaches regrouping before Voltara needs it. |

**In all:** 2 Keep, 7 Words, 6 Deeper.

## 8. What could prove this wrong

- **Your students already loved the station with little story.** They wanted to keep playing through break. So the message is one line per part, read aloud. Ask them after a task: "Why did you do that?"
- **A call for help may worry some children.** Keep it hopeful. Nobody is hurt.
- **Children who finished the station on the class link will not hear the message.** Voltara must still make sense without it. It does today.
- **The storm is my link between your pieces.** Your Voltara material may give the crisis another cause. If it does, the storm clashes with it, so check this first.
- **The hidden sign in Weapons Coding can be guessed** once part of it shows. Before it is built, check whether the sign should appear only after "Check".
- **More to build is more to break.** Every new state needs a test.
