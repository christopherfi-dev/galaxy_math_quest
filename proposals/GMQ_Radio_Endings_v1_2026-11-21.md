# The mission ship's radio: three endings to choose from

**Date:** 21 November 2026. **For:** Marc. **Nothing in this note is built.** You asked to see two or three radio endings before one is built. Everything is **[Proposal]** unless it says [Source].

---

## What is there now

- **[Source]** Operation Stardust's ship "leaves at dawn on a year-long trip, to follow a strange signal".
- **What the game does now:**
  - On the radio, the signal beeps in bursts, such as 4, 8, 12, 16. Then it waits.
  - The child sends the next number. The game says: "Somebody out there can count!" and gives a card.
  - Then nothing happens. At lift-off, the game does not check whether the signal was answered. It ends: "A new friend who needs help? A treasure? In one year they will know."
- **The fault:** the story stops where it should begin. You called it a lost story.

**Every ending below fixes that.**
- **Signal answered:** the ending happens at lift-off.
- **Signal not answered:** the radio is still beeping at lift-off: "Maybe on the way, someone will answer it." Nothing is lost, and the child can come back.

---

## Ending 1: a ship caught in the storm

**What the child sees at lift-off:** a reply comes in words this time. "This is the cargo ship *Little Comet*. The storm caught us. Our engines are dead. Thank you for counting back. We thought no one could hear." Mission Control changes the course: Operation Stardust has a new first stop.

**The maths:** one more step of the pattern. The signal's next two numbers are the *Little Comet*'s position (along, then up). The child writes them, and the course is set.

**What it joins:** the cosmic storm from the station. The storm wrecked the station, darkened Voltara, and caught this ship. A child sees that one storm touched everything.

**What it costs:** about one session. It adds one new name, the *Little Comet*.

---

## Ending 2: someone learning to count

**What the child sees at lift-off:** the signal sends a new pattern, harder than the first. It skips by 4, then by 5, then by 6. Somebody out there is learning numbers, and wants to practise with you. When the child answers, the beeps make a tune. "A new friend joins the voyage, by radio."

**The maths:** a growing pattern (the jump gets bigger each time), and the next number in it. That is a step past the first signal.

**What it joins:** your words "A new friend who needs help?" [Source]. The friend can come back in later places, always by radio, always with a pattern.

**What it costs:** about one session for the ending. A friend who comes back costs a little in each later place.

---

## Ending 3: a map in the beeps

**What the child sees at lift-off:** the beeps turn into dots on the Control Room screen. They make a small picture: a planet nobody has charted, with water. Mission Control goes quiet, then says: "Cadet, that could be a home." Operation Stardust's year-long trip now has a reason.

**The maths:** each burst gives one dot, (along, up), by the signal's own rule. The child plots the last two dots. Then the picture appears.

**What it joins:** Project Second Home, "find the perfect new home for Earth" [Source: your opening]. This is the far goal you agreed to (decision 15). It gives the mission ship its own place in that story.

**What it costs:** about one to two sessions, because the picture appears dot by dot.

---

## My recommendation

**Ending 3, a map in the beeps.**
- It ties the mission ship to the far goal, finding a home for Earth, without ending the game: a planet that *could* be a home is a reason to keep exploring.
- Its maths is plotting points, which the children now practise in Coordinate Plotting.
- Ending 1 is the most cohesive with the storm, but a second rescue right after Voltara's may feel the same. It could be used later, in another place.

**What could prove this wrong:** children who never touch the radio will not see any ending. Today the radio is an optional side job. If you want every child to see the ending, the signal could become one of the ship's main jobs.
