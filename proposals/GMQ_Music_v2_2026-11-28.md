## 2. What changed

**Songs, not loops.**
- Each song has a **verse** (8 bars) and a **chorus** (8 bars) with its own chords and a higher melody. About 50 to 60 seconds before it repeats (the first samples: 26 to 30; today's game: 8 to 16).
- The music changes colour at the chorus. In A, the strings give way to a soft choir. In B, a second line joins under the melody, and a soft kick joins the shaker.
- At the turn into the chorus and back: in A, a soft timpani roll; in B, a soft rise.

**Richer instruments** (ideas learnt from how the summoning demo makes sound; none of its notes or code):
- **Strings:** two slightly out-of-tune tones for each note, with a gentle wobble (vibrato). This is what makes a chord sound "full".
- **Choir:** the same, through filters that make an "ah" sound (style A's chorus).
- **Flute** (style A's melody), and a **soft horn** (style B's melody: its sound opens at the start of each note, like a breath into a horn).
- **Bells and a music box:** one tone wobbles another (FM), which gives a real bell's shimmer.
- **A low part:** soft in A; plucked in B.
- **Soft drums, style B only:** a shaker on the off-beats; in the chorus, a soft kick on beats 1 and 3. No loud drums anywhere.
- **The place:** the station's air and computer blips; Voltara's pulse that opens bar by bar like a charge building, its sparks, and a rising "charge" tone.

**Still the same:** never the same way twice in a row (the melody may rest, change a few notes to other notes of the chord, or play one high note; every third time round it rests, and in B the drums rest too); quieter while a task box is open; off until "Music" is tapped; no sound files; nothing copied.

**Fixed on the way:** a click. Each new note's volume began at full for one sample. The check now listens for clicks.

| Voltara, old | -25.8 dB | -33.3 dB | 4 |
| Voltara A | -24.5 dB | -37.9 dB | 21 |
| Voltara B | -24.0 dB | -38.1 dB | 27 |
| The game's beep (a right answer) | -24.6 dB | | |

- **Quieter:** on average about 5 dB quieter than today. The loudest moments are about as loud as one beep (station A: 2 dB louder, for a moment).
- **Tones:** never more than 28 at once (the limit). Tested on 2,000 random seeds per song, 20 times round each. Chords, low notes, melody and drums are never dropped; only extra blips or bells, when it is busy.
- **Cost: this is the price of "richer".** Best of 3 runs, the time Chromium took to make one minute of music:

  | | Old | New A | New B | New B, light (no echo) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Station | 0.4 s | 3.5 s | 5.3 s | 2.6 s |
  | Voltara | 0.5 s | 3.8 s | 6.4 s | 2.5 s |

  - About 10 times the old music's work, or 5 times in light mode. Here, 5 seconds per minute is about 9% of one processor.
  - About half of it is the echo (reverb). On iPads, Safari does the echo's long part on a second processor, but **this is not measured on an iPad.**
  - **The light mode** (no echo) is one switch: `GMQMusic.setup({ light: true })`.
  - Already made cheaper: the shaker and the sparks reuse one noise (no new sound for each tap); the horn and the low part share one filter; the flute and the low parts are one tone with chosen overtones instead of two.
- A cheaper echo (short repeats that feed back) was tried and dropped: it came out different each time it played, a sign it may misbehave in Safari.

## 4. Wiring it in (for the main thread, after Marc picks)

In `prototypes/kis_station_sketch.html`, the block `MUSIC.   [Source: Marc, 2026-11-22` …:

- **Remove:** `const TUNES = {…}`, `MUS`'s scheduling parts, `musParse`, `musNote`, `musTick`, `musStart`, `musStop`, `musWake`.
- **Keep:** `musPick()` (it already returns the place ids), the Music button, `getKeep('gmq-music')`, and the `visibilitychange` line (it suspends `AC`; the piece works with that).
- **Paste:** everything between `/* GMQ MUSIC: start` and `/* GMQ MUSIC: end */` from `docs/music/index.html`.
- **Then:**
  ```js
  GMQMusic.setup({ ctx: function () { AC = AC || new (window.AudioContext || window.webkitAudioContext)(); return AC; },
    pick: musPick,
    busy: function () { var t = false; try { t = speechSynthesis.speaking; } catch (e) {} return !!((typeof widget !== 'undefined' && widget) || READ.open || t); } });
  GMQMusic.setStyle('A');      // or 'B': Marc's pick
  if (getKeep('gmq-music') === 'on') GMQMusic.setOn(true);
  ['pointerdown', 'touchend', 'click', 'keydown'].forEach(function (ev) { document.addEventListener(ev, GMQMusic.wake, true); });
  ```
  The Music button calls `GMQMusic.setOn(on)` where it now sets `MUS.on` and calls `musStart`/`musStop`.
- **Until every place has a new tune,** a place without one is silent. So wire it in only when all ten are done (after Marc's pick), or keep the old player for the rest.
- `checks/music_test.js` reads `TUNES`, `musParse` and `MUS`: it must change with the wiring. `checks/music_v2_test.js` checks the piece itself.

## 5. Marc's decisions, shortest first

1. **A or B?** (Or A for some places, B for others.) My pick: **A** for the station, Chronosia's cave and the mission ship; **B** for Voltara, the small worlds and Xylophora. Calm where children think; more tune where they explore. But your ear decides.
2. **Loud enough?** About 5 dB quieter than today on average. One number changes it. My pick: try it in class, and turn it up if children cannot hear it.
3. **Try it on one class iPad** (the preview link's music room). If it stutters or the iPad gets warm, I switch on light mode. My pick: test on the oldest iPad first.
4. **"Moments"** (short dramatic pieces for a task solved, a card, a launch, a landing): yes or no. Asked on 28 November.
5. **Still open from the first music note (decision 1):** off or on at first. Kept **off**, as the brief says. (Algora's music is on at first since 24 November.)
