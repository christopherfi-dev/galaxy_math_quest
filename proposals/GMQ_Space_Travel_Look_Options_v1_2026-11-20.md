# Space travel that looks like Exolune: can we, and what would it cost

**Date:** 2026-11-20
**For:** Marc
**You asked:** "Can you access this?" and "I wonder if we could do this eventually. Not now."
**Marks:** [Source] is from the Exolune repository. [Proposal] is mine.

Nothing here is built. You said not now. This is so the idea is not lost.

---

## 1. The short answer

- **Yes, I can read it.** It is a public repository.
- **We cannot do that, on an iPad.** Not now and not later. It needs a gaming PC.
- **We can do a small cousin of it.** A planet that grows as you fly to it, with clouds that move, and a way down to the ground with no cut. That is possible in a browser on an iPad.
- **It would not change the maths.** It would change how arriving feels.

---

## 2. What Exolune is [Source]

- **One planet at real size,** 6,378 km across, like the Earth. You fly from orbit down to the pebbles with no loading screen.
- **Built for a gaming PC.** Windows 10 or 11, a DirectX 12 graphics card, made on an RTX 4070 Ti. It does not run in a browser, on a Mac or on an iPad.
- **Big.** About 35,000 lines of C++ and graphics code, 182 graphics programs, 448 MB to download. The plants alone take another 750 MB to build.
- **A world with no game yet.** Its own page says so: "What is not there yet is the game around it: a solar system to cross, reasons to land, things to do on the ground."
- **Not free to reuse.** Its licence (PolyForm Shield) lets people read and build it. It does not let anyone make a rival product from it.

**About how it was made.** The repository says the author built a first version in Unity in 2012. Its work journal names branches called "claude/..." and "codex/...", so AI coding tools were used. I did not find the name of a model in what I read. I cannot confirm "Opus 5.5" from the files.

I did not run it. I read its front page, its licence, its file list and two of its pictures.

---

## 3. Why we cannot copy it, twice over

1. **It cannot run on your students' iPads.** Wrong graphics system, and it needs far more power than an iPad has.
2. **Your rule.** Every place, name and picture in GMQ is original or yours. And its licence would not allow it anyway.

So the only question is whether we make our own, much smaller.

## 4. GMQ and Exolune are opposites

| | Exolune | GMQ |
|---|---|---|
| The world | one huge real planet | many small drawn places |
| What you do there | nothing yet | quests |
| Where you can land | anywhere | at the places that have a quest |
| The machine | a gaming PC | a child's iPad, in a browser |

GMQ has the part Exolune is missing. Exolune has the part GMQ draws simply.

## 5. What a small cousin could be [Proposal]

| | Choice | What a child would see | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Leave space as it is** | the flat star map, as now | nothing |
| B | **Living planets, drawn the way the game already draws** | each planet is a lit ball with its own colours and slow clouds. When you land, the picture moves in on the planet until the landing place fills the screen. | an evening or two. Works on every iPad. No new kind of code. |
| C | **A real 3D approach** | you fly at the planet. It grows. You pass through a cloud layer and come out over your landing place. One shot, no cut. | several nights for the first planet. A second kind of drawing code in the game. It must be tried on your students' real iPads before anything is built on it. |
| D | **Land anywhere, at real size** | what Exolune does | not possible on an iPad |

**My pick, when the time comes:** B first. It is cheap, and it tells us whether the moment of arriving matters to your students. C only if it does.

## 6. The reason it could be worth doing

You asked for a world that feels real: an immersive experience "that is not happening because of the math". [Source: Marc, 2026-11-17]

Today a planet is a dot on a map, then a place. Nothing joins the two. A way down from space would join them. That is the one thing here that serves your goal.

---

## 7. What could prove this wrong

- **A fly-in is a wait.** Your daughter was annoyed by a wait when a pop-up held the game. A ten-second landing, every time, could be worse. It must be short, and a tap must skip it.
- **It may be seen once and never noticed again.** Children may love the first landing and ignore the tenth.
- **Old iPads.** I do not know which iPads your class has. Choice C could run badly on the older ones.
- **It is not maths.** Every night spent on it is a night not spent on the four standards with nothing, or on the card battle.

**A cheap test before any of it:** show one child a ten-second film of a landing, then the game without it. Ask which one they want.
