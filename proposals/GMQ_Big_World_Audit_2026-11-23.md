**For:** Marc. **From:** the big world thread. **Date:** 23 November 2026, night.
You asked: "Do a clean audit of yourself over how well you think everything is going." [read from "Do you need to clean audit yourself"]

## 1. In one paragraph

The building and the checking are sound. What is built works, and it is checked hard: every number rule on 20,000 made-up children, a map check that must catch 15 broken copies, and a play test at three screen sizes. **The judgment about the child's experience has been weak.** Nine independent reviews and every play test missed what you found in a few minutes of play: a child could not see where to go. I checked that things were correct. I did not check that they felt right to an eight-year-old. The maths for the next quest is getting closer, but slowly, and it may be getting too complicated for your class.

## 2. What is going well

- **Planet Algora works,** and it is live: the Mender Meadow and the Reach, typing, music, the well quest, two ship parts to Level 2.
- **Faults get caught before you see them.** The reviews found real faults every time (the Reach's main problem was wrong three times on paper). Each was fixed or the design was stopped.
- **The numbers are recomputed in code,** never trusted from a note.
- **The two-thread way of working is starting to pay off.** The story thread caught things I missed, such as the seller who "counted ahead" when nobody on Algora can.
- **Tonight:** the walking fix is measured, not guessed (the walking note, section 4).

## 3. What is going badly

1. **I measured what was easy to measure.** Every check asks "is it right?" None asked "can a child find their way?" The play test knows where everything is, so it can never be lost. You found the problem; my tests could not.
2. **I kept choices I was handed without weighing them.** The black mist came from the first brief ("as on Chronosia"). Nobody asked what it cost. Other inherited choices may be the same: the loose cog that interrupts a walk, the size of the squares, the typing.
3. **My own designs keep failing review.** The Terrace Hills failed twice. The sunk raft failed. The deciding years have had three joint reviews and are still not built. Each fix adds more: tally sticks, count days, pots, hooks, dark windows, shells, Market day, white months, songs. **Nobody has asked whether an eight-year-old can hold all of that at once.** The reviewers check that it is consistent, not that it is simple.
4. **You carry too much.** The report has 18 choices for you, the story note has more, and the maths note has more. You also carry messages between threads at night. One of them was misread ("six blockers" were six points, not blockers).
5. **My notes are long.** You asked for short sentences and plain English. The sentences are short, but there are too many of them.
6. **Nobody has played it but you.** No child, and no real iPad except yours. Every time and every "too hard" is a guess.

## 4. Tonight's own risks

- **I made small choices without you:** the big tree, three signposts, the listener moved, "go" that follows a path. You asked me to make walking clearer, so I did. But you also said "stop inventing while building". Each is small, marked Proposal, and easy to take out.
- **The lost-child check is my own model, and it could flatter my own changes.** One thing suggests it does not simply agree with me: it showed that the old sign by the ship made things worse, which I did not expect. Still, only children can confirm it.

## 5. What I would change [Proposal]

1. **Watch two or three children before building anything more** (step 2). Fifteen minutes in class is enough. The testing sheet is ready (`proposals/GMQ_Big_World_Testing_Sheet_2026-11-22.md`).
2. **Keep the lost-child check** with the other checks, so "can a child find it?" is asked every time.
3. **Try the deciding years on paper first.** Read the first problem to one child, with a paper stick and paper windows. Do it before anyone builds it. If the child is lost in the story, no review will show it.
4. **Give you a short list.** Three decisions that block work, and everything else on "the threads' pick unless you say no." Section 6 below is a first try.
5. **Write each list of inherited choices with its purpose and its cost,** so the next black mist is caught on paper.
