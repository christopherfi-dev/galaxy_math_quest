## 2. How to do it inside the one-file game

**Yes, the game can do this.** Your tools are HTML pages. The game is one too. It already holds three tools of this kind:
- Voltara's plan table: 21 places, an energy budget, four endings.
- Galactic Trade: a star map, fuel and gems.
- Target practice: played inside the picture.

**The way to fit a big tool in, without breaking your rules:**

- **The picture becomes the board.** The map, grid or chart is drawn where the picture is, and the child taps on it. The picture does not move or scroll. It changes what it shows, as Xylophora's arrival screens already do. [Source: your rule, "the picture stays still"]
- **The desk holds the dashboard:** the counts, the budget, the buttons.
- **Each tool is data plus three small parts:**
  - how to draw the board
  - what a tap does
  - what the dashboard shows
- **The same pattern as the finds kit:** each cadet gets their own numbers, a check reads the data before a child sees it, and a test plays it.

**What one tool costs:** about one to two working sessions each, including its test. A second tool of the same kind costs much less.

**One trap to design against** (independent insight): **a live dashboard can do the maths for the child.** In the Forest tool, the screen adds the costs. A child can tap until everything turns green without adding anything. So the game version should make the child work out a number first, then let the dashboard confirm it. For example: "Your path costs how much? Write it, then press Build." The dashboard then shows if they were right. The struggle stays theirs.

## 5. What could prove this wrong

- **A small screen.** Your Forest map is 10 by 10 squares on a laptop. An iPad held sideways has room for about the same. An iPad mini or a phone may not. Test the smallest screen first. [Source: your rule, "test the worst case on purpose"]
- **A dashboard that thinks for the child** (section 2). Watch for children tapping until everything turns green.
- **Too many kinds at once.** Fifteen kinds of play means fifteen things to learn. A few kinds, used often, may teach better than many used once.
- **Reading load.** Data cards and rules are reading. Your Grade 3 readers vary.
- **No single right answer is hard to mark.** The log can record what each child chose and why. You would still read some of it.
