# The station's story: does each task have a reason?

**Date:** 21 November 2026. **For:** Marc. **Nothing in this note is built.** The game is unchanged.

[Source: Marc, 2026-11-21] "So go through the space station tasks. Do the students know why they're doing each one? How they could relate to their journey in space. Should that be the story goal for this space station? Cohesive?"

**How I checked:** I opened all 15 tasks in the game (version 65) and copied the words a child sees before the maths starts. I compared them with your 2023 station pages.

---

## 1. The short answer

- **Only 3 of the 15 tasks tell the child why before the maths starts.** These are Star Map, Coordinate Plotting and Power Allocation. All three were rebuilt in the last two days, after your rule "the maths serves a task told before it starts".
- **6 tasks give part of a reason.** There is a lock, a red gauge or "rocks are coming", but the child is not told why this maths, or what it does for the trip.
- **6 tasks give no reason.** The child gets a rule and nothing more: "Every rack must hold an even number."
- **Almost no task says what the work did for the trip when it ends.** Most endings say what happened to the object: "Click. Every rack is even."
- **The station has a goal: "Five parts are missing. Earn each one."** That is a goal about collecting, not a reason for each task. Nothing in it points at a journey.

**My view: yes, the journey should be the station's story goal.** Section 5 says why, and lays out three options with their costs.

**Missing from both:** where the ship is going first, and why each task matters for getting there.

## 4. Faults found on the way

These break your rules, whatever story you choose. I have not fixed them; I am waiting for your go-ahead.

1. **Cargo Load adds for the child.** The desk shows "0 of 30 tons", and the number goes up with every crate tapped. A child can tap until it says 30 without adding anything. [Your checklist: "a running total, a dashboard that does the adding".]
2. **Cargo Load: the choice means nothing.** Your 2023 page said "fill cargo wisely". In the game, any 7 crates that make 30 tons pass. What is packed never matters.

4. **Fuel Calculation names its stops A, B, D and F.** C and E are missing. A child may wonder where they went.

7. **Star Map** [Conflict, already open]: its reason is the shield, but it earns the Engine Core.

## 5. Should the journey be the station's story goal?

**Yes, in my view.** [Proposal] Here is why, from your goals:

- **Your rule:** the maths serves a task told before it starts, and the task serves the story. A goal of "earn five parts" is a reward, not a story. It cannot give 15 different reasons. "Get ready for the first trip" can.
- **It is already half there.** The console sets a course for Galaxy Numerix. Planet Voltara is Numerix's first planet on your map. The opening says "fix the ships, explore five galaxies". Fuel, Coordinate Plotting and Star Map already point outward.
- **It makes the numbers real.** "30 tons" becomes what the ship can lift. "Even" becomes "the cannons fire in pairs". "Share equally" becomes "a rock hits the weakest part of the shield". The reason creates the rule, so the rule makes sense.
- **It joins the station to everything after it.** What a child does on the station can show up on the trip: the cargo they packed, the aliens they graphed, the shield they balanced. That is your "quests have effects".

**One frame that settles the conflict in section 2:** [Proposal] the station holds the parts and gives each one when the crew shows it is ready for the trip. Each task is one of two kinds:
- **A repair, so the ship can fly:** open the core, wire the board, balance the shield, fill the racks.
- **A plan, so the trip is safe:** fuel, route, cargo, the aliens ahead, the rocks ahead, the stars ahead.

"Prove your worth" [Source, 2023] stays, but now it means "show you are ready for this trip".

### Three options

| | What it is | What it costs | What it gives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. The first trip** (my recommendation) | One line in the ship report names the trip: "Your first trip is to Planet Voltara, in Galaxy Numerix." Each task opens with one or two sentences of reason, and ends by saying what it did for the trip. A **flight plan** in the Launch Bay fills in as tasks finish: fuel to spare, the cargo list, the shield's strength. | About one to two working sessions: about 45 lines of words, some pictures, test updates, and checking every screen on three sizes. No number rules change. | Every task has a reason. The station feels like one story. |
| **B. Prove your worth** (your 2023 frame) | Keep the frame "the station tests you". Add one sentence of reason to each task that has none. | About half a session. | Cheaper, but the station stays a set of tests. The reasons have no shared destination. |
| **C. The first trip, and it remembers** | A, plus the results carry forward. The cargo you packed is in the hold on Voltara. The aliens you graphed are the ones you meet. Your shield's balance is part of the trip. | Two to four sessions. It changes Voltara, and it may touch your battles. | Your "quests have effects". The most immersive, and the most work. It is also the most likely to break a quest that already works. |

**A can become C later.** If the flight plan from A is stored, Voltara can read it when you are ready.

The first trip is the station's share of that same idea, and option C here is its option C. One answer from you can settle both notes.

## 7. What could prove this wrong

- **Your students already loved the station without much story.** They wanted to keep playing through break, and the station has the least story of any place. [Source: `GMQ_Story_and_Size_Options_v1_2026-11-20.md`, section 7] So a story must not slow the play down. That is why each reason is three sentences or fewer, and why it should also show in the picture. Ask the children what they would want more of before building it.
- **Children may not read the reason.** This is the real test. After a task, ask three children: "Why did you do that?"
  - If they say "to get the part" or "to get a card", the story is not landing, whatever the words say.
  - The fixes would then be reading the reason aloud (the game can speak) or showing it in the picture.
- **Reading load.** Every sentence added is more reading for your weaker readers. You have said the game is mostly visual, with short commands. Keep each reason to three short sentences or fewer. Where the reason can be a picture (the route to Voltara, the twin cannons, the weak spot on a shield), show it.
- **A forced reason feels fake.** Children notice. Where a reason is weak, "it is a drill" is more honest than an invented cause.
- **Order.** Children do the station tasks in different orders. Each reason must stand on its own and not assume a task before it.
- **Two versions in one class.** Children who finished the station on the class link (version 54) will not see the new words.
