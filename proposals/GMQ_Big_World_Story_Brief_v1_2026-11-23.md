# What a story for Algora must carry: a brief for a story thread

**Date:** 23 November 2026. **For:** Marc, and any thread that writes Algora's story. **From:** the big world thread.
Everything is **[Proposal]** unless it says [Source]. One page. No quest is designed here.

---

## 1. The maths the story must hold [Source: Marc, 23 November]

> "For these they use the multiples. 3, 6, 9, 12. We could do like food storage. How much do they need by month 11. Or however long you make their year. Farming. Collecting. How many farms do we need. It's all multiplication. But it's skip counting. And making predictions based on math."

- **Now:** things that grow by the same amount each time, **from nothing.** 3, 6, 9, 12.
- **Later, not now:** things that start with some already (5, 8, 11, 14). Marc: "I was thinking we'd get to that. But later." The story should leave room for it: a store that is not empty.
- **Two questions:** "How much by month 11?" and "How many farms do we need?"

## 2. The rule for mistakes the story must allow [Source: Marc, 22 and 23 November]

A wrong decision is carried out. Nobody says "wrong". The child sees what follows. Then a new problem of the same size. The maths skills engine may step the child down to a smaller one. A child who is right again and again gets harder ones, and is never told.

## 3. So the story must give these seven things

| | The story gives | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A calendar.** How long is Algora's year? Do the months have names? What happens in month 11? | "By month 11" must mean something a child cares about |
| 2 | **Six or more things that grow by the same amount each month, day or trip,** each with its unit: sacks from a farm, fish from a boat, stones from a quarry, logs from a wood | The code makes the numbers. The story makes the things |
| 3 | **Decisions, not answers.** What do the people *do* with the child's number: store, plant, build, send? | Marc: "they must analyze it and make a decision" |
| 4 | **What follows when the number is too small, and when it is too big.** Both must be seen in the picture, both must be kind, and both must be put right later | Without this, a mistake is only "wrong" with a picture |
| 5 | **The same kind of need in four or five places:** three towns, several farms, and one small version (a child's garden, a hut) | A new problem of the same size, a smaller one, and several in a row |
| 6 | **A reason for a table.** Who counts, and why are the counts kept? | The child reads a table and writes their own |
| 7 | **A reason to come back.** A season later, the store, the farm or the wall is as the child left it | Marc: "Your decisions must matter" |

## 4. What is built now, which the story may keep or replace

That choice is Marc's. Replacing costs a rebuild of the words and pictures.

- Planet Algora, in Galaxy Patternox. Its Gate is broken. Five peoples each keep one rule of its code.
- **The Menders:** small copper folk who keep old machines. A meadow, a village, an elder.
- **The Rillfolk:** river people with rafts. A harbour and three towns: Stilt, Reed and Mud. "The high water is coming."
- **The Furrows:** farmers on hill terraces. Named, not built.
- Two suns. A ship's five parts go to Level 2, one for each people's big quest.

## 5. What the story must not do

- No name, creature or place from a book, film or game. [CLAUDE.md]
- No sum and no answer inside anyone's speech.
- No place that is used once and never again.
- No long sentences. Grade 3, many children learning English.
- No harm that stays. A flooded store is bailed out. A hungry month ends.

## 6. What should come back

One table, not a tale: the calendar; the peoples and places; the growing things with their units; three decisions, each with "too little" and "too much"; the small version of each. **No numbers.** The code makes those, and checks them.

Then the big world thread fits the maths to it, and an independent reviewer reads both together before anything is built.
