# Music

**Date:** 22 November 2026. **For:** Marc. **Built:** version 88, on the preview link only. The class link stays on version 81.
Everything is **[Proposal]** unless it says [Source].

[Source: Marc, 22 November] "Do music? Is it easy to do? Can you make your own or can you find it somewhere? Each world or each place get their own music ..for now." A child asked for it.

## 1. Made, not found

- **The game makes its own music.** I wrote the tunes. The game plays them with its own sound maker, as it already does for beeps.
- **Why not find music:**
  - Music found online comes with licences, and many free tracks have rules about credit and use in schools.
  - It needs sound files. The game is one file that also runs with no internet.
  - Your rule is that everything is original or yours.
- **What it sounds like:** simple tones, like an old game console or a music box. Not recorded instruments.

## 2. What is built

**One tune for each world** (10 tunes in all):

| Where | Tune |
|---|---|
| The start screens | calm and hopeful |
| The KIS Station | a soft working hum, with a ripple of notes |
| Space | slow and wide |
| Voltara | bright and bouncy |
| The 20 small worlds | playful, with a light beat |
| Chronosia | a music box, with a clock's tick |
| Chronosia's dark cave and the Clockmaker's workshop | dark and slow, with drips |
| Xylophora | wooden, like a marimba |
| The mission ship (Stardust) | quiet and mysterious |
| Lengthar | a steady march |

**How it behaves:**
- **A Music switch beside Sound,** under More. Music and Sound are separate.
- **Off until someone taps it.** Then that iPad remembers it.
- **An iPad only lets sound start after a tap.** So after the game is opened again, the music starts at the first tap.
- **Quieter than the game's beeps.** When a task box is open, words wait to be read, or "Read to me" speaks, it gets softer still. The maths and the words come first.
- **Each loop is 8 to 16 seconds.** Every third time round, the melody rests and only the bass plays, so it tires less.

## 4. How it was checked

- **`checks/music_test.js`, on three screens:** passes.
  - **Every tune, as data:**
    - each voice has the same length
    - every note is in the tune's key and in range
    - no two tunes share a melody
    - none is unused
  - **Every world plays its own tune.**
  - **Off at first, with no sound at all.**
  - **The switch** turns it on and off, and is remembered after closing.
  - **The tune follows the cadet** from world to world.
  - **It gets softer** while a task box is open.
  - **Sound and Music** turn on and off separately.
- **Loudness, measured from a silent recording of each tune:**
  - The loudest moment of every tune is far below distortion.
  - In the game it plays at 60% of that, under the beeps.
- **The Sound switch** still works on its own: checked, because its line was edited.
- **The other tests:** music touches only the sound code. `no_secret_needed_test.js` ran again and passes. The rest of version 87's tests were not run again.

## 5. Your decisions

1. **Off until tapped**, or on at first? With 14 iPads in one room, I chose off.
2. **Headphones in class?** If not, you may want it off at school and on at home.
3. **Tunes for single places,** not only worlds: for example, the dark passage on the station, or the Backward Mountain. Later, if the children like it.
4. **A teacher switch** to turn music off for the whole class. It needs the server, so it comes later.
