## 2. Options [Proposal] (written before the correction above; test them in the lab first)

### A. The station's card, in the big world too (recommended)
- In the picture, at the bottom, wide, over a darkened band, as on the station. The same look, the same "Read…" bar, the same wait.
- **For:** you have seen it work in class. It is like Prodigy's box. One card in the whole game, so children learn one habit. Small work.
- **Against:** while the words wait, it covers the bottom of the picture. In the big world's side scenes, that is where people stand, so it can hide the speaker.
- **What eases that:** the card names the speaker in big letters (it already says "A MENDER"). Later, the speaker's face in the card, as Prodigy does; Elder Thimble's Gemini test would give us his faces.

### B. Keep it beside the picture, and make it loud
- The picture darkens more while the words wait. A bouncing arrow points from the speaker to the card. The card slides in with a sound. A bigger arrow button.
- **For:** covers nothing.
- **Against:** the eyes still have to leave the picture. On the class board the side panel is far away. It may still be missed.

### C. A speech bubble next to the speaker, in the picture
- **For:** the most eye-catching; it points at who is talking.
- **Against:** the most work. A bubble must never hide the thing the words talk about. Story pages with no speaker need another place.

## 3. How long OK waits

- **Your number, 1.5 seconds, is what the station already does** for a short card (0.5 s + 0.16 s a word). Long cards wait longer, up to 3.5 s. [Proposal: use the station's rule in the big world too.]
- **What a timer cannot do:** make a child read. An average Grade 3 reader reads about 100 words a minute (Hasbrouck and Tindal's reading norms), so a card of 12 words takes about 7 seconds to read. The wait only stops the "tap, tap, tap" habit.
- **What makes them read:** a card where they are already looking; short words; words that matter to what they do next; and "Read to me" for children who need it.

---

## 4. What could prove this wrong

- Watch three or four children after the change. Do their eyes go to the card before they tap? Ask one child what the card said.
- If the card at the bottom hides what the words talk about (a scene where the thing is low down), the big world thread should list those scenes.
