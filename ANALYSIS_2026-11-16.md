## 1. The short version

1. **GMQ is real and it worked.** Five quests and a station ran with a class. The cards were the engine. The story is yours and it is good.
2. **The quests are rougher than the design docs assume.** Of 33 tasks and levels, 4 can go into a world as they are. Most of the rest are fixable. Five need rebuilding.
3. **The printed sums are mostly right.** The faults are elsewhere: tasks that are really several lessons, rules that were only in a video, versions that disagree, and maths above Grade 3.
4. **The MUD plan has a sound skeleton but is designing the wrong half first.** It is detailed on checkpoints, cards and battles. It is almost empty on the part you asked for: a world where kids explore and do random things.

## 2. What the review shows

### 2.1 A "task" is usually several lessons
- Station tasks take 10–30 minutes. They are the right size.
- Galaxy tasks take 45–110 minutes each. Voltara Task 1 is three lessons. Operation Stardust is listed as two tasks and is really six lessons.
- This matters for "one card per finished task". A child could wait two weeks for one card. [Proposal] Decide whether a card is earned per task or per level.

### 2.2 The too-long task is the Eating Schedule
- You remembered a ship supplies task. It is almost certainly **Stardust Task 1 Level 2**. [Source for the task; Proposal for the match]
- A child fills four tables. Each must reach 360 energy points while obeying shelf life, balance and "no waste" at once.
- That is 36 to 76 calculations, some 2-digit × 1-digit, and about 84 written cells.
- It has no single right answer, so a child cannot tell when they are finished.
- One rule ("use up all the food") cannot be met, because the food list has no amounts.

### 2.3 Open tasks with no finish line
- Eating Schedule, Plan the Perfect Day, the Colony design and 3-1 all share this.
- They were fine with a teacher in the room. In a typed world, code needs a rule for "done". These are the redesigns that need the most thought.

### 2.4 The same answer for everyone
- 1-1, 1-2 and 4-1 give every child the same answer. So do the Google Forms, and their keys are readable in the page source.
- Per-student numbers fix this. Rules for all of them are tested in `checks/`.

### 2.5 Above Grade 3 in more places than the docs list
- 24-hour time (Chronosia 1 and 3).
- Facts past 10 × 10 (3-2, 3-3).
- Triangle areas and 12.5 m² (Xylophora).
- A map scale of 1 cm = 42.3 km (Zephyron).
- Percent cards (Voltara Task 2).
- A graph with two vertical scales (Stardust).
- Practice links that point to Grade 4 and Grade 5 IXL.

### 2.6 Less is built than the map says
- Xylophora has one real task. Tasks 2 and 3 are empty folders. Task 4 is a title.
- Patternox and Comets are not playable.
- So the first world is smaller than the world map's "about 25" quest places suggests.

### 2.7 The design docs need eight corrections
They are listed in `SUMMARY_FOR_MARC.md` §3. The two that matter most:
- **The Voltara pilot draft is built on the wrong numbers.** The class was 570 short, not 290. The draft uses a deck the class did not use. Its "solvable" rule fails for 4 in 5 students once the cards are added.
- **5-2 is not broken.** The site already fixed it.

## 4. The MUD plan: what is missing

Everything in this section is [Proposal]. Each item says what would prove it wrong.

### 4.1 The explore layer is nearly empty
- You said: kids can explore and do random things. That is the heart of a MUD.
- The design has one ambient place. Its only object is a fish that "knows nothing" and is a dead end. The world map says of ambient places: "None are listed here yet."
- So the plan today is a quest list with typed answers. The exploring is still to be designed.
- **Suggestion:** give ambient places equal weight. Write them at the same time as the quest places, not after.

### 4.2 "Dead end by default" is the wrong rule for eight-year-olds
- Novus gives 3–4 false leads for each real find. That suits older students in one long session.
- A Grade 3 child on an iPad may manage 15–25 typed actions in a lesson. If most lead nowhere, the world feels broken.
- **Suggestion:** turn the ratio around for ambient places. Most things should answer back in a small way: a creature reacts, a door opens, an object can be carried, a secret is found.
- **What would prove this wrong:** watch five children for 15 minutes in a dead-end-heavy room and a responsive room. See which they return to.

### 4.3 "Do random things" needs a short list of saved changes
- You said: "if you want to dig a hole in the ground, you dig a hole in the ground."
- The template allows one change: leaving a flag.
- The AI can describe anything. The code can only save what it has a slot for. Without slots, the hole is gone on the next visit.
- **Suggestion:** define about eight kinds of lasting change: dig, build, plant, move, open, mark, leave an object, feed or befriend a creature. Each place says which kinds it allows and how many it holds. The AI turns a child's words into one of these. The code saves it.
- These carry no free text. So no child reads another child's raw words, and no answers leak.

### 4.4 Your own story already holds the exploring hooks
All [Source]:
- The intro says: "Hidden around the station are special parts." In the tasks, parts are earned, never found. Hidden things on the station are a ready-made reason to explore.
- The intro promises to "find the perfect new home for Earth." No quest ends that search. It could be the long goal of exploring planets.
- Voltara has talking animals, the giggling Gumblefish and FlavorBursts.
- Three drafted, unbuilt quests exist: Multiplo-Planet, Planet Lengthar and Geometria.

### 4.4a Update: mini quests fill this gap
- After reading this file you described mini quests: quick problems inside the world, different for each student, each earning a card.
- That is the missing pay-off for exploring. The design is in `proposals/GMQ_Mini_Quests_v1_2026-11-16.md`.
- The Canva planets and alien letters are not GMQ, so they are not listed above.

### 4.5 Keep the AI out of the numbers, both ways
- An AI can miscopy a number when it writes a scene: 570 becomes 750.
- **Suggestion:** when a task is open, the code prints the child's numbers in a fixed panel. The AI tells the story around it.
- For answers, use the separate answer line (handoff §5.3, option b). The AI never reads or passes on an answer.
- This keeps everything typed. It removes the two places where the AI could break the maths.

### 4.6 The cost in reading and typing
- Every action costs a child time to type and time to read the reply.
- The risk: 10 minutes of maths inside 30 minutes of reading.
- The 1.4 grade-level gain came from a version with paper, blocks, cards and teams. It was not a text game. [Source: Edutopia draft, your account]
- **What to measure in the pilot:** minutes of maths per session, and how long each AI reply takes to arrive. A thinking model that takes 10 seconds per turn will lose this age group.

### 4.7 Groups
- The class worked in groups of about four. The Trade Quest "buzzes with teams". [Source]
- One iPad each, with different numbers each, turns a team game into solo work.
- **Options:** (a) solo numbers, but children help each other with method; (b) one set of numbers per group; (c) solo for the station, groups for galaxy quests.
- Each costs something. (a) loses the team buzz. (b) lets one child carry the group. (c) needs both modes built.

### 4.8 The pilot
- The docs propose Voltara. It is the most tangled quest: four supplies, three decks, three lessons, and block work that belongs on a table.
- **Suggestion:** pilot a slice of the station instead: Arrival Dock, the Navigation Bay (2-1, 2-2, 2-3), and two or three ambient rooms.
  - 2-1 and 2-2 are already "keep". 2-3 needs one decision.
  - All three are typed (group A).
  - It is the real start of the game.
  - It tests the thing most in doubt: do Grade 3 children enjoy exploring by typing?
- **Cost:** it does not test scenario cards or class-wide rewards. Voltara would come second.

### 4.9 Two things to settle before students log in
- **Where typed words go.** Children's free text would be sent to DeepSeek. Check this against KIS policy and what parents have agreed to. Use nicknames.
- **Open files.** Some Drive files are open to anyone with the link, including a saved ChatGPT history. The Google Forms show their answers in the page source.
