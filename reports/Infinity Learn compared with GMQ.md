# Keep GMQ's direction; borrow five habits

Report date: 17 November 2026. Written for the teacher who makes Galaxy Math Quest (GMQ).

**The straight answer.** Keep doing what you are doing, and borrow five plain habits. Infinity Learn is not a rival to GMQ and not a model for it. It is an exam-coaching business for teenagers. What it gives a Grade 3 child is a school companion app of videos, quizzes and school-set tests, free worksheet PDFs, and live small-group classes. It has no story, no world, no hands-on maths, and no independent proof that it teaches. It is still better than GMQ in ways that matter: it covers the whole year's maths, it runs on a routine, it has timed practice, it promises reports to adults, and real children have used it for years. GMQ's design fits the independent evidence for 8-year-olds better on four points: a teacher in the room, real objects, maths as the way forward, and code (not AI) marking the sums. But GMQ is unproven too. One child has played the current build, there is no outcome data, only number work is playable, there is no timed fluency practice, and the AI has never answered a child. The most useful next step is not a feature from anyone's app. It is to put GMQ in front of your class and measure what happens.

**How to read this report**

- **[Source: …]** means the fact is in the research notes, with its link. GMQ facts link to your own project files.
- **[Proposal]** means it is my suggestion. The decision is yours.
- **[Conflict]** means two sources disagree. Both are given.
- **Company claim** means the company said it about itself and nobody outside checked it.
- **Search summary only** means the page would not open. Only a search engine's summary was seen. Trust these less.
- Effect sizes are in standard deviations (SD). As a rough guide, good maths practice software adds 0.05 to 0.20 SD. Good human tutoring adds about 0.3 SD. [Source: [Oreopoulos and Low 2026][oreo]]
- No student names, passwords, keys or teacher codes are in this report. Nothing in `drive/` was opened.

## The evidence backs GMQ's bones and questions four of its bets

This section judges both against independent research, not against what apps commonly do.

| What independent evidence says | Infinity Learn | GMQ |
|---|---|---|
| **Teacher-run beats stand-alone.** In games, teacher support gave the largest effect (g = 0.58, against 0.26 for success/fail/points alone). UNESCO: technology should "support, but never supplant" the teacher. [Source: [Clark et al. 2016][clark]; [UNESCO 2023][unesco]] | Partly fits. An adult is always in the loop, but the app is used at home on a parent's phone. | **Fits.** The teacher is built into the game. |
| **Real objects help**, when linked clearly to the idea. Rated strong by the WWC. A meta-analysis found small to moderate gains over symbols only. [Source: [WWC][wwc]; [Carbonneau et al. 2013][carbonneau]; [EEF guidance][eef-maths]] | Does not fit. None found for maths. | **Fits**, with one warning. Your own analysis says the station tasks "start with doing" and skip the "see it" step. [Source: [Projects §4a][projects]] |
| **Maths should be the game's core move**, not a quiz bolted on. Children learned more, and chose to play 7 times longer. Small samples (58 and 16 children). [Source: [Habgood and Ainsworth 2011][habgood]] | Does not fit. A quiz follows a video. | **Mostly fits.** "Every piece of maths opens something." But several Voltara steps carry no maths. [Source: [Voltara guide §2 and §4][voltara]] |
| **An AI that hands over answers harms learning.** Teenagers with a plain chatbot scored 17 percent lower on a later test without it. A hints-only tutor did no harm and showed no gain. [Source: [Bastani et al. 2025][bastani]] | Unknown, and a risk. AINA solves "doubts". Nobody has shown whether it hints or answers. | **Fits on paper, and a bet.** Code marks. A reply is stopped if it has a number the world did not give, marks or gives an answer, or hands out a reward. Never run with a real model. No study was found on an AI used as a narrator. [Source: [Server README][server]] |
| **Rich story does not reliably help.** Games with no story or a thin story beat medium-depth stories (g = 0.44 and 0.47 against -0.03). Thick stories scored 0.36, from only five studies. The samples average age 13. [Source: [Clark et al. 2016][clark]] | No story, so no risk and no pull. | **A bet.** GMQ's story is rich. No study was found on explorable text worlds or on "decisions with consequences" for primary maths. |
| **Reading load hides maths.** Simplifying the wording helped English learners most. The WWC also rates teaching maths language as strong. [Source: [Abedi and Lord 2001][abedi]; [WWC][wwc]] | Likely a lower load. Not seen. | **A bet.** High load. Your rule to use maths words openly fits the WWC. The volume of other text does not. [Source: [INFO_FROM_MARC][info]] |
| **Fluency needs timed practice.** WWC recommendation 6, rated strong. Understanding and procedure feed each other. [Source: [WWC][wwc]; [Rittle-Johnson et al. 2015][rittle]] | Fits. | **A gap.** None in the visible game. |
| **Rewards for finishing a task can lower later interest**, more in children than adults (d = -0.36 for completion rewards). **[Conflict]** This finding was disputed in print. No research at all was found on collectible cards or streaks. [Source: [Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999][deci]; [published comment][deci-comment]] | Ranks, scholarships and certificates. | **A bet.** One card for each finished task is a completion reward. Whether a card acts as pay or as a record of success is not settled. The battle rules are yours and are out of scope here. |
| **Competition shows no learning benefit.** Single-player competitive games showed no gain (g = -0.06, against 0.45 without competition). The samples span ages 6 to 25. Leaderboards can demotivate those ranked low. [Source: [Clark et al. 2016][clark]; [SMU leaderboard review][smu]] | Does not fit. Ranks and "live quiz competitions". | **Mostly fits.** No leaderboard is described. Children can compare Voltara towers and gem counts if they choose. Battles are not built. [Source: [Voltara guide §11][voltara]; [Trade guide §8][trade]] |

Two things follow.

- **GMQ's bones are sound.** Teacher in the room, real objects, maths as the way through, and code doing the marking are the parts the evidence supports best. Infinity Learn's primary products have only the first of these, and only in part.
- **GMQ's four bets are unproven, not wrong.** Rich story, heavy reading, cards for finishing, and an AI narrator have no good evidence for or against at age 8. Fitting the evidence in design is not the same as working in class.

## Both sides have real weaknesses, and neither has proof

**Infinity Learn's weaknesses**

- **No independent evidence.** Every outcome number comes from the company, mostly through paid press releases. No trial, no outside evaluation and no EdTech Tulna review was found. [Source: [Central Square Foundation on EdTech Tulna][tulna]; [press release][aina]]
- **Its rank claims do not test the app.** They are mostly about the parent group, Sri Chaitanya. The researchers add that its press releases do not separate online-only learners from classroom students. [Source: [course page][il-neet11]]
- **Tests without feedback.** "We only get a score of around 10/15. But we're not getting the correct answer." [Source: [Google Play: Meta Jr.][mjr-play]]
- **Marking errors are reported.** "Even when we provide correct answers, the app often marks them as wrong." Single reports. [Source: [App Store reviews][mjr-ios-reviews]]
- **Children cannot get in alone.** Logins need an admission number, a PIN and a one-time password. Of the 20 most recent written App Store reviews, 17 gave one star. The App Store average is 3.08 from 284 ratings. Google Play shows 4.1. [Source: [App Store reviews][mjr-ios-reviews]; [App Store][mjr-ios]; [Google Play][mjr-play]]
- **Sales and refund complaints**, including one each from families of a Grade 2 and a Grade 4 child. Trustpilot shows 2.4 out of 5, with 60 percent one-star. Angry customers post more, so the size is unknown. The pattern repeats across three sites. [Source: [Trustpilot][trustpilot]; [ConsumerComplaints.in, Grade 2 case][cc-grade2]; [ConsumerComplaints.in][cc-company]]
- **[Conflict] Privacy statements disagree.** The Play store declaration says the app "doesn't collect or share any user data". The privacy policy lists names, phone numbers, location and "voice samples". [Source: [Play data safety][il-datasafety]; [privacy policy][il-privacy]]
- **[Conflict] Its size is unclear.** The company told the press revenue was about Rs 200 crore in FY24 (search summary only). Tracxn's band is Rs 50 to 100 crore (search summary only). No audited figure was found. [Source: [Business Standard][bs-2024]; [Tracxn][tracxn]]

The parent group has separate, documented problems at its physical colleges. They are not about the app, so they are left out here.

**GMQ's weaknesses**

- **One child has played the current build.** She tapped through without reading. [Source: [Roadmap §1][roadmap]; [INFO_FROM_MARC][info]]
- **No outcome data of any kind.** The checks prove the numbers work. They do not prove children learn. [Source: [Roadmap §1][roadmap]]
- **Number work only.** Four of five strands have no playable quest. The school's scope and sequence has not been supplied, so nobody has checked coverage. [Source: [Roadmap §3][roadmap]; [INFO_FROM_MARC, item 6][info]]
- **No timed fluency practice.** The skills bank is hidden because you judged it "a bit too much". [Source: [Bigger Universe §4][universe]; [INFO_FROM_MARC][info]]

## Five things worth borrowing

None of these is special to Infinity Learn. They are ordinary school habits that it happens to have and GMQ does not yet have. All five are **[Proposal]**. The choices are yours.

### 1. A fixed slot in class, before any home use

- **What Infinity Learn does.** The school sets the work. Live classes run twice a week. [Source: [HOTS trial page][wk-hots-trial]; [App Store reviews][mjr-ios-reviews]]
- **Why it applies to 8-year-olds on iPads.** Two software trials with measured gains used scheduled time with an adult in the room. One was in primary and middle grades, one in middle school. Dutch primary pupils "made little or no progress while learning from home". Games played over many sessions beat one-off play (g = 0.44 against 0.08). [Source: [J-PAL, Rajasthan][jpal-raj]; [Engzell et al. 2021][engzell]; [Clark et al. 2016][clark]]
- **Options and cost.**
  - Class only. Nothing to build. Costs lesson time.
  - Class first, home later. Needs the server on your site, safe logins and a privacy plan.
  - Home too, from the start. The most work and the least support in the evidence.
- **This bears on your open question** "Class only, or at home too?" [Source: [Roadmap §5.1][roadmap]]

### 2. A few minutes of timed fluency practice somewhere in the week

- **What Infinity Learn does.** Timed quizzes and daily practice. [Source: [FAQ][il-faq]]
- **Why it applies.** The WWC rates timed fluency activities as strong evidence for Grades K to 6. GMQ shows ideas and uses them once. It does not repeat them until they are quick. [Source: [WWC][wwc]]
- **Options and cost.**
  - Do it off the screen: paper or spoken, in your normal lessons. Nothing to build. If your class already does this, nothing to change.
  - Show a small slice of the hidden skills bank as a short warm-up. The bank exists, so I expect the build to be small. That is my estimate. The risk is the one you named: "too much". [Source: [Bigger Universe §4][universe]]
  - Leave it out of GMQ on purpose. Then say so, so no one thinks GMQ covers fluency.
- **Limits.** The WWC guide is about pupils who struggle. It may not carry in full to a whole class. Keep any drill free of ranking (see the list below).

### 3. After a wrong answer, show the idea again. Never just a score

- **What Infinity Learn does.** The opposite. This is its most named teaching fault. Borrow the lesson, not the feature. [Source: [Google Play: Meta Jr.][mjr-play]]
- **Why it applies.** In games, real support after an attempt (g = 0.48 to 0.58) beat success/fail/points alone (g = 0.26). An 8-year-old who is told "wrong" learns only that. [Source: [Clark et al. 2016][clark]]
- **Options and cost.**
  - First, check what each lock does now on a miss. The guides do not say. Cost: an hour of play.
  - Where a "see it" moment exists (Voltara's fish, the sparks and rods), replay it on a miss. Small build.
  - Where none exists (the station), one must be made first. Your own analysis already asks for this. [Source: [Projects §4a][projects]]
- **Limit.** The replay must not hand over the answer. That is the lesson of the chatbot trial, which was with teenagers. [Source: [Bastani et al. 2025][bastani]]

### 4. A one-page skills map, and a plain record for each child

- **What Infinity Learn does.** Its content follows the syllabus chapter by chapter, and it promises reports to adults. [Source: [worksheets page][il-ws3]; [Google Play: Meta Jr.][mjr-play]]
- **Why it applies.** The researcher found no document that maps GMQ's tasks to a Grade 3 skill list. In one large trial, software raised scores on the researchers' tests and had no effect on school exams. A map is how you would see that coming. [Source: research notes on your files, see [Roadmap §3][roadmap]; [J-PAL, Rajasthan][jpal-raj]]
- **Options and cost.**
  - A table: each skill, where GMQ teaches it, where it does not. Cost: you supply the school's scope and sequence, then an afternoon. [Source: [INFO_FROM_MARC, item 6][info]]
  - Add to the class view: which locks each child opened on the first try. This needs a build. The server does not yet mark the maths. [Source: [Server README][server]]
  - A parent report. Not needed for a class project. No evidence in the notes for or against.
- **Borrow the map, not the method.** Covering every chapter with videos and worksheets is what Infinity Learn does. Nothing in the notes shows it works.

### 5. A "read it to me" button. Listening, not speaking

- **What Infinity Learn does.** Its lessons are led by video. Its AI mentor for teenagers speaks English and nine Indian languages (company claim). [Source: [Google Play: Meta Jr.][mjr-play]; [press release][aina]]
- **Why it applies.** Language load lowers maths scores for second-language learners. For Grade 5 and 6 pupils, video beat illustrated text, and weak readers gained most. Both studies are with older children, so this is a step down in age. Your files mention children still learning English, but give no numbers. [Source: [Abedi and Lord 2001][abedi]; [Learning and Instruction 2025][video]; [INFO_FROM_MARC, item 7][info]]
- **Options and cost.**
  - Record the reading cards in your own voice. Costs your time. Fits the Commander role.
  - Use the iPad's built-in speech. Likely cheap. This is my assumption. The researchers did not check it.
  - Leave reading as it is, and teach the words in class first.
- **This is already on your list of open decisions.** [Source: [Station guide][station]]
- **Do not borrow the other half.** No microphones, no voice recordings of children. See below.

|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Video lessons or live online classes as the main teaching** | Young children often learn less from video than from a person (a review of ages 0 to 6). Dutch primary pupils taught remotely made little or no progress. You are in the room. [Source: [Strouse and Samson][strouse]; [Engzell et al. 2021][engzell]] |
| 2 | **An AI that answers maths questions** | Answer-giving AI lowered later scores by 17 percent. In middle schools, a hints-only AI tutor added about what plain practice adds, and the median pupil used it in only 17 percent of sessions where they made a mistake. No rigorous trial exists for ages 6 to 10. Keep your rule: code marks, the AI tells the story. [Source: [Bastani et al. 2025][bastani]; [Oreopoulos and Low 2026][oreo]] |
| 3 | **Voice-first AI and voice recordings** | Infinity Learn's policy collects "voice samples" and sets its minimum age at 18 while selling to children. A 2026 test of AI toys found 27 percent of outputs unfit for children. [Source: [privacy policy][il-privacy]; [Common Sense Media 2026][csm-toys]] |
| 4 | **Ranks, scholarship tests, quiz competitions, leaderboards** | Competitive single-player games showed no learning gain. Leaderboards can demotivate children ranked low. **[Conflict]** One study with older students found no harm from leaderboards. In one small class, every child knows who is last. That last point is my reasoning, not a study. [Source: [Clark et al. 2016][clark]; [SMU review][smu]; [2022 physics study][physics]] |
| 5 | **Score-only tests** | See habit 3 above. It is the complaint parents make most about the teaching. [Source: [Google Play: Meta Jr.][mjr-play]] |
| 6 | **Compulsory home use on a parent's phone, with one-time passwords** | Children cannot log in alone. Parents resent it: the school "is forcing and compelling us to use mobile App". [Source: [Google Play: Meta Jr.][mjr-play]] |
| 7 | **A full adaptive practice engine, now** | Well-run trials give 0.05 to 0.20 SD. The largest effects came where normal teaching was very weak. That is not your class. It is heavy to build, and you already judged it "too much". Try habit 2 first. [Source: [Oreopoulos and Low 2026][oreo]; [J-PAL, Rajasthan][jpal-raj]; [INFO_FROM_MARC][info]] |
| 8 | **Points, badges and streaks, because apps have them** | Infinity Learn's primary products do not even advertise them. No research at all was found on streaks. Rewards for showing up are where the harm findings sit. [Source: [Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999][deci]] |
| 9 | **Counting your own numbers as proof** | UNESCO: "Good, impartial evidence on the impact of education technology is in short supply", and company evidence may be biased. This applies to rank lists and download counts. It applies equally to "all tests pass". [Source: [UNESCO 2023][unesco]] |

Infinity Learn's selling methods (home visits, loans, hard refunds) have no place in a class project and need no further comment. [Source: [refund policy][il-refund]; [ConsumerComplaints.in, Grade 2 case][cc-grade2]]

## Conclusion

The comparison ends up saying more about evidence than about features. Infinity Learn's one real advantage over GMQ is contact with thousands of real children, and its reviews show what that contact exposes: logins that fail, tests that teach nothing, and use that feels forced. GMQ has had almost none of that contact. Its designs are checked to the last plan, but a child has not yet been checked against them.

The research also points somewhere neither product advertises. Much of the measured gain from learning software seems to come from what surrounds it: a set time, an adult who knows the children, and a reason to keep going. You supply all three by being the teacher. That is GMQ's real edge, and it is hard for a company to copy. The five habits above are small. The step that would change what is known is a class trial with a before-and-after check, and that needs no new build at all.
