# Keep GMQ's course; borrow five small things

Report date: 17 November 2026. Written for the teacher who makes Galaxy Math Quest (GMQ).

This report is about **Infinilearn**, the browser maths role-playing game at infinilearn.com. It is not about "Infinity Learn by Sri Chaitanya". An earlier report covered that company. It is an Indian exam-coaching business for teenagers, and that report ruled it out as a model for GMQ.

**The straight answer.** Keep doing what you are doing, and borrow five small things. Infinilearn is a different kind of game from GMQ. It is a fantasy battle game in which every attack is a maths question, most of them four-option multiple choice, picked by an adaptive engine from a fixed question bank. It is built for older children. Its own pages say in several places that Grade 4 is its real floor, and its samples are Grade 6 work. It is better than GMQ in ways that matter: real classes use it, it covers many topics, it gives a lot of practice in a short time, it adapts to each child, it shows the teacher accuracy by topic, and it reads questions aloud. GMQ fits the independent evidence for 8-year-olds better on four points: the maths is the task itself, real objects are used, the teacher is in the room and in the game, and children type answers that code checks. But GMQ is unproven. One child has played the current build. It has no outcome data. Only number work is playable. It has no timed fluency practice. It has no adaptive question choice in play. Its AI has never answered a child. Infinilearn is also unproven, and also very small: one founder, a student, with no independent review and no study. Nobody on this project played it. Its user figures are its maker's own. The most useful next step is still not a feature from anyone's game. It is your class playing GMQ while you count what happens.

**How to read this report**

- **[Source: …]** means the fact is in the research notes, with its link. GMQ facts link to your own project files.
- **[Proposal]** means it is my suggestion. The decision is yours.
- **[Conflict]** means two sources disagree. Both are given.
- **Maker claim** means Infinilearn says it about itself. Nobody outside checked it. Most facts about the game are maker claims.
- **Seen** means a researcher saw it on a public page or in one of the maker's six store screenshots. Nobody saw the live game.
- **My reading** means it is my inference from the evidence, not a sourced fact.
- Effect sizes are in standard deviations (SD, d or g). As a rough guide, good maths practice software adds 0.05 to 0.20 SD. Good human tutoring adds about 0.3 SD. [Source: [Oreopoulos and Low 2026][oreo]]
- No student names, passwords, keys or teacher codes are in this report. Nothing in `drive/` was opened. No account was made on Infinilearn and no form was submitted.

## Infinilearn is one student's battle game, built for older children

**What it is**

- A free-to-start, browser-based, multiplayer fantasy game for maths practice only. Its own summary: "A free fantasy RPG where students battle monsters by solving real math problems." Maker claim. [Source: [home page][home]]
- It is practice, not teaching. The founder writes: "It's practice, not lessons... If a concept is brand new, your child needs a teacher, a video, or you." [Source: [founder's comparison post][vs-prodigy]]
- It names Prodigy Math as its rival. Its page keywords include "prodigy alternative". [Source: [home page][home]; [After Prodigy page][after]]
- It does not use AI with children. The researchers searched 12 core pages and 174 blog posts and found no claim that AI writes the questions, the story or the feedback. The one stated AI use is screening of usernames. Questions come from "a standards-aligned question bank". [Source: [sitemap][sitemap]; [Steam page][steam]; [llms.txt][llms]]

**What a child does** (maker claims and six store screenshots; nobody played it)

1. Makes a hero. Walks around a top-down, pixel-art world called Numeria. Seen in screenshots. [Source: [Steam page][steam]]
2. Taps through short story lines. One reads: "Something has happened. The Seal that protects Numeria... it's breaking." Seen. [Source: [Steam page][steam]]
3. Takes a quest. Two seen examples: "Defeat creatures 0/4" and "Defeat 10 Fractured Slimes in Hollow Grove". [Source: [Steam page][steam]; [home page][home]]
4. Walks to a monster and taps "Attack". A question screen covers the scene. The one real question seen: "If 5 apples cost $40, what is the cost per apple?" with four buttons ($40, $7, $9, $8), a "Hint" button and a "Cast Spell" button. Seen. The sum is right: 40 ÷ 5 = 8. [Source: [Steam page][steam]]
5. A right answer is an attack. A wrong answer costs hit points. Maker claim. [Source: [switching post][switching]; [blog, fraction games][blog-fractions]]
6. Earns experience points, levels, gold, gear and pets. Maker claim. [Source: [Steam page][steam]; [premium page][premium]]

The maker says the engine picks each question from the child's weakest topics. So the question has no link to the place or the monster. [Source: [llms.txt][llms]] My reading: the maths is the gate for combat. It is not the shape of the task.

**How small it is**

- The founder, Adrian Martinez, wrote on his About page that he is "a high school student in Orange County, California". An October 2026 interview says he is now a first-year student at UC Berkeley. **[Conflict]** The About page looks out of date. [Source: [About page][about]; [Phaser interview][phaser]]
- "Production access is limited to the founder." He says: "I ship fixes myself." No staff are named anywhere. No funding was found. [Source: [security page][security]; [founder's comparison post][vs-prodigy]]
- The domain was registered in March 2024. He says the project "pivoted four times". Public records fit that: an AI "Personalized Learning Companion" in September 2024, a learning management system in December 2024, then the maths game in 2025. So the game is one to two years old. [Source: [domain record][rdap]; [Product Hunt][ph]; [Hacker News post][hn]; [Phaser interview][phaser]]
- He names his own weak point: "Our servers are the weakest part of the system right now and I'll say that openly." [Source: [Phaser interview][phaser]]

So this is not a big company against a small teacher. It is two very small projects. One has many users by its own count. The other has one.

**Grade 3 sits at its edge**

- The home page says grades 3 to 9, and the sign-up form has a "3rd" button. Seen. [Source: [home page][home]; [student sign-up page][signup]]
- Other pages say: "Infinilearn starts at 4th grade" and "Grade 4 is the floor. For a child in 3rd grade or below, Prodigy or another elementary game is still the better choice". [Source: [After Prodigy page][after]]
- Every sample on the home page is Grade 6 or 7 work: "Solve for x: x ÷ 3 = 9", "4x = 28", dashboard bars labelled "6.RP Ratios". Seen. [Source: [home page][home]]
- All five teacher quotes on the home page come from Grade 5 to Grade 8 teachers, or name no grade. None is from Grade 3 or 4. [Source: [home page][home]]
- No Grade 3 question was seen by anyone on this project.

Two things it has that look like advantages but are not clearly so:

- **Painted pixel art.** The founder says real artists made it. GMQ's pictures are drawn by code. In the games research, plain schematic pictures (g = 0.48) did better than cartoon (0.32) or realistic ones (-0.01). So better art is not shown to help learning. The samples average age 13. [Source: [Phaser interview][phaser]; [Roadmap §2][roadmap]; [Clark et al. 2016][clark]]
- **Privacy pages.** They are long and candid for a project this size. They are also self-declared, and all about US law. A researcher saw a Meta tracking script load on the marketing home page and the adult sign-up pages. The privacy policy does not list Meta. It did not load on the student sign-up page. [Source: [security page][security]; [privacy policy][privacy]; [home page][home]]

## The evidence backs GMQ's design on four points and Infinilearn's on three

This section judges both against independent research, not against what games commonly do.

| What independent evidence says | Infinilearn | GMQ |
|---|---|---|
| **The maths should be the game's core move.** Children learned more when division was the game action than when the same game had a separate quiz. Given a free choice, they played the built-in version about 7 times longer. Small samples: 58 and 16 children. [Source: [Habgood and Ainsworth 2011][habgood]] | **Weak fit.** A question screen covers the scene. The topic has no link to the monster. This is nearer the "separate quiz" version. My reading from screenshots. | **Mostly fits.** The sum is the way across the river. But some steps have no maths. |
| **Real objects help**, when tied clearly to the idea. Rated strong by the WWC. [Source: [WWC practice guide][wwc]; [Carbonneau et al. 2013][carbonneau]] | **Does not fit.** None. | **Fits**, with one warning. Your own analysis says the station tasks "start with doing". [Source: [Projects §4a][projects]] |
| **Teacher-run beats stand-alone.** Teacher support in games: g = 0.58. Success, fail and points alone: g = 0.26. Software trials that worked used set time with an adult present. [Source: [Clark et al. 2016][clark]; [J-PAL, Rajasthan][jpal-raj]] | **Can fit.** It suggests "warmup practice, station rotation, early finisher work". The teacher is beside the game, not in it. It is also sold for home use. [Source: [teachers page][teachers]] | **Fits.** The teacher is in the game. |
| **Typed answers beat guessing.** This is logic, not a study. A blind guess among four is right one time in four. | **Weak.** Its own answer to "Can students just guess?" is that guessing "shows up in the data". [Source: [teachers page][teachers]] | **Fits.** A number pad. |
| **Fluency needs regular, often timed, practice.** WWC recommendation 6, rated strong. The guide is about pupils who struggle. [Source: [WWC practice guide][wwc]] | **Partly fits.** Lots of quick questions. Core questions are untimed. Timed "speed bounties" are optional. [Source: [accessibility page][access]] | **A gap.** None in the visible game. |
| **Adaptive practice gives small gains.** 0.05 to 0.20 SD in good trials. Only 4.7 percent of pupils reached Khan Academy's weekly dose by choice. [Source: [Oreopoulos and Low 2026][oreo]; [Education Next 2024][five-pct]] | **Fits**, if the engine works as described. Not seen. | **A gap.** |
| **Reading load hides maths.** Simpler wording helped English learners most. The WWC also rates teaching maths language as strong. [Source: [Abedi and Lord 2001][abedi]; [WWC practice guide][wwc]] | **Mixed.** Short lines and read-aloud help. Invented names and US wording ("cost per") do not. English and French only. | **A bet.** High load, no read-aloud. Your rule to name maths words fits the WWC. |
| **Rich story does not reliably help.** No story or a thin story beat medium-depth stories (g = 0.44 and 0.47 against -0.03). The samples average age 13. [Source: [Clark et al. 2016][clark]] | **Unknown.** It claims "a real narrative". Nobody saw how deep it is. | **A bet.** GMQ's story is rich. No study was found on explorable worlds or on choices with consequences at this age. |
| **Playing with or against others shows no learning gain.** Multiplayer g = -0.05. Single-player competitive g = -0.06. Single player without competition g = 0.45. [Source: [Clark et al. 2016][clark]] | **Does not fit.** A shared world and fights between players. They can be switched off. | **Fits for now.** No multiplayer. Battles are yours and not built. |
| **Rewards for finishing can lower later interest**, more in children than adults (d = -0.36). **[Conflict]** The finding was disputed in print. No research was found on streaks or cards. [Source: [Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999][deci]; [published comment][deci-comment]] | **A bet, and a bigger one.** Points, gold, gear, pets, streaks, daily gifts. | **A bet.** One card for each finished task. |
| **Extra game features can crowd out the maths.** Common Sense Media says of Prodigy that children "may get distracted with the battles, pets, spells, and upgrade suggestions". A search summary only. [Source: [Common Sense Media on Prodigy][csm-prodigy]] | **A risk.** Same design as Prodigy. Its claim of 20 questions in 10 minutes argues against it, if true. | **A risk too.** Exploring, secrets and dead ends take time. Not measured. |

What follows:

- **GMQ's bones fit the evidence best** on four points: maths as the task, real objects, the teacher in the game, typed answers.
- **Infinilearn fits it best** on three: amount of practice, adaptive choice, and help with reading. These are the three places GMQ has a gap.
- **Fitting the evidence in design is not the same as working in class.** Neither game has shown that.

## Both are small and unproven, and both should say so

**GMQ's weaknesses, stated plainly**

- **One child has played the current build.** "Every 'it works' in my reports means 'a test passes', not 'children enjoy it'." [Source: [Roadmap §1][roadmap]]
- **That one child tapped without reading.** "She thinks it's just a click click click game." [Source: [INFO_FROM_MARC][info]]
- **No outcome data of any kind.** No scores, no times, no completion rates.
- **Number work only.** Four of five strands have no playable quest. [Source: [Roadmap §3][roadmap]]
- **No timed fluency practice.** A skills bank of 72 skills exists and is hidden. You called it "too much" as a trainer. [Source: [Bigger Universe §4][universe]]
- **No adaptive question choice in play.**
- **The AI has never answered a child.** "A constraint file exists as a draft (version 0.3). It has never been run against a real model." This is the half of the design that defines it. [Source: [Roadmap §2 and §6][roadmap]]
- **Not tested on a real iPad, or with a class of 20.** [Source: [Voltara guide §9][voltara]; [Server README][server]]
- **Voltara may be too hard.** Of 5,000 random players, 170 reached the best ending. Nobody has tested whether children can hold three numbers for each place in mind. [Source: [Voltara guide §5 and §9][voltara]]
- **The whole build is two days old.** Lesson counts are guesses. [Source: [Station guide §8][station]]
- **No privacy plan for the AI.** No document says how a child's typed words would be handled if sent to an outside AI provider.
- **It needs you in the room.** A child cannot finish the station without you. That is a strength in class and a limit anywhere else.
- **[Conflict]** `CLAUDE.md` still says "Nothing has been built yet." The roadmap lists the station, Voltara and Galactic Trade as built. The standing note is out of date. [Source: [CLAUDE.md][claude]; [Roadmap §2][roadmap]]

**Infinilearn's weaknesses, stated as plainly**

- **No evidence that it teaches.** No study, no named pilot, no published data. "400,000+ problems solved, 78% accuracy" is use, not learning. A child practising what she already knows also scores 78 percent. [Source: [Steam page][steam]]
- **No independent review at all.** One six-word review on Product Hunt, about an earlier product. No Steam reviews. No teacher blog, forum thread or press article was found. Its own blog of 174 posts fills the search results. [Source: [Product Hunt reviews][ph-reviews]; [Steam page][steam]; [blog][blog]]
- **Its figures cannot be checked.** Signups went from "more than 50,000" in an interview dated 7 October 2026 to "100,000+" on 17 November. A signup needs only a first name and a grade. No school or district is named. [Source: [Phaser interview][phaser]; [home page][home]]
- **The maker has overstated before.** In December 2024 he described an earlier product as "our nations most advanced education platform", made "in collaboration with Meta". No support for that was found. [Source: [Hacker News post][hn]]
- **Nobody outside knows how the questions are made or checked.** One person covers seven grades and about twenty curriculum versions. Four questions were seen. All four were right. Four is too few to judge. [Source: [llms.txt][llms]; [Steam page][steam]; [home page][home]]
- **Guessing works.** Four choices. No typed answers below Grade 9.
- **Grade 3 is its thinnest part.** See the conflicts below.
- **One-person risk.** The founder alone has access, and is now a full-time university student. [Source: [security page][security]; [Phaser interview][phaser]]
- **A child can sign up alone,** with no adult step, and enter a shared world. [Source: [privacy policy][privacy]]
- **Money is in the room.** Premium adds a "gold name tag" and a special pet in a shared world. Prodigy drew a complaint to the US Federal Trade Commission in 2021 over this kind of visible divide. Nobody saw how Infinilearn shows Premium to children. [Source: [parents page][parents]; [Education Week 2021][edweek]]
- **iPad is not its home.** No app. Pinch zoom is switched off. Its help page says to "try Chrome or Edge". [Source: [support centre][support]; [accessibility page][access]]
- **Its compliance claims are about US law.** Nothing about Thailand. [Source: [security page][security]]

| 12 | **User counts** | "100,000+ signups." [Source: [home page][home]] | "Currently used by 10,000+ students and teachers" on an undated Steam page. One interview gives the teacher count as 3,300 in its opening and 3,100 in its body. [Source: [Steam page][steam]; [Phaser interview][phaser]] |

The two that matter most for you are numbers 1 and 2. If Grade 4 is the true floor, the game is not for your class. If only the first zone is free, a class would hit a paywall.

## Five things worth borrowing

All five are **[Proposal]**. The choices are yours. None is a feature to copy as it stands. Each is an idea that Infinilearn has, that GMQ lacks, and that the independent evidence gives some reason for.

Do one thing first: put GMQ in front of your class. Three of the five below depend on what you see there.

### 1. A plain record of hits and misses, skill by skill

- **What Infinilearn does.** It keeps "accuracy per topic" for each student and shows the teacher each child's "current working level". Maker claim. [Source: [privacy policy][privacy]; [teachers page][teachers]]
- **Why it applies to 8-year-olds on iPads.** You are GMQ's judge and helper. In games, teacher support gave the largest effect of any kind of help (g = 0.58). You can only help with what you can see. In one large trial, software raised scores on the researchers' own tests and had no effect on school exams. A record tied to your own skill list is how you would catch that. [Source: [Clark et al. 2016][clark]; [J-PAL, Rajasthan][jpal-raj]]
- **Options and cost.**
  - A paper table: each Grade 3 skill, and where GMQ meets it. Cost: you supply the school's scope and sequence, then an afternoon. That item on your information form is still blank. [Source: [INFO_FROM_MARC, item 6][info]]
  - Add to the class view: for each lock, did the child open it on the first try? This needs a build. The server does not mark the maths yet. The page does. [Source: [Server README][server]]
  - A parent report. Not needed for one class. No evidence in the notes for or against.
- **Do not borrow** the picture of a class as "81% avg mastery". Percent right on multiple choice includes lucky guesses.

### 2. Bring a missed skill back later, and make the next step easier

- **What Infinilearn does.** "When you make a mistake, the game shows the correct answer and serves more problems on that topic." And: "A miss is not a dead end; the game simply serves something they can handle next." Maker claims. Nobody saw a wrong answer in the real game. [Source: [blog, common mistakes][blog-mistakes]; [switching post][switching]]
- **Why it applies.** Adaptive practice has real trial support, though the gains are small (0.05 to 0.20 SD). GMQ has none in play. Your own quest "bones" already include "Spiral back". This is the same idea, chosen by each child's misses. [Source: [Oreopoulos and Low 2026][oreo]; [Projects §4][projects]]
- **Options and cost.**
  - Do nothing in the game. Use the record from item 1 and bring the skill back yourself, in class. No build.
  - Let each small world pick its one lock from the skills that child has missed. The 20 small worlds and the hidden skills bank both exist. This needs the server to keep misses. It also answers one of your open decisions: should small worlds carry the skills bank? [Source: [Bigger Universe §4 and §9][universe]]
  - A full adaptive engine. See the "not worth borrowing" list.
- **Limits.** Do not borrow "shows the correct answer". With teenagers, an AI tool that handed over answers cut later unaided scores by 17 percent. That was a chatbot, not a game, so this is a caution and not a finding about games. On a miss, GMQ should show the idea again, not the number. [Source: [Bastani et al. 2025][bastani]]

### 3. A short burst of quick questions somewhere in the week

- **What Infinilearn does.** About 20 questions in 10 minutes, by its own count. Its core questions are untimed. Timed "speed bounties" are optional extras. [Source: [founder's comparison post][vs-prodigy]; [accessibility page][access]]
- **Why it applies.** GMQ shows an idea and uses it once or twice. It does not repeat it until it is quick. The WWC rates "regular timed activities to build fluency" as strong evidence for Grades K to 6. Infinilearn's untimed core with optional timing is a gentle way to do it. [Source: [WWC practice guide][wwc]]
- **Options and cost.**
  - Do it off the screen, on paper or aloud, in your normal lessons. Nothing to build. If your class already does this, nothing to change.
  - Show a small slice of the hidden skills bank as a five-minute warm-up before a quest. The bank exists, so I expect the build to be small. That is my estimate. The risk is the one you named: "too much".
  - Leave it out of GMQ on purpose, and say so. The founder does this well: "It's practice, not lessons." GMQ could say the mirror: quests, not drill.
- **Limits.** The WWC guide is about pupils who struggle. Keep any timing private to the child. No ranking. See the next list.

### 4. A "read it to me" button and a large-text switch

- **What Infinilearn does.** "Math problems can be read aloud with built-in text to speech." It also has Large Text and High Contrast modes, all free. Maker claim. It does not say story text is read aloud. [Source: [accessibility page][access]]
- **Why it applies.** Your class has children still learning English. Language load lowers maths scores for second-language learners. For Grade 5 and 6 pupils, video beat illustrated text, and weak readers gained most. Both studies are with older children, so this is a step down in age. [Source: [Abedi and Lord 2001][abedi]; [Learning and Instruction 2025][video]; [INFO_FROM_MARC, item 7][info]]
- **Options and cost.**
  - Record the reading cards in your own voice. Costs your time. Fits the Commander.
  - Use the iPad's built-in speech. Likely cheap. That is my assumption. Nobody checked it.
  - Leave reading as it is and teach the words in class first.
- **This is already one of your open decisions.** [Source: [Station guide][station]]
- **A caution from the one child who played.** Read-aloud helps a child who tries to read. It does not help a child who taps past the words. Only needing the words does that. [Source: [Station guide][station]]

### 5. Preset words, not free typing, wherever one child sees another's words

- **What Infinilearn does.** Usernames are made by the game. Chat is preset phrases and emotes only. "Students cannot post free-text content or type messages to other players." Maker claim. [Source: [privacy policy][privacy]; [premium page][premium]]
- **Why it applies.** This is a safety habit, not a learning one. No learning evidence backs it. The reason is your own rule: "children cannot write curse words. For the ship name or anywhere." Your filter has gaps you already know. "No word list is complete." Thai words in English letters are not on it. Lists cannot be got round. [Source: [INFO_FROM_MARC][info]; [Station guide][station]]
- **Options and cost.**
  - Ship names: pick two words from lists. Small build. Costs the child some freedom. You already have this as one of three options, beside a filter and your approval.
  - Keep free typing for commands to the game. Only the child sees those. This is not the same risk.
  - If battles or trading ever put two children on one screen, start with preset phrases. The battle rules are yours. This is only a note for when you write them.

## Ten things not worth borrowing

The verdicts are mine, so they are **[Proposal]**. The facts behind them are tagged.

| # | Not worth borrowing | Why not, for 8-year-olds on iPads |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A question that pops up to power an attack** | It is the weaker form of game. Children learned more, and chose to play longer, when the maths was the action itself. GMQ already does the stronger thing. The study is small. Your battle rules are out of scope here and I am not judging them. [Source: [Habgood and Ainsworth 2011][habgood]] |
| 2 | **Four-option multiple choice** | A blind guess is right one time in four. Infinilearn's only answer is that guessing shows in the data later. Your number pad is better. [Source: [teachers page][teachers]] |
| 3 | **A shared live world, trading and fights between players** | Multiplayer games showed no learning gain (g = -0.05). Nor did competitive single play (g = -0.06). The samples span ages 6 to 25. It also brings the safety work of item 5 above. [Source: [Clark et al. 2016][clark]] |
| 4 | **Experience points, levels, gold, gear and pets** | Rewards for finishing a task lowered later free-choice interest, more in children than adults. **[Conflict]** That finding was disputed. Games that gave only success, fail and points did worse (g = 0.26) than games with real help (0.48 to 0.58). GMQ already has cards. More layers need a reason, and "games have them" is not one. [Source: [Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999][deci]; [published comment][deci-comment]; [Clark et al. 2016][clark]] |
| 5 | **Streaks and a daily gift** | These reward showing up, which is where the harm findings sit. No research at all was found on streaks. [Source: [Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999][deci]] |
| 6 | **Quests that count kills** | "Defeat 10 Fractured Slimes" is a count, not a task. Your rule is "a job, not a worksheet". [Source: [home page][home]; [Projects §4][projects]] |
| 7 | **A full adaptive engine across every topic, now** | Good trials give 0.05 to 0.20 SD. The largest effects came where teaching was very weak. That is not your class. It is heavy to build, and you judged a trainer "too much". Try items 2 and 3 above first. [Source: [Oreopoulos and Low 2026][oreo]; [J-PAL, Rajasthan][jpal-raj]; [INFO_FROM_MARC][info]] |
| 8 | **Fantasy names for their own sake** | In about 45 words of real game text, a researcher counted at least 15 invented names or hard words. Interesting but irrelevant detail costs a little learning (about g = -0.16, from a conference summary). GMQ has this risk too. Count your own invented names. [Source: [Steam page][steam]; [AERA 2026 summary][aera]] |
| 9 | **Children signing themselves up, and a paid tier that shows** | You make the class. Keep that. A "gold name tag" in a shared world is the kind of divide the Prodigy complaint was about. Neither has a place in a class project. [Source: [privacy policy][privacy]; [Education Week 2021][edweek]] |
| 10 | **Counting your own numbers as proof** | UNESCO: "Good, impartial evidence on the impact of education technology is in short supply." That applies to "100,000+ signups". It applies equally to "all tests pass". [Source: [UNESCO 2023][unesco]] |

**Should your class use Infinilearn itself, beside GMQ? [Proposal]** Not as it stands, and not before the ten-minute check below. Its own page says a third grader is better served elsewhere. The samples are Grade 6 work in American wording. There is no Thai. A US one-person company would hold your students' first names and learning data, under US rules only. It could suit one or two strong readers who are working above grade. That is your call and the school's.

## What could prove this wrong

All the checks are **[Proposal]**.

### A ten-minute check you can do yourself

You are the only person on this project who can settle the main unknowns. It takes one iPad and a stopwatch.

1. On a school iPad, in Safari, open the game and make one student account **yourself**. Use a made-up first name. Pick "3rd". Note that the form opens with "6th" already picked. Do not use a student's name. Do not have students sign up. [Source: [student sign-up page][signup]]
2. Play for ten minutes. Keep a tally of five things:
   - **How many maths questions** you answer. The maker says about 20.
   - **What the first ten questions are.** Are they Grade 3 work? Could your English learners read them?
   - **What a wrong answer does.** Get two wrong on purpose. Does it show the answer, give a hint, or repeat the question? Tap "Hint" once.
   - **Whether any question fits the place or the quest.**
   - **Whether the game asks you, as a child, to pay or to get Premium.**
3. Note how the touch controls feel, and whether the text is big enough.
4. If you have time left, walk towards the second zone. See whether a free player can enter.

What the result means:

- If the questions are sound Grade 3 work that your class could read, I am wrong that Grade 3 is its thinnest part. Using it beside GMQ becomes a fair question.
- If you get far fewer than 20 questions, the "amount of practice" advantage shrinks.
- If the hint teaches well, it is worth a close look for borrow item 2.

Then do the mirror check. Watch one child play GMQ for ten minutes with the same stopwatch. Count the sums she does. That one number, set beside the other, will tell you more than this report can.

## Conclusion

The fairest way to see the two is as opposite halves of one thing. Infinilearn has the half GMQ lacks: lots of quick practice, chosen for each child, counted for the teacher, read aloud. GMQ has the half Infinilearn lacks: an idea shown before it is used, maths that is the job itself, real blocks on a real mat, and a teacher who hears the reason. The evidence gives some support to both halves, and more to them together than to either alone. So the useful question is not which game is better. It is how much of the practice half GMQ needs to carry, and how much your ordinary lessons already carry. Only you know that.

Infinilearn also holds up a mirror. Its maker is one person, working fast, with a product that outside eyes have barely seen and numbers nobody has checked. So are you. The difference is that his game has met thousands of children, by his count, and yours has met one. He cannot easily get what you have: a room, a class he knows, and the right to watch them play. You can get what he has in a single lesson. That lesson is worth more than any of the five things above.
