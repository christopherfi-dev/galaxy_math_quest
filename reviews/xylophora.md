# Review: Planet Xylophora (Galaxy Metrica, Quest 2)

**Date:** 2026-11-16
**Reviewer:** review agent for Galaxy Metrica
**Stage:** review and verdict only. Nothing here is a redesign.

**Summary.** Xylophora is the least finished of the built quests. Only Task 1 has its sheets. Task 2 has a map but no sheets. Task 3 has no materials at all. Task 4 is an empty page on the site. The story is strong and all Marc's own: a planet made of shapes, a sleeping giant whose skin becomes building material, trees with square leaves. The maths is the problem. Task 1 needs ½ × base × height for two triangles whose base is never given, and 12.5 for the third. Task 2's map is a photo under an 816-square grid with no scale and no outline of the skin. Task 3 asks how many 2 × 2 leaves cover roofs made of 5 m squares and triangles, which never comes out whole: one square gives 6.25, so the answer is 7 or 9 depending on the method. The handoff is right about the triangle areas and the "meters" label, and there is more besides.

**Marks used:** [Source] found in Marc's material. [Conflict] his sources disagree, or a source has an error. [Proposal] my suggestion. [Assumption] a stated assumption. [Estimate] my own time estimate, not measured.

**One assumption runs through this file.** [Assumption] Every triangle tile has a base of 5 m, because its short edge clicks onto the side of a square. No source says so. An earlier draft in Marc's saved chat gives the small triangle as "5x4", which supports it for that tile. If the base is not 5 m, the big and small triangle areas below change.

## Task 1: Architectural Origami

**1. Sources:** site Task 1 page and "intro" (same text) [S1, S2]; calculation sheet [S3]; explanation sheet [S4]. Versions: the table above.

**2. Skill, and exactly what is above Grade 3**

- Strand: Measurement Quest 4, Levels 1–2. The page's practice is "IXL Grade 3 - Practice: MM8" (area of rectangles and squares). No practice is given for triangles.

| Step the sheet asks for | Maths needed | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Area of a square (S) | 5 × 5 = 25 | Grade 3 |
| Area of a big triangle (BT) | ½ × base × 10, and the base is not on the page | **above**: triangle formula, missing number |
| Area of a medium triangle (MT) | half of 25 = 12.5 | **above**: decimal, or "twelve and a half" |
| Area of a small triangle (ST) | ½ × base × 4, and the base is not on the page | **above**: triangle formula, missing number |
| Total area of squares | count × 25 | Grade 3 top end (2-digit × 1-digit) |
| Total area of medium triangles | count × 12.5 | **above**: decimal × whole number |
| Total area of a building | add up to four subtotals, some ending in .5 | **above** when a .5 is present |

- [Source] " Updated Quests - Measurement Universe." puts "Understand how to calculate the area of a right-angled triangle" in an **advanced** quest ("Level 3: Triangles and Areas"), not in the Grade 3 quest.
- The quest page tells students to "Prepare those blueprints and calculators".
- What is at Grade 3 level and worth keeping: two medium triangles make one square. A child can see that with the tiles and needs no formula.

**3. Time [Estimate]**

- Building 4 buildings with tiles: 20–30 minutes.
- Explanation sheet, once per building: 4 written questions × 4 buildings = 16 written answers. 30–40 minutes.
- Calculation sheet: 40 blanks. 30–40 minutes, more with decimals.
- **About 90–110 minutes: two to three periods.**

**4. Steps and typing**

- Site: 4 mission steps.
- Calculation sheet: 4 tile areas, then for each of 4 buildings 4 counts, 4 subtotals and 1 total. **40 blanks.**
- Explanation sheet: building type, "What Shapes Did You Use?", "How Did You Design Your Building?", "Why Did You Design It This Way?", "What's Special About Your Building?".
- Typed world: one building is 4 counts and 1 total, which is 5 numbers. Over the 3-number limit. It would need two answers per building, or a higher limit.

**5. Reading**

- Page: about 340 words. 10 of 32 sentences are over 10 words.
- Longest instruction (30 words): "You need to create the blueprint for 4 types of buildings that our colony will need to survive - a house, a hospital, a food store, and a solar factory."
- Hard words: embark, crucial, blueprint, architectural, articulate, functionality, innovative.
- "Surface area" is used for the flat area of a plan.

**6. Maths check** (`checks/xylophora_t1_check.py`, Part A)

| Tile | Area from the site's numbers | Working |
|---|---|---|
| S | 25 m² | 5 × 5 |
| BT | 25 m² | ½ × 5 × 10 [Assumption: base 5] |
| MT | 12.5 m² | 25 ÷ 2 |
| ST | 10 m² | ½ × 5 × 4 [Assumption: base 5] |

- One big triangle covers the same as one square (25).
- A child who multiplies 5 × 10 and 5 × 4 without halving gets 50 and 20, which is double.
- The site's heights are story numbers, not the tiles' real proportions. A real triangle with three 5 m edges is 4.33 m high (area 10.83). A real tall triangle with a 5 m base and 10 m long edges is 9.68 m high (area 24.21). The tiles will not measure exactly what the page says.
- Two sample buildings, my own, to show the sums: 3 squares + 1 big + 2 medium + 1 small = 75 + 25 + 25 + 10 = **135 m²**. 2 squares + 3 medium + 2 small = 50 + 37.5 + 20 = **107.5 m²**.
- Any building with an odd number of medium triangles has a total ending in .5.
- **Perimeter:** no Xylophora source asks for one. For the record: a square is 20 m. Every triangle has a slanted side that needs a square root (MT 17.07 m, BT 25.62 m, ST 14.43 m). Far above Grade 3.

The calculation sheet's labels:

- Every area blank is labelled "meters". It should be square meters. That is 23 of the 24 area blanks; the 24th has no unit (Solar Factory, total area of big triangles).
- Two count blanks carry "meters" by mistake: "Number of Big Triangles" (Solar Factory) and "Number of Small Triangles" (Food Store).
- The sheet's order is House, Hospital, Solar Factory, Food Store. The site's is Home, Hospital, Food Store, Solar Factory. All four are numbered "1."

**7. Checkpoints**

- Tile areas: 4 × **one answer**, if Marc keeps this step.
- Each building: **hands-on result**. The student builds, counts tiles, types the counts and the total. The code checks the total against the typed counts.
- What code cannot check: that the typed counts match the real building. That needs the photo or the teacher.
- Design explanation: **teacher-confirmed**.

**8. Per-student numbers**

- Each student's design is already their own, so the numbers differ without a seed. The world does not hand out numbers here.
- With the original numbers: in a test of 10,000 random students (4 buildings each; 1–6 squares, 0–2 big, 0–4 medium, 0–3 small triangles) **8,730 (87%) met a decimal total in at least one building.** The 87% depends on my guess at tile counts. The cause does not: an odd number of medium triangles.
- [Proposal] Rule tested: medium triangles are used in pairs (the page already says two make one square), and the tile areas are given, not worked out: S 25, BT 25, a pair of MT 25, ST 10. **Result: 40,000 of 40,000 buildings give a whole number.** Every total is a multiple of 5; the largest is 280.
- [Proposal] Another option, not tested beyond the arithmetic: an even side (2, 4, 6 or 10 m) makes half a square a whole number (2, 8, 18, 50). That changes Marc's 5 m.

**9. Hands-on:** group **B**.

- **What the tiles are for:** fitting shapes together into a building; seeing that two medium triangles make a square; counting pieces. That is composing and decomposing shapes, which is Shapes Quest 1 work and right for Grade 3.
- **What the student would type back:** how many of each tile, then the building's total area.
- The explanation and the photo stay with the teacher.

**10. Verdict: fix (and shorten).** Keep the tiles, the four buildings and the story. Fix the triangle areas and the decimal, give the units correctly, and cut 40 blanks and 16 written answers to what one period holds.

## Task 2: Efficient Use of Alien Resources

**1. Sources:** site Task 2 page and "intro" [S1, S2]; "alien map.pdf" [S5]. The page lists "Calculation Sheets" and "Building Plan Sheets". [Conflict] Neither exists in Drive or Canva, and the Task 2 folder is empty.

Marc's plan [Source: S8]: "I give them a grid that shows the total amount [of] resource available. They need to calculate how much area there is. We can say it's the dried skin of a giant alien creature … They decided how to maximize the resource available by calculating how many of each buildings they can make."

**2. Skill, and what is above Grade 3**

- Strand: Measurement Quest 4, Level 2, and "Solve real-life problems involving area".
- The page's practice link is meant to be IXL MM12 (area of complex figures by dividing them into rectangles). The link is broken: two addresses are joined into one.
- **Above Grade 3:**
  - the area of the grid: 34 × 24 = 816, a 2-digit × 2-digit product;
  - or counting squares under an irregular shape with no drawn edge;
  - then dividing a 3- to 5-digit total by building areas of 100 or more, some ending in .5;
  - then choosing a mix of four building types "using all the resources", which is an optimisation with many right answers.

**3. Time [Estimate]:** 40–60 minutes, and I hold that loosely. Without the sheets I cannot tell what the student was meant to write. Counting 816 squares alone could take a period.

**4. Steps and typing:** site: 4 steps. Answers: one total area; four counts (Homes, Hospitals, Food Stores, Solar Factories); an explanation. Typed: the plan is 4 numbers, over the 3-number limit.

**5. Reading**

- Page: about 320 words. 13 of 29 sentences are over 10 words.
- Longest instruction (20 words): "Plan Your Buildings: Decide how many Homes, Hospitals, Food Stores, and Solar Factories you can build using all the resources."
- Hard words: bizarre, colossal, eternal slumber, leathery, utilization, allocation, maximized.
- The "Objective" opens mid-story ("It's oddly fascinating, and incredibly, it's the perfect building material…"). The task itself comes in its second sentence.

**6. Maths check** (`checks/xylophora_t2_check.py`, Part A)

What the map is: an A3 sheet. A picture of the creature lying across farmland, with a white grid on top. No numbers, no scale, no key, no outline of the skin.

- **Grid: 34 columns × 24 rows = 816 squares.** Counted in code from the PDF at 150 dpi.
- The grid is 5 copies of a 7-column block side by side; the last is cut to 6 columns. The joins print as double lines. A thin strip at the bottom is not a full row.
- One square is about 12 mm wide on A3.
- The skin does not fill the sheet. Two corners are fields. So "the total area available" has no single answer.
- With no scale, the whole sheet could be read as 816 m² (1 square = 1 m²), 3,264 m² (1 square = one 2 × 2 leaf) or 20,400 m² (1 square = one 5 m tile).
- How many 135 m² buildings fit: 816 ÷ 135 = 6 remainder 6; 3,264 ÷ 135 = 24 remainder 24; 20,400 ÷ 135 = 151 remainder 15.
- The assessment bullets are copied from Task 1 ("Creativity and functionality in building design").

**7. Checkpoints**

- Total area: **one answer**, but only if the skin becomes a shape with a definite area.
- The plan: **conditions** (it fits; nothing more would fit). Many plans are right.
- The explanation: **teacher-confirmed**.

**8. Per-student numbers** [Proposal]

- The original cannot vary, and cannot be checked, because it has no definite area.
- Rule tested: count everything in squares. The skin is an L-shape of two rectangles, each side 5–10 squares, which is exactly the MM12 skill. The four buildings cost four different sizes from 3 to 12 squares (in the world these would be the student's own Task 1 buildings). A plan passes if it has at least one of each building, fits, and leaves less than the smallest building.
- **Result: 10,000 of 10,000 seeds are solvable and stay inside the 10 × 10 facts.** Skin totals run from 50 to 200 squares. Right plans per student: fewest 4, middle 244, most 8,097. So the plan is a conditions checkpoint, never a single answer.

**9. Hands-on:** as written, group **A**: it is arithmetic on a printed grid. The tiles are not needed, unless students lay their real buildings on a real grid, which no source describes.

**10. Verdict: redo.** The idea (a creature's skin as the supply) is worth keeping whole. The task as it stands has no definite numbers, no sheets, and maths well above Grade 3.

## Task 3: Roofing Challenge

**1. Sources:** site Task 3 page and "intro" [S1, S2]. The page lists "Leaf Area Calculation Sheets" and "Roof Design Sheets". [Conflict] Neither exists, and the Task 3 folder is empty.

Marc's plan [Source: S8]: "We learned that the material isn't good as a roof. It's collapsing. We need [to] cover [the] roofs with special leaves. Square shaped, 2x2. They need to calculate how much area of leaves are available from another grid. They need to recalculate how much surface area their roofs use. And then they need to figure out in the end how many buildings they have."

- [Conflict] No "another grid" of leaves exists. The objective says "Determine the quantity available"; there is nothing to determine it from.
- The site says "shaped like 2x2 squares" with no unit. The chat says "2x2 meters".
- No source says which part of a tile building is its roof.

**2. Skill, and what is above Grade 3**

- Strand: Measurement Quest 4, Level 2. The page says: "There is no IXL practice that can prepare you for something like this!"
- **Above Grade 3:** dividing an area by 4 when it does not divide (25 ÷ 4 = 6.25); rounding up a remainder for a real purpose; covering triangles with squares.

**3. Time [Estimate]:** 40–50 minutes, uncertain for the same reason as Task 2.

**4. Steps and typing:** site: 3 steps. Answers: leaves per roof for 4 buildings; a roof design (a drawing); a written strategy. Typed: one number per roof.

**5. Reading**

- Page: about 350 words. 15 of 29 sentences are over 10 words.
- Longest instruction (16 words): "Calculate Leaf Coverage: Work out how many leaves are needed to cover each building's roof completely."
- Longest sentence (25 words): "Your strategic approach has not only ensured the stability of our colony's buildings but also laid the groundwork for a flourishing new world on Xylophora."
- Hard words: unsuitable, recalculate, incorporate, precision, rationale, ingenious, adaptability.

**6. Maths check** (`checks/xylophora_t3_check.py`, Part A)

One leaf is 2 × 2 = 4 m².

| Tile | Area | ÷ 4 | Leaves if cut and rounded up |
|---|---|---|---|
| S | 25 | 6.25 | 7 |
| BT | 25 | 6.25 | 7 |
| MT | 12.5 | 3.125 | 4 |
| ST | 10 | 2.5 | 3 |

- **No tile gives a whole number of leaves.**
- If leaves may not be cut, a 5 × 5 square needs **9** (three rows of three, hanging over the edge). The script shows 9 is the fewest when the leaves are laid straight, in line with the roof's edges.
- So one square roof has two defensible answers, **7 or 9**. 5 is odd and a leaf is 2 wide, so leaves never fit a square exactly.
- Squares cannot cover a triangle exactly at all.
- The 135 m² sample building: 135 ÷ 4 = 33.75, so 34 leaves by area.

**7. Checkpoints**

- Leaves per roof: **one answer**, once roofs are shapes that leaves fit.
- Roof design: **teacher-confirmed** (a drawing), or **hands-on result** if real paper leaves are laid on a real roof plan.

**8. Per-student numbers** [Proposal]

- The original cannot be checked: it has two right answers per square and no leaf supply.
- Rule tested: each student gets four rectangular roofs with even sides (4, 6, 8 or 10 m). Leaves = (width ÷ 2) × (length ÷ 2).
- **Result: 40,000 of 40,000 roofs have exactly one answer, the same by area and by laying whole leaves.** The most on one roof is 25. Every product is inside the 5 × 5 facts.

**9. Hands-on:** group **A** as written. It could be **B** if students lay paper leaves; no source describes that.

**10. Verdict: redo.** The twist is good story: the skin fails as a roof, and the square leaves save the colony. The numbers cannot work as they are.
