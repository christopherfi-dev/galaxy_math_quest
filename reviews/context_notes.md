# Context notes for the final summary

**Date:** 2026-11-16
**What this is:** short, factual notes from the top-level and planning documents. No task verdicts. No student names or stories about single children.

**Tags:** **[Source]** = found in Marc's material. **[Proposal]** = my reading or suggestion. **[Conflict]** = sources disagree.

## The ten things most worth knowing

1. **A real school curriculum sits behind the strand docs.** Marc fed "our entire scope and sequence for third grade" to ChatGPT. The strand docs are the AI's re-write, not the school document. [Source: Edutopia draft]
2. **The strand docs contain Grade 4 and above.** The doc itself labels some of it "Grade 4". Details in §1.3.
3. **The Measurement quests were re-ordered.** A later doc makes time Quest 1. That is why Chronosia is Metrica Quest #1 on the site. [Source]
4. **"Quest 2 in the Numbers Universe" is most likely "Basic Addition and Subtraction".** It was never built. [Proposal, from three sources that agree]
5. **Quests were written during the year, one step ahead of the class.** File dates run from July to December 2023. [Source for the dates]
6. **Station first, then the galaxies.** Then students moved at different speeds. [Source: Edutopia draft, class level]
7. **Group play is in the design.** Scenario cards were printed in "4 sets". The Trade Quest is described with "teams". Two station tasks say "you may work as a two-person team". [Source]
8. **The material was used again in 2024–25.** Station sheets carry "printed 07/10/24" and many files were edited from September 2024 to April 2025. [Source for the dates; Proposal for "used again"]
9. **No source states how many minutes a task took in class.** The only minute figures are AI estimates in unrun drafts and a 2026 plan that gives maths about 80 minutes a day. [Source]
10. **The cards were the main motivator, in Marc's words.** [Source: Edutopia draft]

## 3. The Edutopia draft

"Edutopia - Galaxy Math Quest: Revolutionizing Math Education Through Gamification" (id `1w98b3d9gVVmtHsVHRaZJl0mwu_6B9tcffFmQyAxNo50`). Made 2024-09-27, last edited 2024-10-10. It is outside the main folder. [Source]

**Privacy note.** The draft opens and closes with two named third-graders, one who struggles and one who is advanced. The names may be invented, but I have left them out. The points below are at class level only.

### 3.1 How GMQ came about [Source]
- Marc wanted maths that was more engaging and relevant for a class with a wide range of ability.
- He "took our entire scope and sequence for third grade, put it into ChatGPT to help me organize it better, and then created an adventure math game".
- Each foundational skill was meant to build on earlier ones. Students do "skill-based project quests in order to receive rewards".
- His two aims: games make using maths more fun; and, more important to him, advanced students get constant challenge while others apply new skills in "real-life scenarios, albeit in a fantasy space adventure".
- He divides the curriculum into "five main universes": Numbers, Data Handling, Shapes and Space, Measurement, Patterns.

### 3.2 How the class ran [Source]
- **Order:** the "initial 'Space Station' quests" came first. Quests "in different universes" came after.
- **Pace:** students were at different points. The fastest finished new quests "as quickly as I could make them". So quests were being written during the year.
- **Groups:** the Trade Quest is described as a class that "buzzes with teams debating moves, comparing profits". It ran in "trading rounds".
- **Peer help:** a fast finisher went on to help classmates through "the trickier quests".
- **Free time:** students chose to do maths quests in free time.
- **Time:** the draft gives no minutes, no lessons per week and no number of sessions per task.
- **Length of the run:** results are reported "after seven months of implementation".
- **Class size:** "20 students".

### 3.3 What worked [Source]
- An average rise of 1.4 grade levels in maths on the IXL assessment, across 20 students.
- More enthusiasm for maths.
- Better problem solving, "particularly in applying math to new situations".
- **The card game** is called "one of the main motivating factors". Cards are drawn at random after each quest, from Common to Epic. Students keep their own decks and battle each other, which uses more maths.
- **Scenario cards** in the Trade Quest kept students adapting. A Galactic Festival might double profits. Space Pirates could wreck a planned route.
- **Small early wins** on the station tasks, plus a lucky rare card, helped a hesitant learner try harder quests.
- The Trade Quest turned rounding "from a mechanical exercise" into decisions about risk and return.

### 3.4 What was hard [Source]
- A hesitant learner "tended to give up when dealing with word problems or things that seemed difficult".
- Some quests were "trickier" and students needed help through them. The draft does not say which.
- The teacher could not write quests as fast as the quickest student finished them.
- The draft names no failed task and no task that took too long.

### 3.5 The Trade Quest as the draft describes it [Source]
- Start with 50 fuel tokens.
- Round travel distances to the nearest 10 light-years. Round profits to the nearest 100 Galaxy Gems.
- Re-invest Galaxy Gems in fuel to reach further.
- The Voltara agent should compare these numbers with the site and the Drive docs.

### 3.6 What Marc wanted next (written in 2024) [Source]
An interactive app or web game with: concept-based adventures; standards-aligned progression; "adaptive challenges using AI"; "complex, multi-step problems"; "collaborative learning through multiplayer challenges".

### 3.7 Cautions [Proposal]
- This is a draft article meant to persuade. It reports successes. Treat it as Marc's account, not as a record.
- "Seven months" and "20 students" carry no dates. The draft was written in autumn 2024, so they most likely describe 2023–24.
