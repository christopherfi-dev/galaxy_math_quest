# Review — Galaxy Databa, Operation Stardust (Quest 1: Prepping for a Year-Long Space Adventure)

**Date:** 2026-11-16
**Stage:** review and verdict only. Nothing is redesigned here.
**Verdicts are provisional** until Marc fills in `INFO_FROM_MARC.md`.

**Summary.** Operation Stardust is listed as two tasks, but it is really **six lessons**: two levels of food planning and four levels of graphing. Each level is a full class period or more. Every sum printed on the site is correct. The problems are size, rules that are not defined, and source versions that disagree. **Task 1 Level 2, the "Eating Schedule", is almost certainly the task Marc found too long and too hard.** It asks a child to fill four tables (months 1–3, 4–6, 7–9, 10–12) so that each reaches 360 energy points, while obeying shelf life, balance and "no waste" at once. A plan that follows every rule takes at least 20 multiplications and 16 additions and about 84 written cells, and some products are 2-digit × 1-digit. It has no single right answer, so a child cannot tell when it is finished. Task 2 is four drawn graphs. Code can check values read from the data; only a teacher can check a drawing. In Task 2 the site and the printed Drive sheets give different numbers for Levels 3 and 4.

**Assumptions used [Assumption]:** one task = one 40-minute period; at most 3 numbers or one short sentence per typed answer; reading at most 10 words per sentence.

### Level 2 — Eating Schedule

**This is the task Marc most likely meant** by the "ship supplies / inventory" task: a list of supplies for a 12-month trip, in four tables.

**1. Sources.** S2, S4/S9, S7, S8, S10, S18. Versions:
- **S7 draft:** "select food items whose combined Energy Score … meets or exceeds 1440 points", in lists and tables.
- **S10 chat:** an older energy table with scores 1 to 5 for ten foods. Marc wrote that "the energy possibilities dind't seem to equal 1440". The printed sheet has new scores out of 10.
- **Site and S8 (what the class saw):** four 3-month tables, "Energy needed for three months - 360 total points".

**2. Skill.** Data Quest 2, Level 2: "Create displays using lists and tables" [Source: S17]. In practice the load is **multiplication and addition**, not data handling.
- Above Grade 3: 12 × 4 × 30 = 1440 (the page gives the answer, so the child need not do it); 0.5-month shelf lives; products with a count above 10, such as 14 × 6.

**3. Time.** About **75 minutes** (60–90) for a plan that follows every rule. A child who stops at the page's example size would finish in 15 minutes, but with 46 points of the 360 needed.

**4. Steps: the exact instructions and numbers.**
- Given: 12 months × 4 crew × 30 energy points a month = **1440**, "or **360** energy points every 3 months".
- Do: split the year into four 3-month parts. For each part pick foods "based on their shelf life". For each food write its energy score, how many units, and the product. Add the products.
- Also: "Try to make a balanced diet. Select foods with higher nutritional scores." and "Don't be Wasteful - Use up all of the food on our list." Then "Combine all your lists."
- The worksheet has **four tables of 10 rows**, each with four columns.

**Count of calculations** [recomputed, `stardust_t1_check.py` PART 3]

| Plan | Multiplications | Additions | Cells written |
|---|---|---|---|
| Every food used once, 5 foods per part (the smallest plan that obeys every rule) | 20 | 16 | 84 |
| All 40 printed rows filled | 40 | 36 | 164 |

- That is **20 foods × 4 parts of the year**, not 20 × 12 months. There are no prices.
- **Size of the numbers:** energy scores 4 to 10; counts of about 10 to 14 units per food; products 50 to 100; column totals to exactly 360; year total 1440. About 54 units per part, about 215 units for the year.
- **Counts above 10 cannot be avoided in months 1–3.** The four fresh foods must be eaten then. With one more food and counts of 10 or less, the most a child can reach is 300. So that part needs sums like 14 × 6 = 84, or more than five foods.
- **Typing, if moved into the world as written:** 20 or more food names and 20 or more counts. Far over the limit.

**Exactly why it is long and hard**
1. **It is a search, not a sum.** With only three foods there are already 237 different ways to make exactly 360. The child must keep trying counts until the column lands on 360, then redo rows when it does not.
2. **Four rules at once:** reach the energy target, respect shelf life, balance nutrition, waste nothing.
3. **One rule cannot be followed.** "Use up all of the food on our list": the list gives no amounts, so nothing can be "used up".
4. **The target is not clear.** Exactly 360, or at least 360? The draft says "meets or exceeds". The worksheet says "Energy needed … 360".
5. **No finish line.** There is no single right answer and no way for the child to check. The work goes to the teacher.
6. **The example hides the size.** It shows two foods adding to 46 points. A full part needs 360.
7. **Volume.** At least 84 cells by hand, four times over the same kind of table.
8. **It comes straight after Level 1,** which is another 50 minutes on the same 20 foods.

**5. Reading.**
- 488 words. 20 of 37 sentences are over 10 words. Mean 12.8 words.
- Longest instruction (29 words): "Get to know your food: Study the Food Info Sheet, noting how long each food lasts (shelf life), how much energy it provides (energy score), and its nutritional value."
- Hard words: consume, efficient, voyage, consumption schedule, corresponding, segments, nutritional, finalize.
- The page calls the same sheet "Food Supplies Data Sheet", "Food Info Sheet" and "Food list".

**6. Maths check.** Everything printed is right.
- 12 × 4 × 30 = 1440 ✓. 3 × 4 × 30 = 360 ✓. 4 × 360 = 1440 ✓.
- Site example: 5 × 6 = 30 ✓ (Fresh Milk); 4 × 4 = 16 ✓ (Fresh Spinach).
- Worksheet example: Brown Rice 6 × 10 = 60 ✓, in months 4–6; its shelf life is 6 months ✓.
- Foods that last long enough: months 1–3: all 20; months 4–6: 16; months 7–9: 12; months 10–12: 9.
- A full plan exists. The script prints one with every food used once and each part exactly 360.

**7. Checkpoints.**
| Step | Kind |
|---|---|
| Energy needed for 3 months | **one answer** |
| Each 3-month table | **conditions**: every food lasts long enough; the products add to the target |
| "Balanced diet", "not wasteful" | teacher-confirmed (not defined in numbers) |

**8. Per-student numbers.** They can vary, and they can be kept inside Grade 3.
- **Rule L2 [Proposal].** Crew of 2 to 5. Each person needs 10 energy points a month. One month = crew × 10. One 3-month part = that × 3, so the target is 60, 90, 120 or 150. For each part the world offers 3 of Marc's foods that last long enough; the first part always includes a fresh food. The student types 3 counts, each 1 to 10. The check: the three products add to the target.
- **Test:** 10,000 seeds, **0 failures**; at most 3 redraws for one student; 10,000 different puzzles. Largest product 100, largest target 150, year total at most 600. Each part has between 1 and 73 right answers (median 8), so it is a conditions checkpoint.
- **Size after the rule:** 22 calculations for the whole year, 3 numbers typed per answer.
- What it gives up from Marc's version: the crew's 30 points a month, the 360 target and the free choice among all 20 foods.

**9. Hands-on.** Group **A** [Source: handoff §6].

**10. Verdict: shorten (close to redo).**
- The story, the foods, the scores and the "fresh food first" idea all work and should stay.
- The size has to come down by about three quarters to fit a period and the typing limit.
- "Use up all the food" must be dropped or given amounts. "Exactly or at least" must be stated.
- If Marc wants the full four tables kept on paper, the honest label is two periods.

### What a typed world can check, and what it cannot (all four levels)

| Code can check (typed answer) | Needs paper, a photo or a teacher |
|---|---|

| A total of two or three values | Whether the graph is neat and accurate |
| A comparison between two data sets ("Did we eat more grains or fewer?") | The written reflection ("why do you think…") |
| The "strange" month (most fuel and shortest distance) | The video |

## What a child could explore or play with here

All from Marc's own text unless marked.

- **The commanders' own ship, room by room** [Voltara Mission Control Data Sheet]: Living Quarters (23 °C), Engine Room (30 °C), Food Storage (4 °C, a cold room), Greenhouse (26 °C), Control Room (22 °C).
- **The fuel system:** Main Thrusters, Backup Thrusters, Emergency Reserve.
- **The food store:** 20 named foods, some fresh and some that last three years. Fresh Milk and Fresh Spinach spoil in two weeks.
- **The Central Computer,** which "has been keeping tabs" on fuel and distance.
- **The Supply Chief,** the person the child reports to in Level 4.
- **Voltara Mission Control** and the "Voltara Space Agency", who ask for the graphs.
- **The mysterious signal.** "Maybe it's a new friend who needs our help. Or maybe it's a treasure waiting to be found!"
- **Space weather:** solar winds in April and a gravity assist from a planet in July.
- **Planet X-205,** where the ship touches down in Level 2. **Planet Nourish,** named once.
- **Draft only:** three paths to **Planet Zorgon**, with fuel stations, a stranded alien and collectible artifacts.
