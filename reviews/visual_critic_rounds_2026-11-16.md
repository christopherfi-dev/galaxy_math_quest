# Blind visual reviews of the sketch — 2026-11-16

**What this is:** a record of three blind reviews of `prototypes/kis_station_sketch.html`.
**Method:** from the "visual critic" idea in the Abyssal Garden brief (`reference/third-party/abyssal-garden/prompt.txt`).

---

## How it works

1. A script plays the sketch and saves the same 22 views each time: the station rooms, four walking frames, the battle, the cargo task, the space map, Voltara from orbit and on the ground, and three phone views.
2. A fresh agent gets only those pictures. It has never seen the code, the design notes or an earlier round.
3. It is told what the game is for and what you asked for, in your words: "feel like playing a game", "going somewhere", "movement and spatial awareness", "a real space galaxy", "amazing alien planets".
4. It returns defects ranked major, moderate or minor.
5. I fix what I can, render again, and send the new pictures to a new fresh agent.

The capture script is in the session's scratch folder, not the repo. The same idea can be re-run at any time.

## The three rounds

| Round | Major | Moderate | Minor | The critic's one-line verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 | 10 | 7 | "A tidy prototype that still reads as a worksheet with a small diagram on top. It is not yet a place a child travels through." |

| 3 | 9 | 13 | 6 | "The planet looks like a game, but the station still reads as a tidy diagram, and overlaps and cover-ups undercut the best moments." |

**Read the counts with care.** They did not fall. Each critic is new, so each one digs to a fresh level and finds a full list. The counts are not a score that can be compared between rounds. The verdicts are the better signal, and they moved.

**The loop has not finished.** The rule in the Abyssal Garden brief is to repeat until nothing above "minor" is left. The sketch is not there.

## What changed between rounds

**After round 1**
- The game view fills most of the screen. Panels sit on top of it.
- Every station object was redrawn. Doors have frames, sliding panels and lights.
- Signs and floor arrows show where each door leads.
- The astronaut has four facings, legs and arms that swing, and dust.
- One rocket design is used on the station, in space and on the planet.
- The asteroid battle is drawn outside the Shield Bay windows.
- The galaxy map has five different galaxy shapes, routes, planets and a ship trail.
- Voltara has mountains, floating islands, three coloured districts with meters, alien trees and a ringed planet in the sky.

**After round 2**
- The story text moved into its own band under the scene.
- Each room is framed to fill the view.
- Cargo Load is played by tapping crates in the room. Loaded crates stack on a big scale with a dial.
- The Launch Bay has a launch pad. The Shield Bay has a glowing emitter.
- The mystery floor bits were removed.
- Energy beams run from a tower to each district.

## What the round 3 critic said works

- Voltara's ground view is "rich and inviting".
- "The maths sits in the world": the rock grid, crates with ton labels on a scale with a gauge, and city lights that switch on.
- Exits are clear, with chevrons and named doors. The astronaut turns to face what it reads.
- The story line is large and short.

## Still open after round 3 (the critic's majors)

1. On a phone, the three district meters collide on Voltara.
2. The mission report covers the lift-off.
3. The tunnel and store are mostly black, and the astronaut stands upright in a crawl space.
4. The battle has no astronaut in view, and the rocks are small and still.
5. District meters show percent, while the problem is in energy units. Cables cross the labels.
6. On Voltara the rocket is astronaut-sized, and nothing shows where you can walk.
7. Voltara from orbit: the shadow edge is stepped, and the bands read as a beach ball.
8. In still frames the walk reads as a slide.
9. On a phone the station is too small to read.

Its top moderate points: black bands above and below rooms, ghost corners of neighbouring rooms, the station still plainer than the planet, labels piling up on the astronaut, and no ship in the Launch Bay.

---

## Two honest limits

- **Code-drawn art has a ceiling.** The planet got the best verdict because bright colour and big shapes suit it. A station drawn from above in rectangles will stay plainer without painted art.
- **A critic of still pictures cannot feel play.** It cannot tell that a crate slides or that a robot follows you. It is a check on how the game looks, not on whether it is fun. Children are the test for that.
