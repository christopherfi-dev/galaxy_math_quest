# Review: Planet Chronosia (Galaxy Metrica, Quest 1)

**Date:** 2026-11-16
**Reviewer:** review agent for Galaxy Metrica
**Stage:** review and verdict only. Nothing here is a redesign.

**Summary.** Chronosia has four tasks about time, and all four were printed for class in October 2023. The arithmetic in Marc's printed sheets is right: I found no wrong number in any of them. The problems are of other kinds. Two prints of Task 1 show a different Clock 8. Two of Task 3's five exam questions have tied answers, and a third needs 21 hours 15 minutes across midnight. Task 4 takes about two periods and contains almost no time maths: the timetable's three clashes never touch any one person's events, so the only number work is "60 is more than 45". Task 2 is the cleanest task and can go into the world nearly as it is. Chronosia is group A (handoff §6): the paper was only the delivery, so nothing needs to stay hands-on.

**Marks used:** [Source] found in Marc's material. [Conflict] his sources disagree, or a source has an error. [Proposal] my suggestion. [Assumption] a stated assumption from the brief. [Estimate] my own time estimate, not measured.

- [Conflict] A student holding both prints sees Clock 8 as 0:00 on one and 00:90 on the other. The other seven clocks agree: 3:67, 12:15, 14:75, 9:42, 25:00, 7:99, 5:30.

**2. Skill**

- Strand: Timely Transitions, Level 1 (AM and PM).
- What the task really tests: minutes past 59 roll into the next hour, and 12-hour against 24-hour time. The page's own learning objective says "Understand the 24-hour and 12-hour time systems and convert between them."
- **Above the base strand:** [Source] " Updated Quests - Measurement Universe." puts "Understand and compare the 12-hour and 24-hour time systems" in **Advanced Quest 3**, not in the Grade 3 quest. Three of the eight clocks use hours a 12-hour clock does not have (14:75, 25:00, and 0:00 or 00:90).
- The valid times are to the minute (9:42) and to 5 minutes (12:15, 5:30).
- No AM-to-PM conversion is asked, although Level 1 is about AM and PM.

**3. Time [Estimate]**

- Video: 3 min 48 s [Source: S10]. IXL practice (QQ1–QQ3, clock flashcards) comes before and is not counted.
- Checklist, 8 clocks: about 10–15 minutes.
- Restoration Report: 33 blanks, most of them writing. About 30 minutes for a Grade 3 writer.
- **Total about 45 minutes on paper.** Slightly over the 40-minute assumption, and two thirds of it is the report, not the maths.

**4. Steps and typing**

- Site: 5 mission steps.
- Answers: 8 corrected times and 8 ticks on the checklist. On the Clocks.pdf version 5 clocks need changing; on the checklist version 4.
- Restoration Report blanks: name, date (2); clocks fixed, hardest, easiest (3); for each of 8 clocks the first time, the corrected time and "Challenges Faced" (24); 3 reflection questions; signature. **33 blanks.**
- Typed world: each clock is one time, which is 2 numbers. Within the limit. The reflections are sentences.

**5. Reading**

- Page: about 370 words. 18 of 31 sentences are over 10 words.
- Longest instruction (22 words): "Check the Gears: For each corrected time, mark a checkbox on the worksheet to signify unlocking a gear in the clock tower."
- Hard words: malfunctioning, disrupted, plausible, distorted, signify, summarizing, reasoning, diligence, calibrate.
- The checklist itself is easy to read: "Displayed Time", "Corrected Time".

**9. Hands-on:** group **A**. The prints only carried the numbers. No analog clock is used, although the learning objective names one.

**10. Verdict: fix.** The core (broken clocks, gears unlocking) is sound, quick and checkable. Fix: pick one Clock 8; state the roll-over rule in the briefing; Marc decides whether 24-hour clocks stay; cut the report to what a teacher wants to read.
