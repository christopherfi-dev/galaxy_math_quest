#### Plan B: the three levels (D4)

**Level 1: Exploration of 2D Shapes.** Objective: "Explore and discover various 2D shapes, both regular and irregular, in an alien landscape, understanding their unique characteristics."

1. Introduce Shapes. Show pictures of regular and irregular shapes. Talk about "sides, angles, symmetry".
2. Shape Exploration. Students explore "alien landscapes (either real or simulated)" to find 2D shapes. The teacher is to "Create an environment that represents an alien landscape."
3. Shape Art Creation. "Create an alien masterpiece using 2D shapes", with art supplies or digital tools.
4. Reflection and Sharing. Students explain their art and the shapes in it.

- Materials: "2D shapes (cutouts or digital)", "Art supplies or digital design tools".
- Assessment: the art, and "written descriptions".

**Level 2: Comparing and Contrasting Shapes.** Objective: "Master the distinction between various 2D shapes by comparing, contrasting, and sorting."

1. Review Shapes from Level 1.
2. Shape Sorting Activity. "Classify different 2D shapes based on properties." Cut-outs. Pairs or small groups.
3. Shape Comparison Chart. "Create a chart that compares and contrasts different 2D shapes."
4. Reflect and Share.

- Materials: "Assorted 2D shapes (cutouts or digital)", "Paper and pen for charting".
- Assessment: "sorting accuracy", and the charts.

Then comes the one line Marc typed: **"2) for the comparsion chart, what should the different columns be?"** The reply lists nine columns: [Source: D4]

1. Shape Name
2. Number of Sides
3. Number of Angles ("which will typically match the number of sides")
4. Symmetry ("line symmetry, rotational symmetry")
5. Regular or Irregular
6. Length of Sides ("all equal, opposite sides equal")
7. Type of Angles ("all right angles, acute angles")
8. Interior Angles Sum
9. Visual Representation

**"Level 3 -"** (no name). It has two parts.

- "Explore 2D Shapes":
  1. Introduction to Basic Shapes. "Shapes Covered: Circles, triangles, quadrilaterals (squares, rectangles, parallelograms, trapezoids), pentagons, hexagons, and up to decagons." "Properties Explained: Sides, vertices, angles, symmetry." Posters. Students handle cut-outs.
  2. Sorting and Classifying Shapes. Small groups. Students say their reasons.
  3. Shapes in the Real World. Find real objects in the classroom or in pictures. Link to "art, design, and architecture".
- "Demonstrate a Complex Shape":
  1. Choose a Complex Shape. "Examples: Star, complex building design, intricate pattern."
  2. Break it down into simpler 2D shapes.
  3. Students make their own complex shapes.
  4. Reflect and Share.
