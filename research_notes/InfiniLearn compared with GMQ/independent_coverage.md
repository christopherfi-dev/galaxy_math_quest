# Independent coverage of InfiniLearn (infinilearn.com), as of 17 November 2026

**Read this first.**

- All checks were made on 17 November 2026.
- The product spells itself "Infinilearn". It is **not** Infinity Learn by Sri Chaitanya.
- **The premise needs correcting.** Infinilearn is not an AI-generated learning adventure. Today it is a hand-built, Prodigy-style multiplayer math RPG for grades 3 to 9. Its current website does not mention AI at all.
- **Almost nothing independent exists.** I found no teacher blog post, no Reddit thread, no YouTube walk-through by an outsider, no press article, no study and no Common Sense or EdSurge entry. The one outside article is an interview on the site of the game framework it is built with. Nearly every number below comes from the maker.
- Tags used: **[Maker]** = only the maker says so. **[Independent]** = a third party or a public record. **[Directory]** = an auto-listing site that repeats maker text. **[Conflict]** = sources disagree. **[Not opened]** = I saw a search snippet but could not open the page.

How hard I looked: about 12 web searches (exact domain, name in quotes, with review, reddit, teachers, founder, Prodigy alternative, Common Sense, EdSurge, LinkedIn, YouTube, X, TikTok), about 20 page fetches, the Hacker News search API, the Verisign domain record, the Apple lookup API, the Steam API and the Wayback availability API.

## 1. Who built it? When did it launch? How big is the company?

### Takeaway
Infinilearn is a solo-founder project by Adrian Martinez, a student from Orange County, California, who is now a first-year student at UC Berkeley. The domain dates from March 2024, the product changed direction several times, and the current math RPG appears to date from 2025. I found no sign of outside funding or of any staff.

### Cited Findings
- **[Maker]** The About page says: "I am Adrian Martinez, a high school student in Orange County, California." It names no one else. It gives no launch date, no funding and no legal company name. — [Infinilearn About](https://www.infinilearn.com/about)
- **[Independent, but based on the founder's own words]** An interview dated 7 October 2026 says Martinez is now a freshman at UC Berkeley. He started with Minecraft servers and mods at age 11. — [Phaser news interview](https://phaser.io/news/2026/10/infinilearn-phaser-math-rpg-interview)

- **[Maker]** The security page says: "Production access is limited to the founder. Any future employee or contractor receives least-privilege access…". This reads as one person with no employees. — [Infinilearn Security](https://www.infinilearn.com/security)

### Inferences
- The timeline that fits the records is:
  1. March 2024: domain registered.
  2. September 2024: an AI "personalized learning companion" (Product Hunt).
  3. December 2024: a learning management system, "canvas but better" (Hacker News).
  4. 2025: the math RPG (Steam listing, then the iOS app in September 2025).
  5. 2026: the browser game with teacher and parent dashboards.
- This matches the founder's "pivoted four times". The math RPG is therefore roughly one to two years old, not a mature product.
- It is a solo project, or very close to one. "Infinilearn, Inc" suggests a registered company, but I did not check a state registry.
- The "in collaboration with Meta" claim from December 2024 has no support anywhere else I looked. Treat it as unverified. It also shows the maker has used inflated language before ("our nations most advanced education platform", with 1 point on Hacker News). That is a reason to be careful with his other numbers.
- AI-tool directories still describe the September 2024 product (an AI companion for homeschoolers). That is why searches make it look like an AI product. It no longer is one.

### Gaps
- **No funding information.** I found no investor, grant, accelerator or Crunchbase-style record. I did not search paid databases.
- **No LinkedIn or X material read.** Product Hunt links to social profiles under the handle "theadrianm". I did not open them, and my searches returned no LinkedIn, X, TikTok or YouTube results for the product.
- **Wayback pages could not be opened.** The snapshots exist, but web.archive.org refused connections from this environment. So the pivot timeline rests on Product Hunt, Hacker News and Steam, not on the archived pages themselves.
- **Company registration not checked.** I did not look up "Infinilearn, Inc" in the California or Delaware registries.
- **Why the iOS app was removed is unknown.** I only saw the US store. Other countries were not checked.
- **Exact launch date of the current math RPG is unknown.**

The maker's numbers have climbed very fast (10,000+ on Steam, 50,000 in October 2026, 100,000+ in November 2026), and none of them can be checked from outside. ### Inferences
- **Growth check.** If the interview numbers were current on 7 October 2026, signups doubled from about 50,000 to 100,000+ in about six weeks, and teachers rose from about 3,300 to 5,600+. - **"Signups" is not "active users".** A student account needs only a first name and a grade, with no email. Accounts are therefore cheap to create and easy to duplicate. A signup count says little about how many children play each week.

- The heavy blog output means that web searches for reviews of Infinilearn mostly return Infinilearn's own pages. That makes the product look more discussed than it is.
- With no iOS app in the US store and no Android app found, the product is in practice a browser game. It is pitched at Chromebooks. I did not test it on an iPad.

## 3. What do reviewers and users praise or complain about?

### Takeaway
There are no independent reviews to report. The only third-party review anywhere is one six-word Product Hunt comment about an earlier version of the product. All detailed praise is on the maker's own homepage, and I found no public complaints at all, which with this little coverage means "unknown", not "none".

### Cited Findings
- **[Independent]** The single Product Hunt review, from about two years ago (so about late 2024), reads in full: "Love the idea ! Keep going". The reviewer is Bilal Harouchi. It dates from the "Personalized Learning Companion" period, not the math RPG. — [Product Hunt reviews](https://www.producthunt.com/products/infinilearn/reviews)
- **[Maker]** The homepage shows five testimonials, described as "pulled straight from our inbox and comment sections". They are undated. The people are given as "Middle school math teacher, Grades 6 to 8", "Bissan A., Grade 6 math teacher", "Angela G., Classroom teacher", "Jill H., Teacher and parent" and "Dave E., Grade 5 teacher". No school is named. — [Infinilearn homepage](https://www.infinilearn.com/)
- **[Maker]** What the testimonials praise:
  - Engagement: students "all got HOOKED on Infinilearn and want to play whenever they have free time".
  - Comparison with Prodigy: "My students like this more than Prodigy."
  - Volume of practice: students "played at home for hours… completed 100-200 questions, all for fun".
  - The teacher data: "helps me see the gaps easily for planning and goal writing".

  — [Infinilearn homepage](https://www.infinilearn.com/)
- **[Maker]** All five testimonials are from Grade 5 to Grade 8 teachers. None is from a Grade 3 or Grade 4 teacher. — [Infinilearn homepage](https://www.infinilearn.com/)

- **[Maker]** The Terms say the service is provided "as is" with "no warranties, express or implied, regarding the reliability, accuracy, or availability of the Service". This is standard wording, but it covers accuracy. — [Terms of use](https://play.infinilearn.com/terms?lang=en)
- **[Conflict, inside the maker's own material]** Grade range differs by page:
  - Homepage and teacher page: grades 3 to 9.
  - Parent page: "grades 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, and 9".
  - Steam and the October 2026 interview: grades 4 to 9.
  - The blog calls itself a "Math Games Blog for Middle School".
  — [Homepage](https://www.infinilearn.com/); [Parents page](https://www.infinilearn.com/parents); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3513130/Infinilearn_Math_Game); [Blog](https://www.infinilearn.com/blog)
- **[Conflict, inside the maker's own material]** A maker blog post dated 28 March 2026 says Infinilearn has "No premium tier, no paywall on content or rewards". The site now sells Premium at $9.99 a month or $59.99 a year. — [Blog post, 28 March 2026](https://www.infinilearn.com/blog/math-rpg-games); [Homepage](https://www.infinilearn.com/)
- **[Maker]** Premium adds parent insight and cosmetics: "Fantasy hair colors, custom username, Finny pet, gold name tag, premium emotes, and chat lines". The math content stays free. — [Parents page](https://www.infinilearn.com/parents)
- **[Directory] [Not opened]** Search snippets show directories giving other grade ranges (6 to 8, 5 to 9, 6 to 12) and calling it "AI-powered". These sites repeat old maker text and are not reviews. — [G2 listing](https://www.g2.com/products/infinilearn/reviews); [topai.tools listing](https://topai.tools/t/infinilearn)

### Inferences
- **Very few reviews is the finding.** One short review in two years, of a different version of the product. The praise on the homepage cannot be traced to a person or a school.
- **Grade 3 is the edge of the product.** The centre of gravity is grades 6 to 8. Grade 3 seems to have been added recently, and the parent page has not caught up. For a Grade 3 class, the content at that level is the least proven part.
- **A paid tier arrived between late March 2026 and August 2026.** The privacy policy dated 6 August 2026 already mentions Stripe billing. The founder's stated line is that Premium never locks math. That is the same promise Prodigy made, and Prodigy was criticised for how memberships were sold to children (see section 6). Visible cosmetics such as a "gold name tag" in a shared world are the kind of feature that complaint was about. I did not see how, or whether, Infinilearn advertises Premium to children inside the game.
- **Reliability is a stated risk.** One person runs the servers and says they are the weakest part.

### Gaps
- **No complaints found.** No bug reports, outage reports, billing complaints or safety complaints in any search. With this little coverage, I cannot tell a good record from an unseen one.
- **I did not play the game.** Creating an account was out of scope. So I cannot describe the questions, the hints, or what happens after a wrong answer.
- **G2, topai.tools and neura.market blocked my fetches** (HTTP 403). I do not know whether G2 has any real reviews. The search snippet suggested none.

## 4. Is there any evidence of learning outcomes?

### Takeaway
No. There is no study, no named pilot and no published data. There are only usage counts and anecdotes from the maker.

### Cited Findings
- **[Maker]** The About page cites no research, pilot or efficacy data. — [Infinilearn About](https://www.infinilearn.com/about)
- **[Maker]** The Steam page says "Piloting in public school classrooms with real results: 400,000+ problems solved, 78% accuracy." These are usage figures. They are not learning gains. No school is named. — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3513130/Infinilearn_Math_Game)
- **[Maker, via interview]** "Over 2 million math problems solved." Again a usage figure. — [Phaser interview](https://phaser.io/news/2026/10/infinilearn-phaser-math-rpg-interview)
- **[Maker]** One unnamed teacher testimonial says: "After a month they have all significantly improved in various areas." No measure is given. — [Infinilearn homepage](https://www.infinilearn.com/)
- **[Maker]** A maker blog post says students who play math RPGs solve "3-5x more problems per session" than with worksheets. It gives no source. — [Blog post, 28 March 2026](https://www.infinilearn.com/blog/math-rpg-games)
- **[Maker]** The teacher page answers "Can students just guess?" with: "The game tracks accuracy by topic over time, so repeated guessing shows up in the data and triggers more practice on weak areas." — [Teachers page](https://www.infinilearn.com/teachers)
- **[Maker]** The Terms call it "a supplemental educational tool… not a substitute for classroom instruction". — [Terms of use](https://play.infinilearn.com/terms?lang=en)
- **[Independent, for context]** The much larger rival Prodigy was challenged on exactly this point. In February 2021, 22 groups told the US Federal Trade Commission that Prodigy's claims of better grades and test scores lacked evidence. Prodigy pointed to a Johns Hopkins report covering two schools. — [Education Week, 23 February 2021](https://www.edweek.org/technology/popular-interactive-math-game-prodigy-is-target-of-complaint-to-federal-trade-commission/2021/02)

### Inferences
- "Problems solved" and "78% accuracy" tell you children played and mostly got questions right. They do not show that anyone learned something new. A child practising what they already know also scores 78%.
- The product is practice, not teaching. I saw no claim of lessons, worked examples or hints for students. The "topic explanations" in Premium are for parents.
- **What to check that could prove the engagement claim wrong:** what share of play time is math versus exploring, trading and decorating. The Prodigy complaint counted exactly this.
- A product this young, run by one student, would not be expected to have a study yet. The absence is normal for its age. It still means the learning claim is unproven.

### Gaps
- **No independent study, evaluation or pilot report found** anywhere.
- **No information on how questions are written or checked.** See section 5.
- **I could not see what feedback a child gets after a wrong answer.**

## 5. Do the concerns about AI in children's products apply here?

### Takeaway
Mostly not in the usual way, because the game does not appear to use generative AI with children: no chatbot, no generated story, no typed chat at all. The live concerns are different: nobody outside can see how the math questions are made or checked, the privacy and safety claims are self-declared by a one-person operator, and children can sign themselves up with no adult involved.

### Cited Findings

**AI use**
- **[Independent check of maker pages]** I counted mentions of "AI", "artificial intelligence", "machine learning" and "GPT" on seven current maker pages (home, security, teachers, parents, premium, homeschool, accessibility). The count was zero on every page. — [Homepage](https://www.infinilearn.com/); [Security](https://www.infinilearn.com/security); [Teachers](https://www.infinilearn.com/teachers); [Parents](https://www.infinilearn.com/parents)
- **[Maker]** The Steam page's AI disclosure says: "Infinilearn uses artificial intelligence-based input moderation to ensure appropriate educational interactions." — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3513130/Infinilearn_Math_Game)
- **[Maker]** The privacy policy describes this as username screening. A proposed username "is automatically checked for appropriateness", and "Only the proposed name itself is shared with our screening provider". The provider is not named. — [Privacy policy](https://play.infinilearn.com/privacy?lang=en)
- **[Maker, via interview]** "All of our in-game art is made by real artists, not AI." — [Phaser interview](https://phaser.io/news/2026/10/infinilearn-phaser-math-rpg-interview)
- **[Maker]** The teacher page describes the adaptivity in plain terms: "A built-in diagnostic finds each student's level as they play, then keeps adapting, easing below grade level when a student needs it." — [Teachers page](https://www.infinilearn.com/teachers)

**Accuracy of the math**
- **[Maker]** Steam says "All math problems aligned to state standards". The teacher page lists Common Core, seventeen US state versions, the Australian Curriculum and the Ontario Curriculum. — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3513130/Infinilearn_Math_Game); [Teachers page](https://www.infinilearn.com/teachers)
- **[Maker, via interview]** On how questions are made or checked, the interview says only that answers "have to be validated" during multiplayer battles. — [Phaser interview](https://phaser.io/news/2026/10/infinilearn-phaser-math-rpg-interview)

**Safety**
- **[Maker]** "No open chat. Players talk through preset phrases and emotes only. Parents and teachers can switch multiplayer off entirely." — [Security](https://www.infinilearn.com/security)
- **[Maker]** "No ads, ever." "We never sell student data." — [Security](https://www.infinilearn.com/security)
- **[Maker]** In multiplayer areas, "other players can see a student's username, character appearance, and level". — [Privacy policy](https://play.infinilearn.com/privacy?lang=en)

**Data from under-13s**
- **[Maker]** The privacy policy was last updated on 6 August 2026. The Terms were last updated on 21 September 2026. — [Privacy policy](https://play.infinilearn.com/privacy?lang=en); [Terms of use](https://play.infinilearn.com/terms?lang=en)
- **[Maker]** The game "is designed for students in grades 3-9 (typically ages 8-15)". — [Privacy policy](https://play.infinilearn.com/privacy?lang=en)
- **[Maker]** A child can sign up alone: "A student who signs up on their own provides only a first name, a grade level, and an auto-generated username and PIN, none of which is personal information as defined by COPPA." Parental consent "is established when a parent creates or links their child's account". When a teacher creates accounts, "the school consents on parents' behalf". — [Privacy policy](https://play.infinilearn.com/privacy?lang=en)
- **[Maker]** Data kept on each student includes "math problems attempted, accuracy per topic, time per problem, and mastery levels", play time, and "a coarse region (country and state)". — [Privacy policy](https://play.infinilearn.com/privacy?lang=en)
- **[Maker]** "We comply with FERPA, COPPA, and applicable state student privacy laws." The page says it is "Aligned with NIST CSF 2.0" and offers signed data sharing agreements. — [Security](https://www.infinilearn.com/security)
- **[Maker]** "Independent security researchers have tested Infinilearn from the outside… Every real finding… has been fixed." The researchers are not named, and no report is linked. — [Security](https://www.infinilearn.com/security)
- **[Maker]** The certifications named (SOC 2, ISO 27001) belong to Google Cloud and Vercel, "infrastructure holding SOC 2 and ISO 27001 certifications". They are not Infinilearn's own. — [Security](https://www.infinilearn.com/security)
- **[Conflict, minor]** The security page lists subprocessors as Google Cloud (Firebase), Vercel and Resend. The privacy policy also lists Stripe and an unnamed username-screening provider, and mentions "customer support via live chat" without naming a chat provider. — [Security](https://www.infinilearn.com/security); [Privacy policy](https://play.infinilearn.com/privacy?lang=en)
- **[Directory] [Not opened]** A BuiltWith search result for the domain appeared under a "Hotjar Usage Statistics" title. I did not open it. I found no Hotjar code in the current game app. Hotjar may have been used on an earlier version of the site, or the title may be generic. — [BuiltWith listing](https://builtwith.com/infinilearn.com)

### Inferences
- **Generative AI concerns (made-up facts, the AI giving the answer away, unsafe generated text) do not seem to apply to play.** There is no free text between the child and the system in either direction. This is the largest single difference from an AI-run world.
- **The accuracy question is still open, in a different form.** One person has produced questions for grades 3 to 9 across about twenty curriculum versions in about a year. The site does not say whether questions are hand-written, built from templates, or drafted with AI and then checked. The answer key has had no outside review that I could find. The only way to settle this is to sample questions at Grade 3 level and recompute them.
- **"No adult in the loop" does apply at signup.** A child can create an account alone and enter a shared multiplayer world before any parent or teacher is linked. The safeguard is preset chat only. Whether multiplayer is on by default for a self-registered child is not stated.
- **The COPPA position is the maker's own legal reading.** It rests on the claim that a first name, a grade and a generated username are not personal information. A school should check that with its own data protection lead. I am not giving a legal view.
- **KIS is in Thailand.** Every compliance statement is about US law (COPPA, FERPA, New York Education Law 2-d). The site says nothing about Thailand's data protection law or about GDPR. Data is stored on Google Cloud; the region is not stated.
- **One-person risk.** The founder alone has production access, writes the code, answers the security email and is now a full-time first-year university student. If he stops, the service and the data sit with one individual. This is a continuity risk, not a criticism of his work.
- The security page is unusually detailed and candid for a product this size. That is a point in its favour. It is still self-declared.

### Gaps
- **How questions are produced and checked: not disclosed.**
- **No independent privacy rating.** My searches found no Common Sense Privacy evaluation, no iKeepSafe badge and no audit report. I did not search those registries directly.
- **The username-screening provider is unnamed.** It may be an AI service. Only the proposed username is said to be sent.
- **Default multiplayer setting for a self-registered child: unknown.**
- **I could not test for wrong answers, answer leaks or unsafe content,** because I did not create an account.

## 6. Which other products are most like it?

### Takeaway
Infinilearn belongs with Prodigy-style math battle RPGs, and it names Prodigy as its target in its own pages. It is not in the family of AI-generated story or quest adventures, and my one search for that family found only vendor pages and listings, with no independent review of any of them.

### Cited Findings

**Prodigy Math (the direct model)**
- **[Maker]** Infinilearn's own page code lists "prodigy alternative" as a keyword, and its site has a "Switching from Prodigy?" page. — [play.infinilearn.com](https://play.infinilearn.com/); [Parents page](https://www.infinilearn.com/parents)
- **[Independent]** Prodigy is a free online math game for grades 1 to 8 with a paid membership. A company press release cited in February 2021 gave more than 100 million registered users. — [Education Week, 23 February 2021](https://www.edweek.org/technology/popular-interactive-math-game-prodigy-is-target-of-complaint-to-federal-trade-commission/2021/02)
- **[Independent]** In February 2021 the Campaign for a Commercial-Free Childhood and 21 other groups complained to the US Federal Trade Commission. They said Prodigy pushes memberships on children, creates a visible divide between paying and non-paying children, and makes unsupported learning claims. Prodigy disputed this and said 95 percent of players use the free version. — [Education Week, 23 February 2021](https://www.edweek.org/technology/popular-interactive-math-game-prodigy-is-target-of-complaint-to-federal-trade-commission/2021/02); [Fairplay complaint page](https://fairplayforkids.org/pf/prodigy/)
- **[Independent] [Snippet only]** Common Sense Media's parent guide calls Prodigy a fun way to practise math skills but says children "may get distracted with the battles, pets, spells, and upgrade suggestions". — [Common Sense Media: Prodigy](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/prodigy-math-game)

**Boddle (younger grades, same model)**
- **[Maker of Boddle]** Boddle is a 3D math game for K to 6. It starts with a placement assessment. Teacher accounts are free, and parents can buy Premium. — [Boddle llms.txt](https://www.boddlelearning.com/llms.txt)
- **[Maker of Boddle]** Boddle claims over 5 million users on one page, and "650,000+ educators" and more than 10 million students, parents and teachers on another. The figures do not agree and are unverified. — [Boddle Premium page](https://boddlelearning.com/premium); [Boddle teachers page](https://www.boddlelearning.com/teachers-page-v1)
- **[Independent] [Not opened]** Unlike Infinilearn, Boddle has outside teacher write-ups, for example from TCEA and TeachersFirst. I saw these in search results but did not read them. — [TCEA blog](https://blog.tcea.org/boddle-math-tool/); [TeachersFirst](https://teachersfirst.org/single.cfm?id=19147)

**Others, as the maker itself places them**
- **[Maker]** Infinilearn's blog post of 28 March 2026 compares itself with Prodigy Math (grades 1 to 8), the DragonBox series (ages 9 to 14, about $8 per app), Legends of Learning (grades 3 to 8, many mini-games) and Adventure Academy (ages 8 to 13, $12.99 a month). It ranks itself first and admits no weakness. — [Blog post, 28 March 2026](https://www.infinilearn.com/blog/math-rpg-games)

**AI-generated learning adventures (a different family)**
- **[Directory]** Llamaroo is listed as a tool that turns a topic into "a safe, story-driven learning adventure for kids". This is a listing, not a review. — [stork.ai listing](https://www.stork.ai/en/llamaroo)
- **[Vendor]** "MathQuest: AI Math Questions" on Google Play offers themed worlds and says it needs an internet connection to generate new AI math questions. — [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mathblast.app)
- **[Vendor]** McGraw Hill presents ALEKS Adventure as an early math program that uses AI and storytelling. — [McGraw Hill: ALEKS Adventure](https://www.mheducation.com/prek-12/explore/whats-new/aleks-adventure.html)

### Inferences
- **Where Infinilearn sits:** the same design as Prodigy (answer a question to attack), aimed older (grades 6 to 8 at its core), with a stricter stance on chat and on paid content. It is far smaller, far younger and unreviewed.
- **Where it differs from an AI-run text world:** Infinilearn is graphical, uses a fixed question set, and gives the child no way to type. It keeps the computer in full control of content. That buys safety and predictability. It gives up open-ended language, story response and reading practice.
- **The Prodigy record is the best guide to what to watch.** The three criticisms were: membership pressure on children, time spent on game features instead of math, and learning claims without evidence. Each can be checked against Infinilearn by playing it for twenty minutes and counting.
- The "AI adventure" products found are all described only by their sellers. I would not rely on any of them as a benchmark without separate research.

### Gaps
- **I did not research the AI-adventure family in depth.** One search only. The three items above are vendor or directory claims, not verified.
- **No head-to-head comparison by an independent reviewer** exists between Infinilearn and any rival.
- **99math, Legends of Learning, DragonBox and Adventure Academy** appear here only as the maker describes them. I did not source them independently.
