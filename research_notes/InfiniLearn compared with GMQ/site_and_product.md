# InfiniLearn (infinilearn.com): site and product fact file

Notes taken on 17 November 2026. The product writes its name "Infinilearn". This is **not** "Infinity Learn by Sri Chaitanya" (infinitylearn.com).

How to read the tags:

- **[Claim]** = the product says this about itself. I did not see it working.
- **[Seen]** = I saw this myself on a public page or in a rendered screen.
- **[Conflict]** = the product's own pages disagree with each other.
- **[Third party]** = a listing or directory outside the product's own site.

I did not create an account. I did not fill in or submit any form. I did not play the game. So almost everything about what happens inside the game is **[Claim]**.

## 1. What is Infinilearn in one paragraph? What kind of product, what subjects, what ages?

### Takeaway
Infinilearn is a browser-based, multiplayer fantasy role-playing game (RPG) for maths practice only, aimed at grades 3 to 9 (about ages 8 to 15). It is a game with a fixed, standards-aligned question bank and an adaptive engine. It is not a tutoring tool, not a course platform and not an AI content generator.

### Cited Findings
- [Claim] Its own summary: "A free fantasy RPG where students battle monsters by solving real math problems. No ads. Grade-level practice for grades 3–9, with parent and teacher dashboards." — [Home page meta description](https://www.infinilearn.com/)
- [Claim] Home page headline: "Math class, but it's an adventure", then "Infinilearn is a multiplayer RPG where 3rd–9th graders build skills, gain confidence, and have fun doing math." — [Home](https://www.infinilearn.com/)
- [Claim] Longer summary written for AI crawlers: "Infinilearn is a free-to-play fantasy RPG designed specifically for students in grades 3-9. Students battle monsters, explore 15 zones, complete quests, and level up characters. Every attack, spell, and ability is powered by solving real math problems." — [llms.txt](https://www.infinilearn.com/llms.txt)
- [Claim] Subject is maths only. Topics listed: "Fractions, decimals, ratios, proportions, expressions, equations, geometry, statistics, and more." — [llms.txt](https://www.infinilearn.com/llms.txt)
- [Claim] Ages: "Infinilearn is designed for students in grades 3-9 (typically ages 8-15)." — [Privacy Policy, section 6, last updated August 6, 2026](https://play.infinilearn.com/privacy?lang=en)

- [Claim] Scale: "100,000+ signups", "5,600+ teachers", "8,200+ parents". — [Home](https://www.infinilearn.com/)
- [Conflict] The lowest grade is not consistent across the site.
  - "grades 3–9" — [Home](https://www.infinilearn.com/)

### Inferences
- The grade range grew downward over time: 6 to 8 first, then 4 to 9, then 4th grade "officially" in August 2026, then 3rd grade on the sign-up screen by November 2026. The marketing text was not all updated together. Grade 3 looks like the newest and thinnest part of the product.
- For a Grade 3 class, the product's own advice on one page is still "use something else". That is worth checking before treating it as a Grade 3 tool.

### Gaps
- I could not find a date for when 3rd grade was added. No page announces it.
- The signup, teacher and parent counts are the product's own numbers. I found no independent check.

## 2. What does a child see and do? World, story, quests, rewards, battles, multiplayer? Is the maths the mechanic?

### Takeaway
The child plays a top-down, pixel-art fantasy RPG: makes a hero, walks around zones, takes quests, and fights turn-based battles in which each attack is a maths question, mostly four-option multiple choice. The site says the maths is the mechanic ("Math is how you win"). But the question shown is chosen by the adaptive engine from the child's weak topics, so it is not tied to the story or the place. I could only see marketing mock-ups, not the live game.

### Inferences
- The maths is the trigger for every action, which is more than "questions bolted on between levels". But the content of the question has no link to the fiction. A slime does not care whether the question is on fractions or angles. The engine picks the topic from the child's weak areas. So the maths is the *gate for combat*, not the *shape of the task*. That is the same basic model as Prodigy, with denser questions.
- Nothing on the site describes choices with consequences, branching story, or a world that remembers what a child did beyond levels, gear and quest counts.
- Most answers are picked from four options. A child can guess. The product's own answer to "Can students just guess?" is only that guessing shows up in the accuracy data.

### Gaps
- I could not see the live game. Everything about story, quests, zones, crafting, trading and PvP is from marketing text and mock-ups.
- No page says how a battle handles a wrong answer in detail (a retry, a hint, a worked solution). Two blog lines say it costs hit points and shows the correct answer.
- No public video demo is linked from the site. The Steam page links to a YouTube account, X and Discord; I did not open those.
- No page names all 15 zones or says how zones map to grades or topics.

## 3. How does it use AI? Who marks the answers? What guard rails?

### Takeaway
The website makes no claim that AI generates the questions, story, pictures or feedback, and no claim that AI marks answers. The only AI use I found is content moderation of names, stated on its Steam page. Questions come from a "question bank". Marking is not explained, but with multiple choice and typed numeric answers it is almost certainly done by code.

### Cited Findings
- [Seen] I searched the text of every page in the sitemap (12 core pages and 174 blog posts) for "AI", "artificial intelligence", "machine learning", "LLM", "ChatGPT" and "generative". There were no mentions about Infinilearn itself. The only hits were blog posts talking about other products, such as Khanmigo and ChatGPT. — [Sitemap](https://www.infinilearn.com/sitemap.xml)
- [Claim] The one AI statement is on Steam: "Infinilearn uses artificial intelligence-based input moderation to ensure appropriate educational interactions." — [Steam, AI Generated Content Disclosure](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3513130/Infinilearn_Math_Game/)
- [Claim] The privacy policy describes name screening without the word AI: "When a player picks or changes their username, the proposed name is automatically checked for appropriateness before it is accepted. Only the proposed name itself is shared with our screening provider, never any other account or student information." — [Privacy Policy, section 4](https://play.infinilearn.com/privacy?lang=en)
- [Claim] Questions come from a bank: "All problems come from a standards-aligned question bank". — [llms.txt](https://www.infinilearn.com/llms.txt); "Each problem is pulled from a Common Core-aligned question bank" — [Blog, Common Core math games](https://www.infinilearn.com/blog/common-core-math-games)
- [Claim] Answer format: "Mostly multiple choice, with typed free-response on harder problems". "harder ones, especially at 9th grade, require a typed free-response answer so students cannot guess their way through." — [llms.txt](https://www.infinilearn.com/llms.txt)
- [Conflict] Another post says flatly "problems are multiple choice rather than free-response". — [Blog, IXL vs Prodigy](https://www.infinilearn.com/blog/ixl-vs-prodigy)
- [Claim] The "adaptive engine" is described as rule-like, not as AI: "The game tracks accuracy on each math topic. During combat, it pulls problems from the student's weakest subjects." — [llms.txt](https://www.infinilearn.com/llms.txt)
- [Claim] The founder's own caution about AI in maths, written about other tools: "AI can sometimes give incorrect math explanations". — [Blog, math tutoring alternatives](https://www.infinilearn.com/blog/math-tutoring-alternatives)
- [Claim] Guard rails described are about people, not AI: no free typing between players, names filtered, no external links, no ads. "Students cannot post free-text content or type messages to other players." — [Privacy Policy, section 5](https://play.infinilearn.com/privacy?lang=en)
- [Third party] Some directories tag or describe it as AI. Product Hunt carries an "Artificial Intelligence" launch tag. A web search summary said G2 calls it "an AI-powered, personalized learning platform". I could not open G2 (HTTP 403), so that wording is unverified. — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/infinilearn)

### Inferences
- Infinilearn is a conventional adaptive practice game. AI does not run the world, write the questions or talk to the child. This is the biggest difference in kind from a design where an AI runs a world inside a constraint file.
- Because answers are multiple choice or short typed values, marking by code is the natural design. The site never says so in words.
- The "screening provider" for usernames may be an AI moderation service. The policy does not name it.

### Gaps
- No page says who wrote the question bank, how big it is, or how it was checked for errors.
- No page says whether AI was used to draft questions, art or the 174 blog posts. The blog grew by 46 posts in March 2026 and 59 in April 2026, all "By Infinilearn Team". That volume from a one-person company is worth noting, but the site says nothing about how they were written.
- No page says how typed answers are compared (exact match, equivalent forms such as 1/2 and 0.5).
- I did not inspect the game's program code. The Terms forbid reverse engineering.

## 4. How is maths covered? Curriculum, grades, skills, adaptivity, progress tracking

### Takeaway
Maths is organised by US grade (3 to 9) and mapped to Common Core, many US state standards, the Australian Curriculum v9 and the Ontario Curriculum. Practice is adaptive by topic inside a chosen "practice path". There is no separate placement test. No page gives a number of skills or questions.

### Cited Findings
- [Claim] Standards: "Common Core, the state standards of Texas (TEKS), Florida (B.E.S.T.), Indiana, Virginia (SOL), Georgia, South Carolina, North Carolina, Pennsylvania (PA Core), Tennessee, Arizona, Arkansas, Alaska, Missouri, Minnesota, New Jersey, Oklahoma and West Virginia (CCRS), the Australian Curriculum v9, and the Ontario Curriculum (2020). Teachers pick which one their dashboard shows, and a Classical option hides codes entirely." — [llms.txt](https://www.infinilearn.com/llms.txt)
- [Conflict] The count of US states differs. The teachers page says "seventeen state versions". The in-app Support Center lists eleven. — [Teachers](https://www.infinilearn.com/teachers); [Support Center](https://play.infinilearn.com/support?lang=en)
- [Claim] No British, IB or Thai curriculum is named. For other countries: "The math is organized by US grade, so pick the grade that matches your child's age". — [After Prodigy](https://www.infinilearn.com/after-prodigy)
- [Claim] Three practice paths: "the full grade-level path, Traditional for core math without statistics/probability, or Custom to pick exactly which topics students practice." — [Teachers](https://www.infinilearn.com/teachers)
- [Claim] Adaptivity: "Attacks pull from your child's weakest topics; difficulty rises and falls with their answers, down to Foundations when needed". — [After Prodigy](https://www.infinilearn.com/after-prodigy)
- [Claim] "A rough stretch drops the game into Foundations practice, real grade 2-3 skills, until confidence is back. Then it climbs." — [After Prodigy](https://www.infinilearn.com/after-prodigy)
- [Claim] Built-in diagnostic: "Every student starts easy and the difficulty adjusts from their very first battles, reaching down to below-grade fundamentals when a student needs them. Your dashboard shows the level each student is currently working at, so no separate placement test is needed." — [Teachers](https://www.infinilearn.com/teachers)
- [Claim] New players are eased in: "New players open with the most approachable topics in their grade. Nobody meets two-step equations on question three." — [After Prodigy](https://www.infinilearn.com/after-prodigy)
- [Claim] Adaptivity is per topic: "the adaptive difficulty engine adjusts by topic, not just overall." — [Blog, Common Core math games](https://www.infinilearn.com/blog/common-core-math-games)
- [Claim] Grade 4 content named: "multiplicative comparison, remainders, equivalent fractions, angles, symmetry, all of it written in short sentences a 9-year-old can read without help." — [Blog, math games for 4th graders](https://www.infinilearn.com/blog/math-games-for-4th-graders)
- [Claim] Data tracked per student: "math problems attempted, accuracy per topic, time per problem, and mastery levels by math standard". — [Privacy Policy, section 2](https://play.infinilearn.com/privacy?lang=en)
- [Claim] On guessing: "The game tracks accuracy by topic over time, so repeated guessing shows up in the data and triggers more practice on weak areas." — [Teachers](https://www.infinilearn.com/teachers)
- [Claim] Language: "The game itself is available in English and French, not yet in Spanish." The marketing site is in English, Spanish and Canadian French. — [llms.txt](https://www.infinilearn.com/llms.txt)

### Inferences
- The centre of gravity is US grades 6 to 8. Grade 3 content is not described anywhere. "Foundations" is described as grade 2 to 3 skills used as a fallback, which may be what a 3rd grader mostly meets.
- Mastery is measured as accuracy by topic. No page describes mastery rules, spaced review or prerequisites between skills.

### Gaps
- No count of skills, standards or questions is published.
- No list of Grade 3 topics is published.
- No evidence of learning gains is published beyond teacher quotes and the product's own usage numbers.

Teachers get free, unlimited classes, a join code, student account creation, a per-topic and per-student dashboard, a choice of standards and practice path, and printable reports. ## 6. Pricing, free tier, platforms, sign-up for children, and the company

### Takeaway
The marketing site says the whole game is free and Premium (US$9.99 a month or US$59.99 a year) only adds parent insight and cosmetics. The live app's own membership and support pages say the opposite: free students get Zone 1 only and Premium unlocks the other zones. It runs in a browser only; there is no native iPad or Android app today. The company is Infinilearn, Inc., run by one founder, Adrian Martinez, a high school student in Orange County, California.

### Inferences
- The "solely for educational purposes" and "no subprocessor may use it for its own purposes" lines would rule out AI training on student data if read strictly. But the policy never states it, so there is no explicit promise to point to.

- A child can make an account alone, with no adult step. The product's position is that no personal information is collected at that point. A first name plus learning data is still stored.
- The student sign-up screen says "Choose a 6-digit PIN", while the policy says the PIN is "auto-generated". Small, but it shows the policy and the app are not in step.

### Gaps
- No statement on AI training exists, so the question "is children's data used to train AI?" has no direct answer from the product.
- The username "screening provider" is not named.
- I found no listing in a third-party privacy register (for example a signed Student Data Privacy Consortium agreement). The site says it signs district agreements on request.
