## 4. What "big" can mean. Be honest about it.

- **The iPad is probably not the limit, but test it before promising it.**
  - A map of 200 × 200 squares should scroll smoothly if only the squares on screen are drawn.
  - Phase 0 below measures this on the three screen sizes.
  - Marc's children have an old iPad (iPadOS 17.6) and an iPad Pro. See `proposals/GMQ_Flyin_iPad_Results_2026-11-22.md`.
- **The real limit is what is in it.** Every place needs:
  - a story reason told before the maths
  - a finished example, about a different quantity
  - numbers checked on thousands of seeds
  - pictures checked at three sizes
  - an independent review
- **So: a big map, then fill it a region at a time.** A huge empty map is worse than a small full one.
  - Every walk between two interesting things should be short.
  - Something should happen on the way: a surprise, a find, a being.

## 5. Rules for the design (from Marc, and from what has already gone wrong)

1. **Two kinds of gate.**
   - **The main road** opens by quests and the things they give: a boat, a lantern, a key, a rope bridge. This is the world web.
   - **Extra lands** may need harder maths, opened by mastery. They are never needed for the main road.
   - Every gate needs a second way through, as Marc said: "or you can do different quests to get those".
   - **Pushback to keep:** a region that opens only with Grade 4 maths shuts most of a Grade 3 class out.
2. **No hard chains.** [Source: Marc's 2023 plan]
   - Patternox waited on a Numbers quest that was never written, so it stayed shut, and Stardust's graphs sat behind it.
   - A missing link must never strand a child.
   - See `proposals/GMQ_Patternox_Options_v1_2026-11-21.md`: "the key is at the door".
3. **No main job needs a secret.** Secrets and finds may help, and may be needed for mini quests.
4. **Not getting lost:**
   - a map the child can open
   - a short "what I still need" list
   - mist over places not yet seen, as on Chronosia
   - a quick way back to the ship
5. **People:** some just say hello, some give a quest. [Source: Marc, 21 November]
6. **The maths does work in the story,** and the child is told why before it starts.
7. **Nothing gives the answer away:** no running totals, no labels that solve it, no card shown too early.
8. **The child does the thinking.**
   - Open problems that take time, like the Backward Mountain on Chronosia, are worth more than many small clicks.
   - [Source: Marc, 19 November: "require them to actually think and solve and struggle".]
9. **Cards:**
   - Finishing a task earns a card.
   - Mini quests that cross to other planets may give an **epic** card. [Source: Marc, 22 November]
   - The battle rules are Marc's. Don't touch them.
10. **The AI's place.** [Source: CLAUDE.md: "the constraint architecture is the foundation"]
    - Build places, beings, problems and gates **as data with a checker**, like the finds kit on Chronosia (`FINDS` and `fCheck` in the game).
    - Then the AI can later write new places inside the rules, and the code still checks every number.
    - Every place should give a short list of facts the AI can use (the game's `worldSlice` and the PLACE / FACTS lines).
    - Never shrink the AI's role to scripted output. Never redesign code-first.
