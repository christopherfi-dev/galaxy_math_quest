## 3. What this thread learned the hard way

Every one of these cost a review round.

1. **Use an independent reviewer on every note, and again after every rewrite.** Give it only `CLAUDE.md`, the brief, and the note. Tell it to hunt, not to praise. Chronosia took five rounds. Algora took five, and then two joint ones. Fix until nothing is above minor, or say plainly what was not re-read.
2. **Say when "now" is.** The game has no clock. A reviewer will ask why the quake waits, what month it is, and what moves time on. Both notes were blocked on this.
3. **A consequence must be seen in the same place, be fair, and cost on both sides.** "Too much" that looks like success was a blocker three times.
4. **Push on your own story like a child:** "Why can't I just...?" If there is no answer, the maths on top falls over.
5. **History is found, not told.** Evidence a child reads at a place (94 notches, a ticking birth clock), people living their customs, someone who was there. Never a character reciting dates.
6. **One question, one reading.** "By month 7" read three ways. Name the day and say what today is.
7. **No numbers for another thread's maths.** You give the story. The code makes and checks the numbers.
8. **A rule you add to explain one thing will contradict another.** After each fix, read the whole fact sheet again.
9. **Say every new name aloud.** "The Count" sounded like a television character.
10. **When you change a note after its review, say so at the top,** and say the change is not reviewed.

## 7. Learned by the second session

11. **Every fix opens a smaller hole.** Round 2 found four blockers; their fixes made round 3's one blocker and thirteen majors. Plan for two or three rounds after any big change, and say which changes no reviewer has read.
12. **Search the built page before you invent.** A role, a machine or a board may already exist. "The speaker", "the loading board" and the Meadow machines were all on the page, and two of them contradicted a fix.
13. **For every cost, ask: "Can the child just make more, or get it elsewhere?"** Floor mats, rolled beads, plaited cups and a neighbour town's mats each undid a cost. Use things that come on their own, from a place that is someone's.
14. **A name in a typed world must not be a common verb, or a word the page already uses.** "Promise" was the game's name and the verb in every line.
15. **Reviews cost Marc tokens:** about 190,000 each, done in full. Keep a re-check narrow: give it the list of changes and the sections they touch.
