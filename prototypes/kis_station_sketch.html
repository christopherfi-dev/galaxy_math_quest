For the teacher: what this sketch shows

Galaxy Math Quest. This is a sketch, not the real build.

  • One station you walk around in. Rooms you have not entered stay dark. Signs by each door say where it leads.
  • Every command is two to four words: a verb, a thing, and sometimes a second thing. Type it, or tap a verb and then tap the thing.
  • The astronaut walks to the thing before acting, so every command shows movement.
  • There are 18 secrets across eight station rooms, and 3 more on Voltara. Things change and stay changed. A thing found in one room often opens something in another.
  • There are 15 station tasks, three for each ship part, named after your own. Each gives one card. A marker floats over each task: an amber ! to do, a green tick when done.
  • Four tasks can be done in real life: Star Map, Shield Calibration, Target Practice and Power Allocation. A switch below chooses. On: they show a purple marker; the student reads the steps, does the task with you, and types the code you give (the codes are in the station guide). Off, as it starts: the same four are played on screen.
  • The ship cannot launch until all five parts are fitted.
  • A Hint button gives the next thing to try, so no one stays stuck.
  • Maths is staged in the world. Asteroids drift in rows outside the Shield Bay windows. Crates stack on the cargo scale. The locker code is the number of stacked crates, and each player gets a different stack.
  • After launch you fly across a galaxy map and see other cadets’ ships. Fly to Galaxy Numerix and land on Voltara.
  • Voltara is one whole quest across nine places: a meadow, a river, a plaza, three streets, the Council Hall, a tunnel and a hidden town. It is drawn from your own words: rainbow skies, cotton-candy clouds, glowing trees, a sparkling river, a Gumblefish, FlavorBursts. The 21 city places and their needs are from your City List.
  • On Voltara the maths is how you get on. You round to cross the river. You regroup crystals to count the tower. You add to learn what each street needs. You estimate, then subtract, to find the shortage. Each one first shows the idea, then asks.
  • A plan lasts one day, and the choice matters. Every place uses energy and makes people happy. Some make people unhappy if they stay dark. The Solar Power Plant and the Factory give energy for tomorrow, but the Factory's smoke makes more people unhappy each day it runs. There are two goals: 600 more happy people than unhappy, and 1,650 or more in the tower next morning. The tower is full at 2,000. So a student can leave Voltara shining, happy but short of power, with power but not happy, or worse off. Flying away shows that as a summary
  • The mystery comes out of the student's own sum: the tower holds 240 less than it should. The last step is done in real life with blocks and a spoken reason. After it, Nomad Hollow is on the plan table, and lighting it or not has its own effect. The code for that step is in the Voltara guide.
  • The robot is a guide. Talk to it and it names the next thing to try, the same as the Hint button. It used to ask 12 ship-check questions. With 15 tasks, those are not needed.
  • 56 more skills are built and tested: clocks, graphs, area, fractions, shapes, star maps and coins. They are filed under your own quests and stay hidden until a planet for that quest exists. The teacher view of the star chart lists them.
  • Each skill shows its maths word and what it means. Code picks every number, draws every picture and marks every answer. Stories come from a bank of 234.
  • This sketch knows about 40 words. On your site, your constrained AI would answer any other sentence.
  • The robot is a new character. It is a proposal. Card names are samples from your files. Odds are 70, 25, 5.

Practice evidence, by your skill map

"Go straight to Voltara" skips the opening and the station. It keeps the cards, the bag and anything already done. It is for you, to look at the quest. Students reach Voltara by fitting their five ship parts and launching.

The opening uses your own words from the student site: the home page, then the Space Station Tasks page, shortened.
